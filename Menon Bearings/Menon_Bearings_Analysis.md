**Ticker:** NSE MENONBE / BSE 523828
**HQ / Plants:** Kolhapur, Maharashtra. Five plants: three at Gokul Shirgaon MIDC and two at Five Star MIDC, Kagal (*per Q2FY26 concall, Nov 2025*).
**Current Date:** November 12, 2026
**Data as of:** Q2FY27 / H1FY27 results and earnings call (Oct 30, 2026; transcript filed Nov 3, 2026)
**FY Referencing:** Indian fiscal year (Apr–Mar). All figures in ₹ Crore (Cr), **consolidated**, unless stated.

**Capital structure and market data:**
- **CMP: ₹348** (intraday Nov 12, 2026, up 6.1% on the day, *screener.in*). **Market cap: ~₹1,950 Cr.**
- **Shares outstanding: 5.604 Cr** (FV ₹1). Cross-checks: FY26 EPS ₹6.83 on PAT ₹38.25 Cr gives 5.60 Cr; Nitin Menon's 1,54,43,454 shares equal 27.56% of the total.
- **Valuation:** Trailing P/E 37.6x (TTM EPS ₹9.26). Book value ₹35.8/share (P/B 9.7x). ROCE 25.5%, ROE 22.3%. Dividend yield 0.6% (*screener.in*).
- **Net debt: ~₹23 Cr.** Gross borrowings were ₹49 Cr at Sep 2026 (*screener.in*), mostly PCFC export credit at ~4.7% (*per Q4FY26 concall*). Cash, bank and mutual funds were ₹25.69 Cr (*per Q2FY27 concall*). Net worth is ~₹201 Cr.
- **Promoter holding: 66.65%** (Sep 2026), down from 68.44%. Aditya, Anshul and Sucheta Menon (promoter group) sold ~10 lakh shares on Aug 14 and 17, 2026, about ₹240/share. Persistence Capital bought ~8.75 lakh shares in the same window. Promoters declared no encumbrance for FY26 (exchange filings via ScanX/BSE).
- **Group context:** Menon Bearings is controlled by the **Nitin Menon** family. Aditya Menon (third generation) and Arun Aradhye (MD, earlier WTD & CFO) are the public faces on the calls. **Menon Pistons** (BSE 531727, CMD Sachin Menon) is a separate listed Kolhapur auto-component company from the wider Menon family. It has a different promoter branch and different products (pistons), so do not mix the two up. None of the six transcripts mentions any inter-company dealings with Menon Pistons. Menon Bearings' own subsidiaries are **Menon Alkop Ltd** (100%, aluminium die-casting), **Menon Brakes** (friction material) and **Menon Ventures** (an exploratory vehicle, e.g. EV charging).

## 1. Business Model Overview

### What the company does
Menon Bearings is a Kolhapur auto-component group with three businesses. The parent company has been operating since 1993:

| Division | Entity | Products | Share of Q3FY26 revenue | EBITDA margin quoted by mgmt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bi-metal** (core) | Menon Bearings Ltd (standalone) | Engine bearings, bushes (incl. PTFE bushes), thrust washers for heavy-duty engines and transmissions | **~74%** | 21%+ (Jul 2025), 23% (Oct 2026) |
| **Aluminium die-casting** | Menon Alkop Ltd (WOS, est. 2008–10) | High-value HPDC castings: engine and transmission covers, EV pump and motor covers, oil and gas and home-automation housings | **~22%** | 23%+ (Jul 2025) → ~20% (May 2026) → 21% (Jul 2026) → 18–19% (Oct 2026) |
| **Brakes / friction material** | Menon Brakes (about 2–3 years old) | Brake linings and pads for HCV/LCV, 2W (Bajaj Pulsar via Endurance), 3W; railway brake blocks in development | **~3%** | 12% (Jul 2025) → 12–13% (Jan 2026) → 25% (Jul 2026) → "30–35%" (Oct 2026) |

*Revenue split per Q3FY26 concall, Jan 2026. Margin quotes from each call. The segment margins quoted have moved around a lot from call to call (see Red Flag 7).*

Standalone Q1FY27 sales were ₹67.06 Cr against consolidated ₹91.79 Cr (*per Q1FY27 concall*). That puts the subsidiaries at ~₹25 Cr per quarter, or ~27% of the total, of which Alkop is ~₹21 Cr per quarter.

### Positioning
- **Niche:** heavy-duty bearings only. Management says it is not in passenger-car bi-metal bearings, where the large global players compete. Its parts go into tractors, HCV/LCV, earth-movers, gensets, compressors and transmissions (*per Q3FY26 & Q1FY27 concalls*).
- **Margins well above peers:** Management cites peer EBITDA of 6–7% at Federal-Mogul/KSPG in bi-metal and 6–8% at large aluminium die-casters, against Menon's ~20% (*per Q3FY26 & Q4FY26 concalls*).
- **Alkop realisation:** ₹700–800/kg normally and ₹900+/kg at current aluminium prices, against ₹450–500/kg for the industry (*per Q1FY26, Q4FY26 & Q1FY27 concalls*). Management attributes this to selectively taking critical, high-machining parts.
- **Long relationships:** John Deere has been a single-source customer for 20+ years. Tata Motors has been a customer for ~30 years. Magna has bought engine bearings for 15 years (*per Q1FY27 concall*).

### Channel mix (Q3FY26, *per Q3FY26 concall*)
| OEM (domestic) | Exports | Replacement / aftermarket | Others (industrial, gensets, compressors, AC) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~48% | ~36% | ~8% | ~8% |

- **Exports:** Exports were ~30% of revenue in Q1FY26 and reached 34–36% from Q3FY26. They have stayed in that range since, although the guidance was 37–40% (*per Q2FY27 concall*). About 80% of exports go to the **USA** (*per Q2FY27 concall*; the Q3FY26 call said 60–65%). The rest goes to 42 countries, including Canada, Brazil and Argentina, and to Africa via a Dubai merchant exporter. Europe has contributed nothing so far and has a ₹35–40 Cr RFQ pipeline.
- **Aftermarket:** The bi-metal aftermarket share has held at ~8% of revenue for 4–5 years. In value it grew from ₹10 Cr to ₹16 Cr (*per Q4FY26 concall*). Alkop has no aftermarket business.
- **Order book (Q4FY26):** 60% export and 40% domestic. About 10% of new orders are EV-linked (*per Q4FY26 concall*).

### End-market mix
**Bi-metal application mix** (*per Q4FY26 concall, May 2026*):

| Tractors | HCV/LCV | Braking systems (via Brakes India etc.) | Transmission (Allison, Magna) | Electrical / industrial / other |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ~35% | 20–25% | 20–25% | ~15% | ~10% |

- Tractors were 54% of the mix a decade ago and are 34% now (*per Q2FY27 concall*).
- Management says HCV + LCV + tractor exposure in bi-metal fell from 70–75% to 58% (*per Q2FY27 concall*).
- **Consolidated auto share:** quoted as ~75% (*May 2026*) and as 60–65% (*Oct 2026*). Within Alkop alone, auto is only 18–20%. The rest is industrial, home automation (Honeywell), oil and gas, and EV.
- **Passenger vehicles:** only via Alkop's EV parts (Tata Punch/Curvv motor covers through TACO Prestolite). The company has no PV ICE bi-metal business.
- **Railways and defence:** zero revenue today. Railways depends on the brakes dynamometer. Defence is only an indirect Tier-2 route (Allison washers that go into defence vehicles); management has said it is "not keen" on direct defence work (*per Q1FY27 concall*).

### Customers and concentration
- **Key names:** John Deere (India, USA, Germany, Brazil and Mexico plants, each with its own vendor code), Allison Transmission (USA; washers and bearings, ~₹30 Cr/yr order from FY26), Federal-Mogul/DRiV, Magna, Escorts-Kubota, TMTL, Brakes India, Cummins, Mahindra (gensets), Mayekawa (compressors), Eaton (EV transmission for Porsche), Concentric (Tier-2 to Tesla), TACO Prestolite (Tata EVs), Honeywell, Endurance/Bajaj, PICL (AC bushes).
- **Concentration:** management says **no customer is more than 15% of revenue** (*per Q4FY26 concall*). At the brand level, though, John Deere across its plants is large in both bi-metal and Alkop. The Alkop growth pipeline is weighted to John Deere globally.

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

### Structural shifts
1. **Becoming an export-led, diversified engine-parts supplier.**
   - US exports grew ~50% in FY26 and Q2FY27 exports grew >75% YoY (*per Q4FY26 & Q2FY27 concalls*).
   - Allison (~₹30 Cr/yr, with billing from Jan 2026) and Federal-Mogul/DRiV are the anchors.
   - About 80% of exports are now on ex-works or FOB terms, which keeps tariff exposure largely with the customer. The remaining DDP accounts share tariff costs: the Allison tariff fell from 50% to 25% and is shared with the customer (*per Q3FY26 concall*).
2. **Products that do not depend on the engine type.**
   - Bi-metal is focused on high-HP tractor, HCV, genset and earth-mover engines, where management expects no electrification for 5–10 years.
   - Bushes and washers go "wherever there is rotation": transmissions, braking systems, AC compressors (PICL), refrigeration (Mayekawa), gensets (Cummins, Mahindra).
   - **PTFE bushes** for EVs and new-age engines: a European customer approved the first of 7 parts. Potential volumes are 1 lakh to 6 lakh pcs/month, worth ~₹1.25 Cr/month (*per Q3FY26 & Q4FY26 concalls*).
3. **Alkop as the EV and industrial hedge.**
   - EV is ~4–5% of Alkop revenue, guided to 8–10% by FY27 end. Customers are Concentric (Tier-2 to Tesla), Eaton Poland (Porsche e-mobility) and TACO Prestolite (Tata EVs, now on 1,600-t, 8 kg parts).
   - 54 parts have entered production over two years, and 51 new parts are in the pipeline (~₹30 Cr, John Deere, Eaton, TACO, Mayekawa).
   - Alkop revenue target is ₹100 Cr in FY27 and ₹125 Cr in FY28 (*per Q1FY27 concall*). This is down from ₹120 Cr for FY27 in the Jul 2025 call.
4. **Opening new markets.**
   - **Europe:** a 15-day trip in Sept 2026, an invitation from CNH and a Netherlands RFQ. Mgmt sizes it at ₹35–40 Cr, contributing from FY28–FY29.
   - **Canada/US Tier-1s:** Magna (9 Canadian plants, ~₹1,800 Cr/yr aluminium buy), Linamar and Allison. These are at the RFQ/NDA stage for Alkop, and a US-based business-development hire has been made.
   - **Africa:** ~₹9 Cr bearings and ~₹6–7 Cr brake linings, all on 100% advance payment, on top of the ₹7–8 Cr Dubai channel.
5. **Brakes as an optional upside.**
   - Revenue now runs ~₹1 Cr+/month from the 2W Bajaj Pulsar program (scaling to 3–5 lakh pcs/month) and Africa.
   - Railway RDSO vendor code is registered. After the dynamometer arrives, mgmt guides ₹5–6 Cr in year one, rising to ₹25–30 Cr within two years. An "auto giant" OEM is in discussion at ~₹1–1.5 Cr/month.
6. **Margin levers.**
   - Solar saves ₹2.25 Cr a year. Yield and process improvements save ₹8–9 Cr a year (*per Q3FY26 concall*). Automation adds more.
   - Raw-material indexation now resets quarterly, and the company is pushing customers to monthly resets.
   - PCFC credit at 4.7% with 2.75% export subvention reduces working-capital cost.

### Forward guidance (latest, *per Q2FY27 concall, Oct 30, 2026*)
- **FY27 revenue growth >30%**, i.e. ~₹390 Cr+, against the ₹360 Cr "conservative" guide. Management expects Q3 and Q4 to be seasonally stronger than H1.
- **FY28: >30% growth again, ~₹500 Cr.** Identified incremental business is ₹30 Cr (FY27), ₹131 Cr (FY28) and ₹157 Cr (FY29), excluding the ~₹35 Cr Europe pipeline. Management "hopes" for 30% CAGR for four years.
- **EBITDA margin:** 20–22% normal range, with a 23% target for FY28. About 20 points of the 30% growth is expected to come from volume.
- **Segment margins quoted:** Bearings 23%, Alkop 18–19%, Brakes 30–35%.

**Takeaway:** On consolidated revenue and margin, management has **met or beaten** each guide since Nov 2025. FY26 guidance was raised from ₹270 to ₹290 Cr and the company delivered ₹299 Cr. On **new-venture timelines** (brakes and railways, the Canada OEM, the export share, the dynamometer) it has **consistently over-promised**. Treat FY28 ₹500 Cr as a bull-case marker, not as the base case.

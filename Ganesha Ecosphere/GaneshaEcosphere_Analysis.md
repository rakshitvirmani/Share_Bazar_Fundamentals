## 1. Business Model Overview

Ganesha Ecosphere Limited (GEL) is India's largest PET recycler with 30+ years of operating history. The company collects post-consumer PET scrap (primarily plastic bottles), processes it through washing and flaking lines, and converts the output into two distinct product streams: legacy textile fiber products and new food-grade packaging granules.

**What they do:** End-to-end PET recycling — scrap procurement → washing → flaking → downstream conversion. Raw material is 100% post-consumer PET waste. Output goes to textile manufacturers (legacy) and FMCG/beverage packaging brands (new rPET business).

### Two-Segment Structure

| Segment | Entity | Products | End Markets | EBITDA Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Legacy rPSF | Standalone (GEL parent) | Recycled Polyester Staple Fiber, Filament Yarn, Non-woven fabric | Spinning mills, home furnishing, apparel, geotextiles | 12–15% |
| Food-grade rPET | Subsidiary (Ganesha Ecopet) | Food-grade rPET granules | FMCG packaging, beverage bottles, Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, Bisleri etc. | 15–20% (at scale) |

**Legacy Segment (Standalone):**
- ~112,000–120,000 MT/year installed capacity; runs at 95–99% utilization
- Product mix diversifying: yarn spinning shifting toward non-woven fabric, home furnishing (>35% of Q3FY26 sales), geotextiles
- Export markets: UK (primary), US, Europe — subject to trade policy headwinds
- Global brand qualification underway for filament yarn (UK-India FTA benefit)
- Backward integration: washing lines at Tiruchirappalli (operational) and Chennai (in progress)
- No new standalone capacity planned; focus is on product mix optimization

**New rPET Segment (Subsidiary — Ganesha Ecopet):**
- Regulatory-driven business: Plastic Waste Management (PWM) Rules mandate escalating recycled content in plastic packaging
- FSSAI-approved food-grade production lines — not all competitors have this approval
- Current capacity: 42,000 MT/year; expanding aggressively
- Warangal, Telangana facility received ₹70 Cr of ₹110 Cr total Telangana government incentives *per Q4/FY25 concall*
- Customers: Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, Bisleri, other FMCG packaging companies

**Scrap Procurement:**
- 100% post-consumer PET bottle scrap — domestic and some imported
- Procured through informal aggregator networks; no hedging mechanism
- Scrap prices are volatile and directly compress margins during spikes (key risk)
- Backward integration (washing lines) reduces scrap dependency at finished-goods level

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

### A. PWM Amendment Rules 2026 — The Central Investment Thesis

The Plastic Waste Management (PWM) Amendment Rules 2026 were gazetted on **March 31, 2026** — the single most important development for GEL's rPET business. These are legally binding mandates, not voluntary targets.

**Mandatory Recycled Content in Plastic Packaging (by weight):**
| Year | Mandatory Recycled Content |
|---|---|
| FY26 | 30% |
| FY27 | 40% |
| FY28 | 50% |
| FY29 onward | 60% |

**FY26 shortfall carryforward:** Brands that couldn't meet 30% in FY26 can carry the deficit forward, but it must be made up over the next 3 years. This means FY27 demand will include catch-up volumes on top of the base 40% mandate. *Per MoEFCC Gazette Mar 31, 2026.*

**Market size implication:** India's total PET packaging output is ~1.2–1.5 million MT/year. At 50% recycled content (FY28), the industry needs ~600,000–750,000 MT of food-grade rPET granules annually. Current organized rPET capacity in India is ~200,000–250,000 MT. Supply-demand gap is structural and large.

**GEL's competitive position:**
- 30+ years of PET recycling experience; deepest institutional knowledge
- FSSAI-approved food-grade lines (regulatory moat; not all competitors cleared)
- Direct relationships with Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, Bisleri and similar anchor customers
- Backward integration from scrap washing reduces cost and improves traceability

### B. Brownfield Warangal Expansion — Near-Term Volume Catalyst

The +22,500 MT brownfield addition at Warangal was commissioned in March/April 2026 (₹130 Cr capex). This:
- Takes rPET capacity from 42,000 → 64,500 MT/year
- Unlocks ~₹225–250 Cr additional annual revenue at full utilization
- Marginal capex per tonne is low (brownfield); margins on incremental volume are higher
- Benefits from existing Telangana government incentives already in place

### C. Greenfield Orissa — Long-Term Scale

The Orissa greenfield (₹500 Cr capex, 67,500 MT rPET) will more than double rPET capacity again:
- Expected commissioning by FY27-end; full utilization by FY28-29
- Combined rPET capacity: 132,000 MT — enough to serve ~10-15% of the FY28 national need
- At 15–20% EBITDA margin on rPET, peak contribution from rPET alone: ₹100–170 Cr EBITDA/year

### D. Legacy Business Diversification — Non-Woven & Home Furnishing

The standalone textile business is evolving beyond yarn spinning:
- Non-woven fabric and home furnishing now >35% of standalone Q3FY26 sales *per Q3FY26 concall*
- These segments have better margins and less import-competition than commodity staple fiber
- Geotextiles (infrastructure applications) emerging as another category
- Filament yarn: pursuing qualification with global UK brands; UK-India FTA reduces tariff friction
- US reciprocal tariff (50%) announced Sept 2, 2025 has blocked US rPET exports for now; but this may reverse

### E. Promoter Conviction — Warrant Conversion at Premium

Promoters infused ₹104 Cr by converting warrants at ₹800/share in Q1FY26 — at a significant premium to the then-prevailing market price. This signals strong insider conviction in the rPET regulatory thesis even during a difficult operational quarter. *Per Q1FY26 concall.*

### F. Telangana Government Support

Warangal facility received ₹70 Cr of ₹110 Cr total government incentives from Telangana state. This is ~53% of the brownfield capex being subsidized — significantly improving brownfield ROCE. *Per Q4/FY25 concall.*

## 5. Red Flags & Risk Analysis

| Risk | Detail | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| **Scrap Price Volatility** | 100% of raw material is post-consumer PET scrap procured through informal aggregator networks. No hedging mechanism. Q1FY26 saw scrap spike to ₹55-56/kg (from ~₹38-40/kg normal), directly crushing margins. Recurrence risk is high — scrap market is unorganized and illiquid. | 8/10 |
| **rPET Competition Intensifying** | FSSAI-approved rPET players grew from 5-6 to 13 as of Q3FY26 concall. As regulatory demand becomes more visible, more competitors will invest. GEL's first-mover advantage is real but narrowing. Pricing power may erode as supply catches up. | 7/10 |
| **Debt Peaking at ~₹700 Cr** | Total debt expected to peak at ~₹700 Cr during brownfield + greenfield execution. This is a significant load for a company whose PAT has dipped to ~₹15 Cr in 9M FY26. If rPET ramp is delayed or margins disappoint, interest coverage could tighten severely. | 7/10 |
| **Regulatory Carryforward Risk** | PWM Rules allow brands to carry forward FY26 shortfalls over 3 years. Some brands may defer rPET purchases in FY27 utilizing this flexibility, slowing near-term demand. Enforcement also remains an open question — India has a history of weak environmental rule enforcement. | 7/10 |
| **Inventory Carrying Risk** | Q2FY26 demonstrated that a scrap price decline after inventory build creates mark-to-market losses (₹10-11 Cr loss realized). With expansion volumes increasing, absolute inventory exposure grows. Company has no hedging on scrap. | 6/10 |
| **US Tariff Exposure** | 50% US reciprocal tariff on rPET exports since September 2, 2025 has blocked the US market. GEL had been building US export business; this creates a revenue hole and diverts capacity to lower-priced domestic market. Reversal timeline unknown. | 6/10 |
| **FY26 Transition Year Earnings Dip** | FY26 PAT will likely be ₹30-45 Cr vs FY25's ₹103 Cr — a 55-70% earnings decline. Market expectations need resetting for a full year. Investors expecting FY25 to be a base may be disappointed. Re-rating is contingent on FY27 recovery. | 6/10 |
| **Subsidiary Utilization Risk** | Ganesha Ecopet (rPET subsidiary) was running at only ~50% utilization as of Q3FY26 despite having 42,000 MT capacity. Adding 22,500 MT brownfield on top of underutilized capacity increases the financial drag. Key question: can rPET demand actually absorb the supply? | 6/10 |
| **Enforcement / Compliance Gap** | PWM Rules exist, but brand compliance auditing is nascent. If government doesn't enforce penalties rigorously, brands can continue using virgin PET without consequence. The rules' effectiveness as a demand catalyst depends on enforcement quality. | 5/10 |
| **Product Mix Complexity** | Simultaneously managing rPSF, filament yarn, non-woven, home furnishing, food-grade rPET, and backward integration (washing lines) across multiple facilities across multiple states. Operational complexity at current scale is high. | 4/10 |

- Brands utilize carryforward provisions → rPET demand grows slowly

- Debt service eats into PAT even as revenue grows
- FY27 PAT: ₹60-80 Cr; no significant re-rating catalyst

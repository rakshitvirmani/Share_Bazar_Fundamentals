## 1. Business Model Overview

MV Electrosystems Limited (Faridabad, Haryana) is a 17-year-old (per AR FY26, 17th Annual Report) engineering company that has pivoted from a legacy switchgear/panel/cable-management business into an **indigenous railway propulsion-systems and rail-electrification technology company**. Per the Directors' Report (*AR FY26*):

> "MV Electrosystems Limited is involved in the designing, development, assembly and manufacturing of propulsion systems, switchgear Panels, electrical equipment and cable management products for railway rolling stock and rail electrification network."

**Two core domains** (*per Sept 2026 concall*):
1. **Rail Propulsion Systems** — IGBT-based 3-phase drive propulsion equipment, auxiliary converters, train/vehicle control units, Driver Display Units (DDUs) — for locomotives (concentrated propulsion) and, under development, EMUs/MEMUs/Vande Bharat (distributed propulsion).
2. **Electrical Hardware for Overhead Electrification (OHE)** — Automatic Fault Locator (AFL) systems, switchgear, cable-protection products, developed via a technology tie-up with South Korean firm PNC Technologies.

**Key milestone:** In September 2025, the company secured CLW/RDSO approval for its indigenously-developed three-phase locomotive propulsion system — one of only ~9–10 propulsion-system manufacturers globally, and one of just two domestic suppliers (alongside market leader Medha) to Indian Railways (*per Sept 2026 concall*). The company's R&D centre is recognised by the Department of Scientific and Industrial Research (DSIR).

**IPO:** ₹290 Cr, entirely fresh issue of 68,23,528 shares at ₹425/share, subscribed 188.32x, listed on NSE & BSE on 06-Aug-2026 (*per AR FY26*). Proceeds earmarked for working capital to scale up propulsion-system manufacturing.

**Revenue model per unit:** Average realization ~₹1.67 Cr per locomotive propulsion system (supply only, excluding a ~3-year AMC that takes the effective per-unit value close to ₹1.75 Cr) (*per Sept 2026 concall*). A 12-coach EMU trainset's full propulsion/electrics package is ~₹16 Cr; a 16-coach Vande Bharat package is ~₹40 Cr (*per Sept 2026 concall*).

**Headcount:** 208 employees as of 31-Mar-2026, including a 45-person R&D team (35 engineers) as of 31-May-2026 (*per AR FY26*).

## 3. Capacity Roadmap

| Milestone | Monthly Propulsion-System Output | Annualized Run-Rate | Status/Timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sept 2026 | 10 units/month | ~120/yr | First scale-up batch post-IPO (*per Sept 2026 concall*) |

| Built-out max capacity | 55 units/month | ~660/yr | Already built; deliberately under-utilized to avoid idle-capacity losses (*per Sept 2026 concall*) |
| Two-shift expansion (flagged in Q&A, timing unconfirmed) | Referenced up to ~4,500 systems/yr theoretical, in response to an analyst question | — | Existing factory, additional shifts; not yet a firm management commitment |

**Facilities:** Unit 1 and Unit 2 (Faridabad, Haryana) are both commissioned; Unit 2 fabrication/assembly work was starting "Monday onward" as of the 27-Aug-2026 call. Management states **no further capex is required for machinery/production** — remaining capex is limited to three additional test setups, expected to be installed within 45–60 days of the call (i.e., by ~Oct/Nov 2026) (*per Sept 2026 concall*).

**FY26 capex actually incurred:** ₹18.2 Cr (Plant & Machinery additions ₹15.4 Cr + CWIP ₹1.6 Cr), largely the Unit-2 buildout (*per AR FY26, Note 2*). This is a meaningfully capex-light model going forward if management's "no more machinery capex" guidance holds — a positive for free cash flow once the topline scales.

**Working capital requirement:** Management pegs total working capital need at ~₹200 Cr to support a ₹1,000 Cr revenue run-rate, on a ~45-day raw-material procurement cycle plus manufacturing/logistics, with railway payments typically received within 15 days of dispatch (total realized cycle ~85 days on the last three dispatches, against a prospectus-disclosed 105-day assumption) (*per Sept 2026 concall*).

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

- **From legacy panel-maker to indigenous propulsion OEM:** The CLW/RDSO approval (Sept 2025) is the structural inflection point — it converts a ~17-year-old, sub-₹65 Cr revenue switchgear business into one of only two domestic suppliers of locomotive propulsion systems to Indian Railways, with fully in-house IPR (never capitalized, always expensed — *per Sept 2026 concall*, a conservative but margin-suppressing accounting choice).
- **EMU/MEMU/Vande Bharat platform (distributed power propulsion):** ₹86 Cr MEMU developmental order in hand (MCF), 24-month supply window, product design/development targeted for completion in ~10 months with RDSO approval taking a further ~9–10 months (~15–16 months total). Once achieved, management says the same platform extends relatively quickly to Vande Bharat, Metro, and other distributed-power trainsets (*per Sept 2026 concall*).
- **OHE Automatic Fault Locator (AFL):** Tie-up with South Korea's PNC Technologies (one of only 2–3 global players in the technology). Revenue potential estimated at ~₹1 Cr per transformer-to-transformer stretch (2× sub-sectioning-point units at ₹12L each + 1× sectioning-point unit at ₹24L + 1× substation-transformer unit at ₹36L), against a 5–7 year installation runway as India doubles its electrified-line infrastructure.
- **Silicon Carbide (SiC) transition:** Development of SiC-based auxiliary converters (lighter, higher-speed-capable) as a precursor to SiC traction-level components — relevant to high-speed rail ambitions.
- **Backward integration via JV/consortium:** Tie-up with Hansung Motors (South Korea) to add traction motors to the portfolio.
- **Optionality/diversification:** Marine propulsion (reusing the 500kW hotel-load-converter modular platform), renewable/industrial power electronics, and export markets (management plans to exhibit at the next InnoTrans event in Germany, having missed the 2026 edition due to the domestic scale-up).
- **Structural demand tailwinds:** ~1,600+ locomotives manufactured annually in India (rising trend since 1950s), a fleet of ~15,000 locomotives requiring periodic propulsion rehabilitation, 300–500 EMUs tendered annually, plus Vande Bharat/Amrit Bharat/RRTS programmes — all cited by management as expanding the addressable order pipeline well beyond the current order book.

## 7. Investment Verdict

**Weighted Fair Value: ~₹301/share | CMP: ~₹855/share | Premium to FV: ~184% | Rating: REDUCE**

MV Electrosystems is a genuinely interesting structural story — an indigenous-IP propulsion manufacturer in a near-duopoly domestic market, riding a real government-backed railway-modernisation tailwind, with a ₹1,000+ Cr order book providing multi-year revenue visibility. That narrative is legitimate. The problem is entirely one of **price versus proof**: the company has zero history of executing at the scale it is now promising, posted losses in both FY26 and the first quarter after listing, burned significant cash from operations in FY26, and received an explicit auditor caution on cash losses — yet trades at a market cap that already assumes the ramp goes close to perfectly.

1. **Bull Case (25% probability) — ₹725/share:** Requires the ₹400 Cr FY27 revenue guidance to be hit essentially in full, with margins reaching ~9% blended for the year (implying the back half of the year runs well above the 10% "stabilized" target management has cited). This is achievable only if the 10→25→40 units/month ramp proceeds exactly as guided with no slippage, and EMU/OHE optionality starts contributing on schedule.
2. **Base Case (50% probability) — ₹181/share:** The more likely outcome for a first-year post-IPO industrial scale-up — output ramp slips by a quarter or two, FY27 revenue lands closer to ₹280 Cr, and the company is only modestly profitable (~4% margin) for the full year as H1 remains loss-making while H2 improves.
3. **Bear Case (25% probability) — ₹117/share:** Scale-up delays extend meaningfully (e.g., testing-setup installation slips, component-supply disruptions, or a tender loss), FY27 revenue lands near ₹160 Cr, and the company remains loss-making for a second consecutive year, pressuring the balance sheet and potentially forcing further capital raises.

**Rating: REDUCE.** This is not a call that the underlying business is broken — the order book, technology moat, and sector tailwinds are real — but the current price embeds an outcome more optimistic than even this note's Bull case. Investors already holding the stock from the IPO/listing pop should size the position for the volatility inherent in a thinly-floated, narrative-driven micro-cap, and should treat any pullback toward the ₹280–320/share weighted-fair-value zone as a meaningfully more attractive entry point than the current level. New buyers at CMP are effectively underwriting flawless execution with no margin of safety.

**Key near-term triggers to watch (per Section 6 shifters):** the actual September/October dispatch numbers (does it hit 10, then 25 by November?), Q2 FY27 results (does the loss narrow or persist?), and news flow on the CLW 748-locomotive tender and the Banaras/Patiala Loco Works tenders expected by calendar year-end.

- **Promoter shareholding stability** post-IPO (any further reshuffling or pledging).

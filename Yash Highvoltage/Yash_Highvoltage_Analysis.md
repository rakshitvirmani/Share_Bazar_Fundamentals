## 1. Business Model Overview

Yash Highvoltage makes **transformer bushings**. A bushing is the insulated pass-through that connects a power transformer's internal winding to the external grid conductor. Every power transformer needs about 7 bushings (3 HV, 3 LV and 1 neutral). Bushings are roughly **2–6% of transformer cost** (*per Jan 2025 investor meet*), or **~5%** (*per May 2026 concall*). The product is critical to safety and needs approvals: utilities, consultants and OEMs take **2–4 years to qualify a new make** (*per FY25 concall, May 2025*). Management sees this approval barrier as the core moat.

**Product segments:**

| Segment | What it is | Revenue share | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RIP / RIS bushings** (Resin Impregnated Paper / Synthetic) | Dry-type condenser bushings with 30–35 yr life; the technology is replacing OIP | **86%** of H1 FY27 revenue (*per Q2FY27 concall, Oct 2026*); 70–80% in FY25 (*Jan 2025*) | Today an **assembly model**: the capacitive core is imported (historically from Moser-Glaser / Pfiffner, Switzerland; 25–30% now from other vendors), then assembled and tested at Halol. Cores are "35–40%+ of RM cost" (*May 2025*). Sold **only in India** under the earlier technology arrangement. |
| **OIP bushings** (Oil Impregnated Paper) | Conventional bushings with 15–20 yr life; fully indigenous | OIP + High-Current = **14%** in H1 FY27 | ~35–40% of OIP/HC volume is exported (*May 2025*). OIP is currently in **shortage**, with longer lead times (*May 2026*). |
| **High-Current bushings** | Niche, low-voltage high-amp bushings | 4–5% (*Jan 2025*) | Highest margin: ~45% product-level EBITDA vs 20–22% for RIP and 13–15% for OIP (*per CFO, Jan 2025*) |
| **Retrofit & Services** | Replacing old bushings in the installed grid | **8% of domestic sales** in H1 FY27 (up from 4%) (*Oct 2026*); 6–7% of FY26 revenue (*May 2026*) | Low material-to-sales ratio (MSR), high margin |

**Geography:** **95% domestic / 5% export** in H1 FY27 (*Oct 2026*). Export order book is ₹40–50 Cr of ~₹760 Cr. Yash has approvals in 60+ countries and a 96% repeat-order rate (*May 2026*). Exports so far are only OIP/HC, because the imported-core RIP could not be exported. **Once the cores are made in-house, that restriction ends.** Management called it a "clean break" with no residual IP restriction (*May 2026*). Export realisations run **30–40% above** domestic for the same kV class (*Oct 2025*).

**Market position:** In India Yash has ~70% share of RIP up to 245 kV and 35–40% of OIP up to 245 kV (*Jan 2025*), and ~30–35% of its addressable Indian market overall (*Oct 2025*). Its global share is ~1–1.5%, and the aspiration is 5% in 4–5 years (*Oct 2026*). The global bushing market is ~₹25,000 Cr. Yash's current range (52–245 kV) addresses ~₹10,000–12,000 Cr, which rises to ₹15,000–16,000 Cr once it reaches 550 kV (*Oct 2025*).

**Competition:** Globally, Hitachi Energy, Trench and GE (via Italy) control ~80% of the market. The rest includes CG Power, Moser-Glaser, MIM/Mehru (Russian JV), and a few Chinese players (*Oct 2025*). In India, CG Power, Hitachi India and MIM Bhiwadi are all building localized RIP capacity (*May 2026*), and "a couple of companies" have announced new bushing entry (*Oct 2026*).

**Group structure:**
- **Yash HV USA Inc.** (100% subsidiary): operational. Rep agreements with Ensales, Gateway Power Sales and EHV Solutions cover ~27 US states (*Oct 2026*).
- **Yash HV Power Components Pvt Ltd** (100% subsidiary): vehicle for adjacent transformer components (*May 2025*).
- **Sukrut Electric** (Pune, ~50% stake, co-owned with Quality Power): transformer components. FY26 revenue ~₹25–26 Cr, loss-making in FY25 and back in profit within 9 months of the Dec 2025 takeover. Target ₹150–160 Cr revenue in 4–5 years (*May 2026, Oct 2026*).
- **Distribution partners:** Weidmann (select Europe / North Africa) and Electrolink (UK, Ireland, Wales) (*Oct 2025*).

**Promoter / management:** Keyur Shah (CMD), Darshan Thakkar (ED Finance), Nirav Patel (ED Global Business), Sumit Poddar (CFO), Mitanshu Shah (Strategic Advisor). The company secretary changed between May 2026 and Oct 2026 (Tushar Lakhmapurkar to Bhoomi Talati).

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

**Yash 1.0 to Yash 2.0: from "Indian assembler of Swiss RIP cores, India-only" to "integrated global RIP/OIP bushing OEM up to 550 kV"**

1. **Core localization is the margin lever.** Import duty plus freight on cores is **18–20% of their cost** today. Localizing removes that, plus FX and overhead differences (*Oct 2026*). Management will share some of the saving with customers if Indian rivals undercut, so the stated aim is to "at least maintain EBITDA or make it a bit better." Upside is real but not guaranteed to show in margins.

2. **Export unlock for RIP.** Today 80–85% of revenue (RIP) cannot be exported. With its own cores, Yash can sell RIP worldwide at realisations 30–40% above India's. The target is **20%+ export share in 2–3 years** (*May 2026*), with RIP export bookings starting "from next year mid", which would change the mix in FY28–29 (*Oct 2026*).
   - US groundwork is in place: a subsidiary, rep coverage of ~27 states, and **seismic qualification for IEEE 138 kV and 230 kV OIP bushings, the first Indian OEM to achieve this** (*Oct 2026*).
   - Agents now cover ~30 countries.

3. **Voltage ladder: 245 kV, then 420 kV, then 550 kV.** Bushing value rises steeply with voltage: a 72.5 kV OIP is ₹30–35k, while a 420 kV unit is ₹36–44 lakh (*Oct 2026*). Yash has unexecuted 420 kV orders (assembly model) to be invoiced in FY27. Localized 420/550 kV comes in FY28. This adds ~₹3,400–3,500 Cr to the addressable market (*Oct 2025*). The first 245 kV wall-through bushing was supplied to a PSU in H1 FY27.

4. **Structural seller's market.** 250–400+ transformer OEMs are expanding capacity, against only 10–13 bushing makers globally (*Oct 2025, Oct 2026*). Management expects a seller's market for "2, 3, 4 years" and no price war for "5–6 years" (*Oct 2026*). It argues that the 2–4 year qualification cycle keeps new entrants from taking share quickly.

5. **OIP-to-RIP technology transition** and **grid replacement / retrofit**: OIP lasts 15–20 years and RIP 30–35 years. Retrofit doubled to 8% of domestic sales (*Oct 2026*).

6. **Operating leverage and systems:** SAP S/4HANA went live globally in H1 FY27, cutting CCC from 81 to 63 days. The ISO 14001/45001 certifications support export qualification.

7. **Optionality:** Sukrut Electric (₹25 Cr scaling to ₹150–160 Cr target), the Yash HV Power Components platform, and a possible PLI for RIP bushings (*Jan 2025*, unconfirmed since).

## 5. Red Flags & Risk Analysis

| # | Risk | Severity | Detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Core localization / type-test risk** | **7/10** | In-house RIP cores are a new technology for Yash: resin, not oil, using a different Swiss consultant's know-how (*Jan 2025*). Type test is still pending, and customer product approvals are needed per core type. Each slip pushes back both the margin lever and the export unlock. The timeline has already slipped ~2–3 quarters versus the IPO plan. Higher kV (420/550) carries more risk and is FY28. |

Mean reversion in pricing would compress both earnings and the multiple. |

## 7. Investment Verdict

### Weighted Fair Value & Entry Recommendation

**Weighted Fair Value: ₹1,155/share** (FY28E basis, 3.06 Cr FD shares). At the **assumed CMP of ₹1,159**, the stock is at a 0.4% premium, so the rating is **HOLD**. Fresh buying is **ACCUMULATE below ₹1,155** and **BUY below ₹1,039**. Consider trimming **above ₹1,212**.

### Bull Case (25%): What Needs to Go Right (FV ₹1,824)
- In-house RIP cores clear type test in Q3 FY27, and both plant and product approvals land in Q4 FY27 as management guided.
- Localization removes the 18–20% duty-plus-freight cost and Yash keeps most of it, taking EBITDA above 30%.
- RIP exports open from mid-FY28 at 30–40% higher realisations. US utility approvals arrive through the 27-state rep network and the seismic-qualified IEEE line.
- 420 kV, then 550 kV, ramp up. FY28 revenue reaches ~₹650 Cr and PAT ~₹140 Cr. The market pays 40x for a 35%+ compounder that also has net cash.

### Base Case (50%): Most Likely Path (FV ₹1,139)
- FY27 lands near ₹410 Cr revenue and ₹75 Cr PAT. That doubles PAT YoY and comfortably beats the May 2026 guidance of ₹360–400 Cr.
- Localization happens with a 1–2 quarter lag. Savings are partly passed to customers as CG, Hitachi and MIM localize too. Margins drift from 28% to 29%.
- Exports begin in late FY28 and reach ~15–20% by FY30. Revenue compounds ~30–38% to ~₹745 Cr in FY29.
- The ₹760 Cr order book (≈1.9x FY27E revenue) de-risks the next 18 months. The question is whether pricing holds beyond that.

### Bear Case (25%): What Could Go Wrong (FV ₹518)
- The localized core fails or delays its type test. Yash stays an assembler dependent on Moser-Glaser and other vendors, with Swiss supply terms renegotiated after mid-2027.
- The shortage eases as Indian rivals localize RIP and transformer OEMs face overcapacity below 400 kV. Pricing gains from FY26–27 reverse, and EBITDA falls to ~24%.
- FY28 PAT stalls near ₹72 Cr, and the SME multiple compresses to ~22x.

### Rating: **HOLD** (at assumed CMP ₹1,159; weighted FV ₹1,155)

**Summary view:** Yash is executing well. H1 FY27 delivered revenue +95%, PAT +165%, 28% EBITDA, a ₹760 Cr order book, 63-day CCC and ₹95 Cr net cash, and every recent guidance has been beaten. The structural story is credible: localization plus export unlock plus voltage climb in a bushing shortage. **The price already reflects most of this.** At ~47x FY27E and ~34x FY28E FD EPS, the stock is near probability-weighted fair value. The next re-rating trigger is binary and near-term: the **localized-core type test (expected Q3 FY27)**. Wait for either a type-test pass (bull probability up, so FV rises toward ~₹1,300+) or a pullback toward ₹1,000–1,040 before adding.

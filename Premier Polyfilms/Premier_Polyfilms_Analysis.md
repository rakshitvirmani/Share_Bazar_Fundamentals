## 1. Business Model Overview

Premier Polyfilm (incorporated 1992, part of the Goenka family's Delhi PVC group) makes **calendered flexible PVC products** from PVC resin, plasticisers and fabric substrates. It calls itself the "quality leader in the vinyl flooring market" with a "significant share" of vinyl flooring, sheeting and leather cloth *(AR FY26, MD&A)*.

### Product portfolio

| **PVC sheeting & films** | Industrial, stationery, packaging, humidity barriers | — | AR FY26 |

**Revenue mix.** The company reports a single segment, with no product split *(AR FY26, Note 41)*. Geography: **domestic ₹311.1 Cr gross, exports ₹27.8 Cr (8.2%)** in FY26. Exports have fallen three years running (₹39.6 Cr → ₹34.3 Cr → ₹27.8 Cr) *(AR FY25 & FY26, MD&A)*.

**Related-party channel.** **RMG Polyvinyl India Ltd**, a promoter-group company, bought **₹48.8 Cr** of goods in FY26 (≈**16.5% of net revenue**) and sold ₹19.0 Cr to Premier. The auditor made this a Key Audit Matter *(AR FY26, Note 32 & KAM 1)*. See Red Flag #1.

**Raw materials.** PVC resin is the key input. India is short of resin and some grades have only a few domestic suppliers *(AR FY26, MD&A)*. Forex outgo was ₹50.5 Cr against earnings of ₹30.8 Cr in FY26, so the company is a net importer *(AR FY26, Annexure I)*.

**Scale.** 323 permanent employees *(AR FY26)*. Gross block is only ₹81.8 Cr plus ₹18 Cr of leasehold-land ROU, so the business turns its fixed assets **~3.6×** (net revenue / gross block). R&D spend is ₹3.66 lakh a year, which is negligible *(AR FY26, Annexure I)*.

**Credit rating.** Upgraded from **[ICRA] BBB+ (Stable)/A2** *(AR FY25)* to **[ICRA] A- (Stable)/A2+** *(AR FY26; upgrade dated 16-Jun-2026 per [Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/news/premier-polyfilm-reports-q1-standalone-profit-of-9-1-cr-and-revenue-of-100-cr-801406-PE1_C))*.

### Key Observations

1. **A margin re-rating, not just volume.** Over FY24–26 production grew only ~4% a year (27.1k → 29.3k MT), but EBITDA margin expanded from 12.6% to 15.5% and PAT grew ~24% CAGR. Management attributes this to buying raw material at "highly competitive prices while maintaining stable selling prices" and **dropping low-margin product lines** *(AR FY25 & FY26, Directors' Report)*. Part of this depends on the resin price cycle (soft resin prices in FY25–26), so it is **not fully structural**.
2. **FY26 growth was price/mix led.** Net revenue rose 12.3% on 3.3% volume growth, so realisation per kg rose ~9%. The ~₹7 Cr increase in traded goods (purchases of stock-in-trade up from ₹8.5 Cr to ₹15.7 Cr, linked to trading in Tamil Nadu ahead of the plant) also contributed.
3. **Q1FY27 accelerated sharply** (net sales +35%, PAT +51%) even before the Tamil Nadu plant produces anything. Without a concall, the split between volume, price, new products and southern trading is **unknown**.
4. **Balance sheet now net cash** (~₹20 Cr). Term debt is almost repaid (₹3.0 Cr left, due within 12 months) *(AR FY26, Note 14)*. However, ₹21.5 Cr of the cash pile sits in **equity mutual funds and thematic index funds** (Defence Index, Alpha 50, Energy Opportunities). These returned −0.65% in FY26 *(AR FY26, Notes 6.1 & 43)*.
5. **Inventory build is the main blemish.** Inventory days went 43 → 70 → 99. OCF still grew (₹26.7 Cr) because payables stretched. If resin prices fall, the high-cost inventory becomes a margin headwind.

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

### DNA shift: from a ~9% margin commodity calenderer to a ~15–16% margin, net-cash niche franchise

| Dimension | FY21–FY23 | FY26 / Q1FY27 |
|---|---|---|
| EBITDA margin | 9–10% | 15.5% |
| PAT | ₹8–12 Cr | ₹31.9 Cr (TTM ~₹35 Cr) |
| Balance sheet | Net debt | Net cash ~₹20 Cr |
| Credit rating | BBB+ / A2 (FY25) | **A- (Stable) / A2+** |
| Footprint | 2 plants, both in UP | 3 sites; entering the South (TN) |
| Product focus | Broad commodity line | Low-margin lines dropped; automotive (IATF) leather cloth, geomembranes, technical flooring |
| Market cap | ~₹200 Cr (FY23) | ~₹1,018 Cr |

### Catalysts

1. **Tamil Nadu (SIPCOT) ramp-up.** A first film line should start around Q2–Q3FY27. The South is a large market for flooring and leathercloth (auto OEM clusters around Chennai and Hosur), and the company is currently serving it from UP with ~₹4 Cr/yr of freight-out *(AR FY26, Note 29)*. Local production could add revenue and improve margins.
2. **Automotive leather cloth.** IATF 16949 certification gives access to auto OEM and Tier-1 upholstery. The soft-touch cushion leather cloth made at Unit II is the higher-value product. Mix shift toward it is likely the main margin driver (inferred; not disclosed).
3. **Infrastructure demand for geomembranes.** BIS/ISI-certified geomembranes (canal and pond lining) and electrical mats sell into government-funded water and power projects.
4. **Import protection.** Management names dumping of cheap imported finished goods as the main threat *(AR FY26, MD&A)*. Any anti-dumping or quality-control order on imported PVC flooring or leathercloth would be a direct tailwind (speculative, not company-guided).
5. **Promoter buying.** Promoter-group holding rose from 67.60% (Mar-25) to 69.62% (Mar-26) to 71.09% (Jun-26). Group entities **D.L. Millar & Co** and **Premier Polyplast & Processors** bought shares, while **Sanghai Holdings** (11.18%, a non-promoter) sold *(AR FY26, Note 12.1; [Screener](https://www.screener.in/company/PREMIERPOL/))*. Insiders are buying at rising prices.
6. **Rating upgrade to A-.** Lowers working-capital borrowing cost and widens the customer and tender base.
7. **Operating leverage.** In Q1FY27, gross profit grew faster than revenue (+48% vs +34%) *([Univest](https://univest.in/blogs/premier-polyfilm-q1-results-fy27-pat-9-crore))*. Employee and admin costs are largely fixed.

## 5. Red Flags & Risk Analysis

| # | Risk | Severity | Detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Related-party sales concentration (RMG Polyvinyl)** | **7/10** | Sales to promoter-group **RMG Polyvinyl India** were ₹37.4 Cr (FY24), ₹45.9 Cr (FY25) and **₹48.8 Cr (FY26)**, equal to 14–17% of net revenue. Premier also **bought** ₹19.0 Cr from RMG in FY26 (₹10.9 Cr in FY25), so the two-way flow totalled ₹67.9 Cr *(AR FY26, Note 32 & AOC-2)*. The auditor made this part of **KAM 1** and checked shareholder approval under **Section 188**. The **Directors' Report says none of the RPTs fall under Section 188(1)**, which contradicts that. Pricing terms are not disclosed ("salient terms: –" in AOC-2). Margin quality on this channel cannot be verified |
| 2 | **Disclosure and governance quality** | **6/10** | No concalls, no presentations, no product/capacity disclosure. **Statutory audit fee is ₹1.32 lakh** for a ~₹1,000 Cr market-cap company *(AR FY26, Note 28.1)*. The auditor changed mid-year when M A R S & Associates merged into **A D V and Co LLP** (Feb-2026). The FY26 secretarial audit report is headed "year ended March 31, 2024" and says the company "has **possibly** complied". NSE fined the company ₹50,000 for late RPT XBRL filing *(AR FY26)*. The MD's son (age 28) was made Executive Director in Jul-2025 |
| 3 | **Valuation after a 2.5× re-rating** | **6/10** | The stock went from a 52-week low of ₹38 to an all-time high of ₹103.8 on 05-Oct-2026. | 8 | **Export decline** | **3/10** | Exports fell from ₹39.6 Cr (FY24) to ₹34.3 Cr (FY25) to ₹27.8 Cr (FY26), from ~13% to ~8% of gross sales. Forex outgo now exceeds earnings by ~₹20 Cr, a net USD short |
| 9 | **Treasury in equity/thematic funds** | **3/10** | ₹21.5 Cr is invested in equity MFs, ETFs and sector index funds (Defence, Alpha 50, Energy). This is non-core risk-taking with listed-company cash; the return was −0.65% in FY26 *(AR FY26, Notes 6.1 & 43)* |
| 10 | **Key-man and succession** | **3/10** | Depends on MD & CEO Amitaabh Goenka (11.6% holder), who also gives the **personal guarantee** on bank facilities *(AR FY26, Note 14.1)*. His remuneration rose 15% to ₹1.62 Cr. The next generation is being inducted |

**Overall risk score: 5.5/10.** The operating franchise is profitable, has net cash and is growing. The main risks are **disclosure opacity, the RMG related-party channel, and a valuation that already prices in strong execution**.

### Investment Verdict

**Weighted Fair Value: ₹92 | CMP: ₹97.23 | Expected return: −5.4% | Rating: REDUCE (borderline HOLD)** (BUY below ₹82.8; ACCUMULATE ₹82.8–92; HOLD ₹92–96.6; REDUCE above ₹96.6)

- **Bull (25%) — ₹137.** The TN plant starts by end-2026, auto leathercloth and geomembranes drive a mix shift, and EBITDA margin moves to ~17%. FY28E PAT is ~₹55 Cr (EPS ₹5.27). Promoter buying and the rating upgrade sustain a 26× multiple.
- **Base (50%) — ₹94.** Growth normalises from +35% to ~18–22% as base effects fade, margins hold at ~15.5%, and TN ramps gradually. FY28E EPS is ₹4.28 at 22×. This is roughly where the stock trades today.
- **Bear (25%) — ₹44.** A resin upcycle or import pressure pushes margins back toward FY24's 13%, TN slips, inventory losses hit, and the related-party and disclosure concerns lead to a de-rating to 15×.

**Bottom line:** Premier Polyfilm has quietly become a **better business**. Over three years EBITDA margin rose from ~9–10% to 15.5%, PAT grew from ₹12 Cr to ₹32 Cr, ROE is ~22%, the balance sheet is net cash, the credit rating was upgraded, and promoters are buying. Q1FY27 (+51% PAT) shows the momentum is intact. However, the stock has **re-rated ~2.5× in a year to ~29× TTM / ~23× FY28E earnings**, close to our base-case value. Disclosure is poor: no concalls, no capacity data, a ₹1.3 lakh audit fee, and ~16% of sales to a promoter-group company. That justifies a discount to quality peers rather than a premium. **At ₹97 the risk-reward is unattractive. Book partial profits, or wait for an entry below ₹83 (BUY zone) or a clear H1FY27 print showing ≥16% margins and TN production.** A strong Q2FY27 (PAT ≥ ₹10.5 Cr) would shift probability toward the bull case and move the weighted FV to ~₹100+, which would upgrade the stock to HOLD or ACCUMULATE.

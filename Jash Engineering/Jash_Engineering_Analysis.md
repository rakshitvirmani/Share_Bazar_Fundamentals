## 1. Business Model Overview

Jash Engineering is a 52-year-old Indian maker of **custom-engineered water-control and water-treatment equipment**. Its products are water control gates (sluice, penstock, stop-log, roller and flap gates), screens (coarse, fine and travelling-band), knife-gate and other valves, and process equipment (disc filters, vortex grit separators, sludge conveyors, surge/air vessels). It also makes niche products such as Archimedean screw pumps and turbines. Management is explicit that Jash **supplies equipment only** and will never take on turnkey EPC projects *(per Q2FY26 concall)*. Its customers are municipal water/wastewater bodies and the EPC contractors that build treatment plants (Welspun, VA Tech Wabag, Ion Exchange and others), plus nuclear (NPCIL), hydro, desalination and industrial users.

**Business is project-based and custom-built, not mass-produced.** Orders typically run ₹1–4 cr, with occasional ₹35–70 cr jobs. Most contracts are fixed-price with stage-wise payments (10–20% advance, 30–35% by inspection) and no raw-material pass-through clause *(per Q3FY26 concall)*. Operations and maintenance is only 5–10% of revenue because equipment lives are 25–35 years.

**Product mix (FY26):** Water control gates ~60%, screens ~15%, valves ~13–14%, other equipment (hydro, pumping, process) ~8% *(per Q2FY26 and Q4FY26 concalls)*.

**Geographic mix (FY26):** India 44.5%, outside India 55.5%. The US (via Rodney Hunt) was ~35% of revenue, down from ~40–45% earlier *(per Q3FY26 and Q4FY26 concalls)*. In Q1FY27, ₹70 cr of revenue came from India and ₹80 cr from outside India *(per Q1FY27 concall)*. Management's target mix is India ~35–45%, US ~30–35%, Europe ~15%, rest of world 15–20%.

**FY26 was a lost year for growth.** Revenue was flat at ₹736 cr (management reports total income of ₹757 cr vs ₹746 cr). There were three causes:
1. **US tariffs:** production for US orders was slowed while tariffs moved from 25% to 50%. Exports from India to Rodney Hunt fell by ₹25–30 cr, and Rodney Hunt's revenue dropped from $36M to ~$29M.
2. **Middle East conflict:** shipments to the Gulf, and in part to the Far East and Southeast Asia, were blocked by a lack of ships and containers.
3. **Stuck projects:** a ₹10 cr Vietnam order sat finished for months *(per Q2FY26, Q3FY26 and Q4FY26 concalls)*.

**Domestic business grew ~18%** and filled most of the ₹50–60 cr export shortfall, at good margins *(per Q4FY26 concall)*.

**Earnings quality flags:**
- **Cash conversion:** cash from operations was ₹56 cr against PAT of ₹76 cr (~74%) in FY26, and ₹55 cr against ₹87 cr (~63%) in FY25.
- **Debtor days** rose to **149 from 112**.
- **Other income** of ₹20 cr, mostly forex gains, was ~26% of FY26 PAT *(per Screener.in; per Q4FY26 concall)*.

**Seasonality is extreme and structural.** Q1 typically delivers 12–15% of annual revenue, Q2 ~20%, Q3 ~25% and Q4 40–45% *(per Q2FY26 concall)*. Q4FY26 alone produced **75% of FY26 PAT** (₹56.65 cr of ₹75.5 cr). Fixed costs are spread evenly across the year, so Q1 margins are always thin and single quarters say little about the full year.

**Q1FY27 read-through:**
- **Revenue:** up +17.4% YoY. Jash standalone grew 21%, with standalone PAT margin near 13%.
- **PAT:** swung from −₹5.17 cr to +₹5.09 cr.
- **Gross margin** held near the Q4 level of ~60% *(per Q1FY27 concall)*.

The headline flatters the result in three ways:
- **Tariff refund:** a ₹5.6 cr US tariff refund was booked as a reduction in material consumption. On its own it roughly equals the quarter's PAT.
- **Other income** of ₹6.13 cr, mostly forex, supported the result.
- **Revenue-recognition timing:** a ₹36 cr overflow from March was recognised in Q1, offset by a ₹27–28 cr reversal at June-end, for a net benefit of ~₹10 cr.

About ₹15 cr of finished goods for Qatar and Singapore could not be shipped, because of the Gulf conflict and a Singapore client's overdue payments *(per Q1FY27 concall)*.

**Capacity math (management):**
- Current plants (five in India plus Rodney Hunt and UK) can produce **~₹1,200 cr** a year if run on two to three shifts.
- Adding Houston and Saudi Arabia lifts this to **₹1,500 cr+**, enough for the FY31 target *(per Q4FY26 concall)*.
- **India capacity is sufficient to FY28.** New India capacity would only be considered if the data-centre vessel opportunity (Section 4) proves sustained *(per Q1FY27 concall)*.

**Operating model:**
- Complex, long-lead, high-margin products are made in India.
- Short-lead or "local content" products are made in the US, UK or Saudi Arabia. For example, of Rodney Hunt's $36M revenue, $9–10M was produced in India *(per Q3FY26 concall)*.
- About 70% of the current US order book is planned for manufacture in America, which limits exposure to tariffs on exports from India.

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

1. **US tariff reset with a built-in margin buffer.**
   - The US tariff on Jash's products is now **15.6%** (10% special + 5.6% existing) *(per Q1FY27 concall)*.
   - **Over 80% of Rodney Hunt's ~$28–29M legacy order book was priced assuming a 50% tariff** *(per Q4FY26 concall)*, so executing it should earn above-normal margins.
   - Offset: raw material costs are up ~20% in both India and the US.
   - Management targets an 8–9% PAT margin at Rodney Hunt in FY27 and ~10% by FY28.
2. **Tariff refunds.** ₹5.6 cr has been received (in Q1FY27) and **~₹7.5 cr more** is being filed, with recovery expected within FY27 *(per Q1FY27 concall)*.
3. **Rupee depreciation tailwind.** Export orders quoted at ~₹82/$ are being billed at ~₹91–92/$. **More than 75% of the ~₹640–675 cr export order book** qualifies for this gain, provided the dollar does not weaken *(per Q4FY26 concall)*. This is why Q4FY26 gross margin expanded.
4. **Data-centre cooling pressure vessels (new, Aug-2026).**
   - A water-hammer surge vessel adapted for data-centre cooling vendors. The first order was for 4 units; a 32-unit order is under negotiation; **quotes cover 600+ vessels**.
   - Each vessel sells for ₹30–60 lakh. Existing capacity is ~75–80 vessels a year, or ~₹25–30 cr of revenue.
   - Margins are expected to be similar to the rest of the business *(per Q1FY27 concall)*.
   - This is a genuine new market, and it could justify a new plant after FY28.
5. **India wastewater cycle.** Management expects Jal Jeevan Mission supply-side spending to complete by 2028–29, which should shift spending to wastewater treatment, reuse and desalination, and unlock "huge potential after 2028–29" *(per Q4FY26 concall)*. Jash already supplies multi-product packages for flagship projects: Bandra STP, Ghatkopar and a ₹35 cr+ Chennai desalination plant for VA Tech Wabag.
6. **Nuclear and desalination credentials.** NPCIL now rates Jash one of its best vendors, earned at the cost of a ₹7–8 cr loss-making entry order. This positions it for private-sector nuclear opening, though domestically only. Jash also has specialist desalination gates.
7. **Rising sea levels, a long-dated option.**
   - **Hong Kong:** the ~₹40 cr Yuen Long barrage order is under execution.
   - **Singapore:** Jash has submitted **budgetary offers of $800–900M for project-report purposes (not bids)**. Tendering starts in 2028, with first orders in 2029 at the earliest, spread over 15–25 years *(per Q4FY26 and Q1FY27 concalls)*.
   - This is not a near-term earnings driver.
8. **Product-basket deepening through acquisitions.** WesTech brought vortex grit separator technology that Jash had sought for years; 12 orders have already been won through Jash. Penstocks UK brings a fast "lego-block" gate design that management wants to sell worldwide *(per Q3FY26 and Q1FY27 concalls)*.
9. **UK scale-up.**
   - The Waterfront + Penstocks UK team has grown from 9 to 26–27 people.
   - FY27 revenue target is £5–6M (~₹60–70 cr), with profits guided *(per Q4FY26 and Q1FY27 concalls)*.
   - The goal is ₹125–135 cr of combined revenue in 3–4 years, timed to the UK's Ofwat AMP spending cycle.
   - The UK FTA is an added tailwind.

### Investment Verdict

**Rating: REDUCE.** The probability-weighted fair value is **≈ ₹490.6/share**, against a CMP of ₹589. The stock is ~20% above fair value, an expected return of about −17% to fair value. It would become an **ACCUMULATE below ~₹490** and a **BUY below ~₹440**.

**Bull Case (25%, ₹768):** The US tariff settles near 15.6% and the 100% Russian-oil tariff never arrives. The legacy Rodney Hunt orders priced at 50% tariff are executed with fat margins. The rupee stays above ₹90/$, the pending ₹7.5 cr refund is collected, and Gulf shipments clear before Q4. The data-centre vessel opportunity becomes a real ₹25–30 cr business with a plant decision in view. FY27 revenue beats the ₹875 cr guide, PAT reaches ~₹120 cr, and the market pays ~40x for a proven 15–18% compounder with 17%+ ROCE.

**Base Case (50%, ₹468):** FY27 lands close to guidance but slightly short, as FY26 did. Revenue of ~₹830 cr from operations and PAT of ~₹95 cr are a solid recovery from FY26's ₹75.5 cr. Margins are held back by 20% raw-material inflation, the Jash Process Equipment turnaround, and Q1 profits that rested on one-off refunds and forex. The business then compounds at ~14%, and the 31x multiple is consistent with a 28x terminal multiple in FY30.

**Bear Case (25%, ₹259):** US policy turns hostile again with the 100% tariff, so the Rodney Hunt order book slows and exports from India stall. The Gulf and Red Sea blockages persist into the Q4 shipping window, which decides the year. The Singapore receivable goes bad, and the fourth guidance cut in 18 months triggers a de-rating to ~24x on ~₹68 cr of PAT.

**Bottom line:** Jash is a high-quality niche franchise. It has 50 years of engineering depth and a product basket no other Indian competitor matches. Its domestic business is growing ~18% at good margins, standalone PAT margins held at 13–14.5% through a very difficult year, and management is unusually candid. But the market already prices in management's full FY27 guidance of ₹100–105 cr PAT (~36x) and more. That guidance comes from a team that has cut revenue targets repeatedly, and it depends on the US policy environment, Gulf shipping lanes and forex levels, none of which the company controls. Add deteriorating receivables, a struggling acquisition, and ~₹140 cr of overseas capex that has doubled in cost, and there is no margin of safety at ₹589. Wait for a pullback towards ₹440–490, or for a clean H1/9M FY27 print that shifts weight to the Bull case.

## 1. Business Model Overview

Arcil was incorporated in Feb-2002. It was **India's first ARC**: it received its RBI certificate of registration on 29-Aug-2003 and made its first acquisition in Dec-2003. It is the **second-largest ARC by AUM** (₹16,853 Cr at Mar-2025) and was the **second most profitable private ARC** in FY25 *(RHP, Our Business, citing CRISIL)*. It runs 13 offices in 12 states with only **206 employees**, plus 206 collection agencies, 218 valuers and 988 empanelled lawyers *(RHP, Our Business)*.

### How an ARC makes money

1. A bank or NBFC auctions a stressed loan or a pool of loans. Arcil wins at a discount: its cumulative cost has been **49.07% of principal debt** (₹44,114 Cr paid for ₹89,909 Cr of principal) *(RHP, Our Business)*.
2. An Arcil-sponsored **trust** buys the assets and issues **Security Receipts (SRs)** to the seller and other Qualified Buyers. Arcil must hold at least the higher of **15% of the transferor's SRs or 2.5% of total SRs** *(RHP; RBI framework Oct-2022)*.
3. Arcil resolves the assets through settlement, restructuring, SARFAESI enforcement, DRT, or IBC. Arcil is one of only ~5 ARCs with net owned funds above ₹1,000 Cr, which allows it to act as a **resolution applicant under IBC**.
4. **Revenue streams:**
   - **Fee income:** management/trusteeship fees of **0.25%–5.0% of AUM**, charged only on AUM under 8 years old, plus collection fees and upside incentives.
   - **Investment income:** realised gains on Arcil's own SRs above cost.
   - **Unrealised fair-value (FV) gains/losses:** SRs are marked to the NAV declared on the basis of recovery ratings.
   - **Recoveries on SRs already written off:** under RBI rules, SRs older than 8 years must be written off, but collection continues.

### Key Observations

1. **FY26 was a capital-deployment year, not a collections year.** Acquisitions rose 50% to ₹5,959 Cr and AUM grew 19.6%. But **collections fell 10%** to ₹3,484 Cr, top-10 portfolio recoveries fell 70%, and recoveries from the old (more than 8-year) book fell 62%. The growth went into the book; the cash has not yet come back.
2. **The quality of reported PAT is weaker than the headline.** Unrealised FV gains were **₹195 Cr, or 35.5% of FY26 PBT** (24.8% in FY25). Management attributes them to rating upgrades and to *"higher initial ratings on certain cash investments"* made during the year *(RHP, MD&A)*, which means day-one gains on new deals. Excluding FV gains, **PAT has been flat at ~₹265 Cr for two years** *(analyst estimate)*. OCF of ₹154 Cr was **only 38% of PAT**.
3. **Fee income re-rated sharply (+63%).** Consolidated management fees rose from ₹112 Cr to ₹180 Cr as structured-deal AUM grew 48%. This is the most "annuity-like" stream, and it is now ~37% of standalone revenue.
4. **The balance sheet started to lever up.** Borrowings rose about 4× to ₹1,205 Cr, D/E went from 0.11 to 0.39, CRAR fell from 88% to 65% and finance cost nearly tripled. 68% of debt matures within a year, while the SR assets resolve over 3–8 years. Capital is still ample by ARC standards: the NOF of ₹2,577 Cr is roughly 8.6× the RBI minimum of ₹300 Cr.
5. **The legacy tailwind is fading.** Recovery of previously written-off SRs and fees fell from ₹287 Cr (FY24) to ₹100 Cr (FY25) and ₹66 Cr (FY26). Management says those balances were *"substantially recovered"* in FY24–FY25 *(RHP, MD&A)*. Future growth has to come from the post-2018 book.
6. **ROE is good for an ARC (~14%) but only around the cost of equity.** Arcil is the best-run private ARC on efficiency, but at an unlevered ~14% ROE it does not deserve a high P/B multiple.

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

### DNA Shift: from a legacy-recovery ARC to a fee-led, retail-tilted platform

| Dimension | FY24 | FY26 | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue driver | Recoveries of written-off SRs (₹287 Cr, 50% of revenue) | Fees (₹280 Cr, 37%) + FV gains (₹195 Cr, 26%) | From legacy windfalls to running-book income |
| Corporate share of AUM | 78.5% | 68.8% | Diversifying |
| Retail share of AUM | 12.8% | 23.6% | Growing fast |
| Structured deals share of AUM | 37.3% | 55.8% | Fee-heavy, capped upside |
| Leverage (D/E) | 0.06 | 0.39 | Levering up to fund growth |
| Ownership | Private (Avenue/SBI) | Listed, 21.3% free float | Market discipline, disclosure |

### Catalysts

1. **Regulatory tailwinds.** The RBI's move to an **expected credit loss (ECL) framework** for banks is expected to *"trigger further sale of stressed assets"* in retail and MSME *(RHP, citing CRISIL)*. Other recent changes: SR TDS cut from 30% to 10% (Feb-2025); NBFCs and HFCs allowed to invest in SRs; ARCs permitted to buy SMA-0 accounts; and the consolidated ARC Directions of Nov-2025, which create a level field for ARCs with strong governance.
2. **Credit-cycle optionality.** Bank GNPA is at a cyclical low of 1.8% (Mar-2026). The RBI stress test projects it rising to 1.9% (baseline) or 3.8–4.1% (adverse) by FY28. NBFC GNPA is projected at 2.8% in FY27 versus 2.4% now *(RHP, Industry Overview)*. ARCs are counter-cyclical, so **a downturn increases supply and lowers acquisition prices**.
3. **Retail stress pipeline.** Retail-segment stress across banks and NBFCs is ₹6.96 lakh Cr, and unsecured personal loan and credit card stress grew at ~24% CAGR over FY20–FY26 *(CRISIL)*. Arcil has the infrastructure in place: 32.9 lakh borrowers and a digital collections stack.
4. **Competitor weakness.** Edelweiss ARC's AUM collapsed from ₹371 bn (FY23) to ₹90.5 bn (FY26), and JM Financial ARC is barely profitable. Arcil's AA- rating, its SBI sponsorship and its first-mover relationships with 138 selling institutions should let it gain share among private ARCs.
5. **Fee-income compounding.** About 86% of FY26 acquisitions were structured deals, which pay management fees on the AUM for up to 8 years. The ₹5,959 Cr FY26 vintage adds roughly ₹60–90 Cr of annual fees at a 1–1.5% fee rate *(analyst estimate)*.
6. **Upgrade-driven FV gains and hidden value.** RR2-rated SRs rose to 54.8% of the rated book from 45.0%. If the FY25–FY26 vintages are upgraded as they resolve, FV gains can repeat. The 8-year bucket (₹7,040 Cr of AUM, Arcil's share ₹760 Cr) is largely written off, so any recovery there is pure P&L upside.
7. **Listing as an ARC "pure-play".** Arcil is the only listed ARC in India and has no direct listed peer, which may attract scarcity flows, especially once Q2FY27 results establish a reporting track record.

**Peer context.** No Indian ARC is listed, and the RHP explicitly declines a peer P/E comparison. The listed government-backed foreign "AMCs" it names (China Huarong, Bangkok Commercial Asset Management) have historically traded **below book value**. Indian specialty lenders with ~13–15% ROE and lumpy earnings typically trade at ~1.2–2.0× book *(analyst judgement; not from the RHP)*. On fundamentals, a 14% ROE against a ~13% cost of equity justifies roughly **1.3–1.4× book** (Gordon: (ROE − g)/(Ke − g) with g ≈ 10.5% from ~75% retention). **The IPO was priced fairly rather than cheaply.** The flat listing despite 20× subscription (QIB-driven, with weak retail demand at 3.4×) is consistent with that.

### What to Track Each Earnings Call / Filing

1. **First listed results (Q2FY27, due ~Nov-2026).** The split of income into fees, realised investment income and FV gains, plus H1 collections against H1FY26.
2. **Recovery cadence.** Collections, redemptions, and progress on the top-10 portfolios and real-estate resolutions.
3. **Rating migration.** RR-band distribution of SRs. Upgrades drive FV gains; downgrades reverse them.
4. **Acquisition pipeline.** Volume, deal-structure mix (structured vs cash) and Arcil's investment share, plus any sign of ECL-driven sales by banks.
5. **Funding.** Debt mix, maturity profile (short-term share), cost of funds, D/E, and any NCD issuance.
6. **Regulatory.** RBI inspection closure, the settlement-policy changes and any penalty.
7. **New businesses.** CaaS mandates, MFI pools and priority-debt funding in real estate.
8. **Shareholder events.** Avenue/SBI sell-downs to meet the 25% minimum public shareholding, and lock-in expiry of pre-IPO holders (~Mar-2027).
9. **Disclosure quality.** Whether the standalone/consolidated bridge and the KPI definitions are reported consistently each quarter.

### Investment Verdict

**Weighted Fair Value: ₹150 | CMP: ₹145.7 | Expected return: +3% (+~2% dividend yield) | Rating: ACCUMULATE** (BUY below ₹135; REDUCE above ₹157.5)

- **Bull (20%): ₹214.** ECL-driven NPA sales and peer weakness lift AUM and fees. Recoveries rebound and FV upgrades recur. ROE reaches ~16% and the market pays 1.9× book / 14× earnings.
- **Base (55%): ₹150.** AUM grows ~10% a year funded by retained earnings plus debt, the fee yield is ~1.4% and the SR book returns ~12.5%. PAT compounds ~10%, ROE stays ~14%, and the stock is worth 1.4× book / 11× earnings, close to where it trades today.
- **Bear (25%): ₹97.** SR rating downgrades reverse FV gains, collections stay weak, eight-year write-offs rise, and the RBI acts on its repeated observations. ROE falls to ~10%, and the stock trades at book like global distressed-asset AMCs.

**Bottom line:** Arcil is the best-capitalised, most efficient private ARC in India: the lowest opex/AUM, the highest PAT/AUM, an AA- rating and SBI as co-sponsor. It is also the only listed way to play India's stressed-asset cycle. However, the IPO was a pure exit by the sponsors, it raised no growth capital, and it was priced at ~1.5× book for a ~14% ROE business. More than a third of FY26 pre-tax profit was unrealised mark-ups, and collections fell. **At ₹145.7 the stock is roughly fairly valued.** Accumulate in small amounts for its counter-cyclical optionality, and buy more decisively below ₹135. The Q2FY27 print and the H1 collections trend are the first real tests of whether FY26's FV-led growth turns into cash.

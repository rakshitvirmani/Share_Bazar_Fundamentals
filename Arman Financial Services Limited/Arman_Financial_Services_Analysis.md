# Arman Financial Services Limited — Equity Analysis

## 1. Business Model Overview

**Core Business:** Arman Financial Services is a diversified microfinance and NBFC holding company with two distinct operational segments:

1. **Microfinance (Namra Finance subsidiary)** — Historical core, now under stress
   - Target: Low-income rural households, group-based lending (JLG model)
   - Loan sizes: ₹40k-₹60k per borrower (moving away from rigid group model)
   - Portfolio decline: ₹2,193 Cr (FY24) → ₹1,686 Cr (FY25) → ₹1,554 Cr (Q1 FY26)
   - Business model shift: From pure JLG (joint liability group) to individual credit assessment
   - Key challenge: Structural deleveraging in rural India; credit saturation and farmer stress

2. **Standalone Businesses (Growth engines)** — Arman brand, expanding rapidly
   - **MSME lending:** Individual business loans, expanding with 600+ branches
   - **2-wheeler financing:** Growing 20%+ YoY
   - **Micro-LAP (Loan Against Property):** New product launched, pilot in Telangana, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh
   - Portfolio growth: ₹560 Cr (FY25) → ₹602 Cr (Q1 FY26), +29% YoY
   - Asset quality: MSME GNPA 3.38%, 2-wheeler GNPA 4.7%, Micro-LAP 0% (new)

**Geographic Focus:**
- Pan-India presence with ~391 branches
- Stress concentrated in specific geographies: Gujarat (eastern areas, western areas with guardrail issues), Rajasthan, parts of UP
- Less stress: Maharashtra (showing improvement), Karnataka (<1% of portfolio), Madhya Pradesh (LAP pilot showing 0 NPA)

**Regulatory/Guarantee Environment:**
- CGFMU (Credit Guarantee Fund for Micro Units) coverage: 34% of MFI AUM as of March 2025
- Coverage protects up to 75% of defaults on 600+ CIBIL score borrowers
- New MFIN guardrails (maximum debt caps, customer assessment requirements) affecting lending velocity

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

### A. **Standalone Business Acceleration — Counter-Cyclical Growth Engine**
- **Problem:** MFI in structural decline; need offsetting growth
- **Solution:** MSME (29% YoY), 2-wheeler (20%+ YoY), Micro-LAP (new, 0 NPA) businesses scaling rapidly
- **Opportunity size:** Addressable market for MSME lending in India >₹1,000 Cr; 2-wheeler financing multi-billion dollar TAM
- **Runway:** Management expects MSME/2-wheeler to contribute 40%+ of AUM by FY27 (vs. ~27% currently)
- **Risk/Upside:** Requires capital (management has headroom), execution on underwriting, and branch productivity

### B. **Rural Credit Cycle Bottom & Recovery** — Macro Tailwind
- **Problem:** MFI AUM collapsed from ₹4.5 Lakh Cr (Dec 2023) to ₹3.3 Lakh Cr (current); rejection rates 75-81%
- **Root cause:** Farmer stress (low real income growth +4-5% over 4 years), high household debt (retail debt +40-50% on household balance sheet), liquidity drought
- **Opportunity:** Monsoons, agriculture output recovery, rural income stabilization = MFI demand should normalize by Q3-Q4 FY26
- **Management view:** "Once situation improves, bouncing back will be quick" — but "growth unlikely to normalize until sector deleverage complete"
- **Catalysts to watch:** Monsoon forecast (July-Sept 2025), farm output indices, rural wage trends

### C. **Asset Quality Stabilization** — Structural Risk Reduction
- **Current:** GNPA 3.45% (Q1 FY26) — uncomfortable but not crisis-level
- **Initiative:** Accelerated write-offs, CGFMU coverage expansion (34% → 50%+), separation of credit/recovery, new BCM underwriting model
- **Progress:** Collections improved 91% → 98.8% (March-June 2025); early bucket stabilizing (Q3 FY25 impairment ₹68 Cr → Q1 FY26 ₹59 Cr)
- **Timeline:** Asset quality should stabilize by Q3 FY26 if rural conditions don't deteriorate further
- **Upside:** If CGFMU covers 75% of defaults on 600-score customers, credit cost pressure eases materially (saves ₹20-30 Cr annually)

### D. **Model Transformation — From Group-Lending to Individual Credit** — Strategic Pivot
- **Old model:** JLG (joint liability group) — 1-size-fits-all, macro credit checks, group dynamics
- **New model:** Individual assessment per customer — cash flows, business type, local geography, collateral
- **Impact:** Higher operating cost (-1% OPEX headwind) but better underwriting, lower stress concentration
- **Competitive advantage:** Separation of credit/recovery at 140 branches already showing early positive indicators
- **Risk:** Execution on 391 branches; training, systems, accountability; attrition among staff (currently 62-63%, target 40%)

### E. **2-Wheeler & Micro-LAP Growth — High-Margin Segments**
- **2-wheeler:** Growing 20%+ YoY; GNPA 4.7% (manageable); disbursements +10% in Q1 FY26; ticket size ₹60,000+ (higher than MFI)
- **Micro-LAP:** Brand new segment; 0 NPA so far; collateral-backed (property); targeting ₹100+ Cr AUM by FY27
- **Upside:** 2-wheeler yields higher NIM than MFI; LAP provides collateral cushion; should offset MFI margin compression

### F. **Capitalization Flexibility** — Room to Grow Without Distress**
- **Current:** Capital adequacy 37% (standalone), 48% (Namra) — well above regulatory 15%
- **Debt-equity:** 1.3x (well-managed); management comfortable with 4-4.5x medium-term
- **Implication:** Can support ₹500-750 Cr additional disbursements before need for capital raise; runway into FY27

## Summary

**Arman Financial Services** is a **high-risk, high-reward turnaround story** caught in an industry-wide MFI deleveraging cycle, but building sustainable alternate growth engines (MSME, 2-wheeler, LAP).

✅ **Strengths:**
- Standalone business (MSME +29%, 2-wheeler +20% YoY) providing growth offset
- Aggressive structural reforms (credit/recovery separation, BCM model, individual assessment) addressing root causes of underwriting failure
- Strong balance sheet (CAR 37%, D/E 1.3x) with capital headroom for growth
- CGFMU guarantee coverage expanding (34% → 50%+ target) reducing credit risk
- Asset quality showing early stabilization signs (collections 91% → 98.8%, impairment cost declining)

⚠️ **Critical Risks:**
- MFI structural decline with no floor visibility (₹2,193 Cr → ₹1,554 Cr; management expects sector deleverage to continue)
- First quarterly loss raises profitability sustainability questions
- High employee attrition (45-63%) threatening operational execution
- Operating cost inflation (32% → 44% cost-to-income ratio) needs reversal by Q3 FY26 or model breaks
- Macro-dependent recovery (monsoons, farm sector, rural wages) — many external variables

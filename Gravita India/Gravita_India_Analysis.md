## 1. Business Model Overview

**Company:** Gravita India Limited – India's leading secondary lead, copper, aluminum, and plastic recycler.

**Business Model:**
- **Primary Revenue Driver:** Recycling of lead-acid batteries, copper scrap, aluminum scrap, plastic waste into high-purity metals and alloys
- **Segments:** 
  - **Lead (60% revenue):** Largest segment; secondary lead for batteries, alloys, ammunition
  - **Copper (new, ramp phase):** Value-added copper alloys; Rashriya Metal Industries acquisition (2024)
  - **Aluminum:** Cast alloys, aerospace-grade ingots
  - **Plastic:** Engineering plastics from e-waste
  - **Lithium (emerging):** Developing lithium-ion battery recycling (R&D stage)

**Competitive Advantages (Moat: 8.5/10):**
1. **LME Accreditation** (2026) – Lead products listed on London Metal Exchange; only select Indian secondary lead recyclers qualify; opens global OEM markets (critical for exports)
2. **Scale & Cost Leadership** – 4.97 Lakh MT installed capacity; 55k MT/quarter throughput; economies of scale in procurement
3. **Value-Added Product Focus** – 63% of revenue from value-added products (alloys, specialized grades); 100% hedged for commodity exposure
4. **Backward Integration** – Building own scrap procurement yards in developed nations (US, EU) to reduce sourcing costs and improve margins
5. **Regulatory Barriers** – Environmental compliance (zero-discharge plants), capital intensity of facilities creates high barriers to entry

**Revenue Mix (Q1 FY27):**
- Consolidated Revenue: ₹1,475 Cr (42% YoY growth)
- Value-added products: 63% of mix (higher margin)
- Geographic: India ~65%, Exports ~35% (expanding via LME listing)

   - **Impact:** Opens premium OEM markets (automotive, aerospace, defense); reduces middleman costs; improves pricing power
   - **Upside:** Global demand for premium recycled lead, esp. in EV battery markets (lithium supply chain)

2. **Copper Division Ramp-Up**
   - Rashriya Metal integration complete by Q2 FY27
   - Gujarat facility commissioning (12 months = Q2 FY27-28)
   - **Copper Margin Path:** INR 55k/ton (current) → INR 60k/ton (FY28) → INR 75k/ton (FY30)
   - **Volume Contribution:** Copper expected to be 20-25% of consolidated revenue by FY29

3. **Value-Added Product Expansion**
   - Already at 63% of revenue; target 70%+ by FY28
   - Aluminum inclusion on MCX (Metals & Commodities Exchange) pending MCX approval
   - ADC12 alloy for automotive, aerospace applications gaining traction

4. **Backward Integration Yield**
   - Own scrap procurement yards in developed nations (US, EU) reduce logistics costs and improve supply stability
   - Expected to contribute margin uplift of 200-300 bps over 2-3 years

**Medium-Term Catalysts (FY29-FY30):**

5. **Lithium-Ion Battery Recycling Scale-Up**
   - R&D underway; targeting black mass processing by FY29
   - EV battery recycling is secular growth driver globally
   - Early mover advantage in India could be substantial

6. **ROCE Expansion Path**
   - Current ROCE: 20% → Target: 25%+ by FY30 (via copper integration and value-add focus)
   - Indicates sustainable competitive advantage strengthening

**DNA Shift:**
- **From:** Single-commodity (lead) recycler with margin pressure
- **To:** Diversified, value-added metals & materials recycler with pricing power, global reach (LME), and emerging battery recycling franchise

|------|----------|---------|-----------|

| **Capacity Utilization Risk** | 6/10 | New capacity (copper, lithium) may take 18-24 months to ramp to target utilization. Capex of ₹1,680 Cr assumes scale realization. | Staggered capex; phased commissioning; management has track record of 70%+ ramp-up success |
| **Copper Alloy Margin Compression** | 5/10 | Copper segment margins lower than lead (4-5% vs. 7-8%) initially; scale required to expand to 7-8%. | Product mix shift to higher-value alloys; backward integration should expand margin to 7-8% over 2-3 years |
| **Environmental & Regulatory** | 4/10 | Recycling is capital-intensive; stricter emission norms could increase opex. Lead handling requires strict compliance. | Zero-discharge facilities already built; strong compliance track record; ISO/LME certifications provide buffer |
| **Lithium Battery Competition** | 5/10 | Lithium recycling is emerging space; multiple large global players entering India (Tesla, Redwood Materials, LG). | Early-mover advantage; focus on black mass (lower value-add initially but learning curve); tie-ups with OEMs |
| **Execution Risk (Capex)** | 4/10 | ₹1,680 Cr capex through FY29 requires disciplined execution; delays possible in copper/lithium facilities. | Management has delivered on prior capex timelines; internal funding reduces financing risk; phased approach |
| **Inventory Build Risk** | 3/10 | Current inventory ₹1,040 Cr (June 2026); scrap sourcing uncertainty could lead to excess working capital. | Management actively managing; inventory cycle to normalize as supply improves; working capital at 95 days is manageable |
| **Currency Risk** | 3/10 | ~35% revenue from exports; INR appreciation could compress margins on export realizations. | Partial natural hedge via imported scrap costs; minimal FX derivative exposure |
| **Demand Recession (Cyclical)** | 3/10 | Global recession could reduce OEM demand for recycled metals; battery demand pullback. | Diversified customer base (OEMs, non-ferrous smelters, alloy makers); value-add focus provides stickiness |

**Most Critical Risks to Monitor:**
1. Copper margin realization (needs to reach 7-8% by FY28-29 for guidance)
2. Copper capacity utilization (must hit 70%+ by FY28)

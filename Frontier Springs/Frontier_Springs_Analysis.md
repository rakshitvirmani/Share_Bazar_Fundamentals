## 1. Business Model Overview

Frontier Springs is a family-run railway components maker in Kanpur, incorporated in 1981. The business goes back to 1968, when it made leaf springs for trucks. It moved from leaf springs to hot-coiled springs for Indian Railways in 1991, and coil springs have been its mainstay for over 30 years. It entered forgings in 2011 and air springs in 2022 *(per Q1FY27 presentation)*. Its springs are fitted on the Vande Bharat Express. It holds RDSO approval and IRIS certification (the international rail-industry standard, which allows direct exports).

**Three divisions (FY26, gross revenue including GST, approximate — per MD, Q4FY26 concall):**

| Division | FY26 Gross Revenue | Share (approx.) | Products | Market Share (Indian Railways demand) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Coil Springs** | ~₹175 cr | ~45% | Suspension springs for coaching stock, freight stock (wagons) and diesel/electric locos; wire sizes of 10–65 mm; chrome-molybdenum and chrome-silicon steel | **~40%** |
| **Air Springs** | ~₹150 cr | ~39% | Secondary suspension for LHB/Vande Bharat/EMU coaches; built under a technology MoU with ContiTech (Continental), Germany, and a Quant-Technik GmbH MoU for LHB coaches | **~40%** |
| **Forgings** | ~₹60 cr+ | ~16% | Anti-roll bar assemblies, screw couplings, draft gear, BSS hangers, brake-block hangers, knuckle throwers; 1-tonne, 3-tonne and 6-tonne hammers (parts from 100 g to 80 kg) | **~20–25%** |

*Market shares per Q2FY26 concall. Management quotes "gross" revenue including GST. FY26 gross revenue was above the ₹375 cr guide, against ₹322 cr of reported (net) revenue from operations, so net is roughly 0.85–0.86x gross. This matters when reading the ₹500 cr FY27 guidance (see Section 6).*

**Customers:**
- Indian Railways is effectively the whole business: production units (RCF Kapurthala, MCF Rae Bareli, ICF Chennai, CLW, BLW, PLW) and zonal railways.
- Wagon builders (Titagarh, Texmaco, Jupiter) are only **10–15% of revenue** *(per Q2FY26 concall)*.
- Business comes through a continuous stream of small tenders (₹8–10 cr/day of orders at times), not a few large contracts. Contracts are **fixed-price with set delivery schedules**, so revenue follows Railways' delivery calendar and cannot be pulled forward *(per Q2FY26 and Q4FY26 concalls)*.

**Competitive position.** There are only "1 or 2" other approved coil-spring makers and "1 or 2" approved air-spring makers; forgings has 7–8 approved suppliers *(per Q2FY26 concall)*. Railways vendor approval takes about 2 years: RDSO approval, then trial orders, then 1–1.5 years in field service before regular orders. That is a real barrier to new entrants. Frontier has supplied **~15,000 air springs over 2.5 years with no field failures**. Some rivals import air springs from China and have had failures *(per Q2FY26 concall)*.

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

1. **Structural shift from coil to air springs.**
   - All new coaches from RCF, MCF and ICF use air springs for secondary suspension and coil springs for primary suspension.
   - About **15,000–20,000 older ICF coaches** running on coil springs are due for replacement over the next ~3 years, and Railways builds ~6,000–7,000 coaches a year.
   - Air springs and coil springs are complements, not substitutes, so both divisions benefit *(per Q2FY26 concall)*.
2. **Railway capex tailwind.**
   - Railways capital outlay is ₹2.65 lakh cr.
   - Production plan: ~6,000 coaches and 1,200–1,400 locomotives a year.
   - A big wagon tender is pending. Railways has placed orders for ~72,000 wagons, with 40,000 more expected, and targets 90,000 wagons over 3 years *(per Q4FY26 concall and Q1FY27 presentation)*.
   - Vande Bharat: **260 rakes planned**. Sleeper Vande Bharat: 12 trains this year and "200+ next year". Frontier's sleeper-variant coil springs are already approved *(per Q4FY26 concall)*.
3. **FIBA (Failure Indication and Brake Application system).**
   - An air-spring safety system that applies the brakes automatically if an air spring fails. Each coach needs 2 FIBA units and 4 indicators.
   - Currently imported (Knorr-Bremse and one other). Frontier's design is patented and developed in-house.
   - **Addressable market:** the estimate has been cut from ~₹100 cr (Nov-25) to ₹40–50 cr (Jun-26). Margins are expected to be similar to air springs.
   - **Status:** RDSO developmental-vendor approval received in Q1FY27. Samples for **25 coach sets entering a 12-month trial** *(per Q1FY27 presentation)*.
   - **The timeline has slipped every quarter:**

   | Call | FIBA revenue expected |
   |---|---|
   | Nov-25 | From Dec-2026 |
   | Jun-26 | Q4FY27 orders, then ₹20–25 cr in FY28 |
   | Aug-26 (12-month trial) | Realistically FY29 |

4. **Forging diversification.**
   - The 6-tonne hammer has two-thirds of its capacity unused.
   - Management is pursuing:
     - Defence (samples submitted; process "slow")
     - Construction equipment (L&T, JCB, Caterpillar)
     - Crane hooks
     - **Forging exports** to Europe and the US, where forging capacity is shrinking
   - Automotive is excluded as a no-margin business.
   - Success would diversify the business away from Railways and absorb fixed costs *(per Q4FY26 concall)*.
5. **Metro, EMU and OEM air springs.**
   - Air-spring approval for metro coaches is being pursued, and EMUs already use air and coil springs.
   - Talks with **Alstom** (and earlier Siemens) on supplying air springs to rolling-stock OEMs were described as "on the verge of completion" in Nov-2025 *(per Q2FY26 concall)*.
6. **Raw-material relief.** The Q1FY27 presentation notes a "softening of raw material prices observed in recent months". Steel is the main input, and steel inflation compressed margins in Q3FY26 and caused the Q1FY27 dip. New Railways tenders are being priced higher to recover cost increases *(per Q4FY26 concall)*.
7. **Product-basket expansion.** For the ₹500 cr → ₹1,000 cr leg, management says "1 or 2 products" requested by Railways have been chosen, but are undisclosed and "on a very initial basis" *(per Q2FY26 concall)*.
8. **NSE listing.** Planned for Q1FY27 but not yet done. It would improve liquidity and institutional access; FII ownership is currently 0% and DII 0.36%.

### Investment Verdict

**Rating: REDUCE (borderline).** The probability-weighted fair value is **≈ ₹1,348.9/share**, against a CMP of ₹1,447. The stock is ~7.3% above fair value, an expected return of about −6.8%. That puts it just over the 5% REDUCE line, so a single good quarter would move it to HOLD. It would become an **ACCUMULATE below ~₹1,349** and a **BUY below ~₹1,214**.

**Bull Case (25%, ₹2,085):** The Q1 disruption proves temporary and the quarterly ceiling breaks. The plant runs near full capacity from Q2, the big wagon tender lands, air-spring output moves above 250 coach sets a month, and Alstom orders finally arrive. Revenue approaches the guide (~₹410 cr net), and 26%+ EBITDA margins hold as steel softens. The 6-tonne hammer finds non-Railways customers. The market keeps paying 30x+ for a business earning 40% ROE and growing 25% a year with near-zero debt.

**Base Case (50%, ₹1,311):** Growth resumes but falls well short of the headline guide. FY27 revenue is ~₹360 cr (+12%) and PAT is flat at ~₹62 cr, because EBITDA margins normalise to ~24.5% and the tax rate is higher. From FY28 the business compounds at ~18% on the Railways capex cycle, sleeper Vande Bharat, wagon orders and a gradual forging ramp, with FIBA a late-FY28/FY29 contributor. A 25x multiple reconciles with a 22x terminal multiple at FY30.

**Bear Case (25%, ₹687):** The ₹75–83 cr a quarter ceiling proves structural, not temporary. Revenue is flat, steel and tender pricing squeeze margins towards ~21%, and FIBA and forging slip again. The market re-rates the stock as a Railways-dependent tender supplier at ~17x, closer to where it traded before FY25.

**Bottom line:** Frontier Springs has transformed. In two years it has gone from a sub-scale, 12–15%-margin spring maker to a ~40% ROE business with ~40% share in both coil and air springs. It sits behind a 2-year Railways approval barrier and on the right side of the coil-to-air-spring transition. The Railways demand story is real, and management delivered its FY26 headline numbers. But the stock now prices in a return to growth that the last five quarters have not shown. Revenue has been flat at ₹75–83 cr a quarter, Q1FY27 went backwards, cash conversion was only 51%, and every secondary growth lever (FIBA, forging, Alstom, defence) has slipped. At ~28x Base FY27E earnings there is no margin of safety.

**Action:** Trim or avoid at ₹1,447. Revisit after the Q2FY27 result (due Nov-2026). A quarter above ₹95 cr of revenue at a 25%+ EBITDA margin would validate the guide and justify HOLD or better. Buy on a pullback towards ₹1,200–1,350.

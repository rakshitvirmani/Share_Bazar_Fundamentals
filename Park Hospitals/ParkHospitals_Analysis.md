## 1. Business Model Overview

Park Medi World Limited (brand: Park Hospitals) is North India's second-largest private hospital chain and the largest in Haryana. Founded in 2005, the company operates on a differentiated **affordable, quality healthcare** model targeting the bottom and middle of India's socioeconomic pyramid — a segment management estimates at 128 crore out of 140 crore Indians.

**Network (as of Q3 FY26):**
- 14 multi-super-specialty hospitals across Haryana, Punjab, Delhi, and Rajasthan
- 3,250 installed beds including ~870 ICU beds
- 30 super-specialties and specialty services
- 1,200+ doctors (75% specialists), 2,400+ nurses
- All hospitals NABH accredited; major labs NABL certified
- 63 DNB seats awarded by Government of India

**Revenue Mix:**
- ~83% Government insurance (CGHS, ECHS, Railways, Paramilitary, Police, State schemes) — of which 93% is Central Government (sovereign-equivalent)
- ~9% Self-pay
- ~8% TPA/private insurance
- Management guiding toward 75% govt / 25% private within 12 months as insurance penetration rises

**Key Competitive Advantages:**
1. **Cluster-based density** — builds critical mass in a state before entering the next, enabling shared HR, equipment, and clinical governance
2. **Full-time doctors only** — no visiting consultants; doctors evaluated purely on patient satisfaction and clinical outcomes (not volume/revenue), resulting in ~18.9% consultant attrition (industry lowest per management)
3. **250-bed hospital model** — purpose-built for affordability and quick operational break-even; 40% general ward / 30% ICU / 20-22% twin-sharing beds
4. **Brownfield distress acquisition playbook** — buys severely distressed assets at deep discounts, standardizes operations; Mohali acquisition delivered a 36x revenue jump in 30 months
5. **Robotics at scale** — 3 da Vinci 5th-generation robots (cardiac, joint replacement, organ transplants, neuro-intervention), improving ARPOB and EBITDA margins while maintaining affordability ethos
6. **Disallowance management** — 8-9% disallowance rate vs industry 12-15%, driven by a dedicated super-specialist clinical team

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

**1. CGHS Rate Hike — Margin Tailwind (FY27+)**
The Central Government revised CGHS rates by 12-15% — the largest hike in recent memory. Park has ~80% of its government revenue from Central Government schemes, making it the maximum beneficiary in the industry. Management is conservatively accounting for only 7.5% impact in FY27 guidance. Full impact (all cascading schemes: ECHS, Railways, NHAI, Paramilitary, State governments, Police) expected to fully percolate by H2 FY27. *(per Q3FY26 concall)*

**2. Payer Mix Normalization — Margin Expansion**
Shift from 83% govt to target 75% govt / 25% private within a year. Private insurance pays faster and at higher rates, reducing working capital pressure and improving ARPOB. Insurance penetration rising in their core demographic is a structural tailwind.

**3. Robotics-Driven Product Mix Upgrade**
3 da Vinci 5th-gen robots → conducting 25-30 kidney transplants/month, 300+ robotic joint replacements, robotic cardiac surgeries. ARPOB has risen INR25,500 → INR27,500, ALOS declining. Robotics improving product mix *without* compromising affordability positioning. *(per Q3FY26 concall)*

**4. UP State Cluster — Zero-to-Scale**
Entering UP from essentially zero; 1,060 beds by FY28 in the country's most populous state with large rural-urban healthcare gap. Cluster dynamics mean once 2-3 hospitals operate in a region, operating leverage kicks in sharply.

**5. Operational Leverage on New Hospitals**
Track record: new acquisitions break even at 30% occupancy → 20% EBITDA margin by Year 2 → 26-27% EBITDA margin by Year 3. Agra and Panchkula launching FY26 = strong margin contribution arriving FY28 exactly when Gorakhpur/Rohtak add volume.

**6. Debt-Free Balance Sheet**
Full repayment of ~INR425 Cr debt post-IPO → significant PAT growth from interest cost elimination, and balance sheet capacity to fund next phase of acquisitions without equity dilution.

## 5. Red Flags & Risk Analysis

| # | Risk | Severity | Detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Government payer concentration** | 7/10 | 83% revenue from government schemes; any policy reversal, rate cut, or payment stoppage would materially hurt. CGHS rate hike is positive now, but dependence creates structural vulnerability. |
| 2 | **Receivable cycle / working capital** | 6/10 | Government payment TAT currently 4-4.5 months (bill → pass → release). Cash-flow intense when scaling rapidly. Management targets 4 months by FY26-end and 3.5 months longer-term. New hospital launches will stress working capital further. *(per Q3FY26 concall)* |
| 3 | **Agra integration execution risk** | 5/10 | Paid INR245 Cr for a hospital at 30% occupancy / 180 of 360 beds. Higher capex/bed (INR68 lakhs) vs typical INR34 lakh. Success depends on replicating Mohali playbook — which worked but is not guaranteed. |
| 4 | **Simultaneous multi-unit commissioning** | 5/10 | Commissioning Agra (360 beds, Feb 2026) and Panchkula (300 beds, Mar 2026) in same month means 660 beds coming online within 6 weeks. Staffing, credentialing, and ramp-up complexity is high. Doctor salary jumped 8% QoQ (Q3 FY26) due to pre-hiring — margin drag continues into Q4 FY26. *(per Q3FY26 concall)* |
| 5 | **Brownfield deal pipeline dependency** | 5/10 | Growth model requires a steady flow of distressed hospital assets at the right price and location. Deal quality and availability is not fully within management control. |
| 6 | **Q3 margin compression** | 4/10 | Q3 EBITDA margin was 24% vs 26% for 9M — partly seasonal, partly pre-hiring costs. If new hospitals ramp slower than expected, blended margins could stay compressed into FY27. |
| 7 | **Maiden listed company / IR maturity** | 3/10 | This was the first-ever earnings call. Limited sell-side coverage, limited track record of guidance delivery as a listed entity. Surprises (positive or negative) will have outsized stock price reactions. |

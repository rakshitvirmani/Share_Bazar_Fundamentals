## 1. Business Model Overview

Adisoft was incorporated in Pune in 2013 and is run by founder-CMD **Ajay C. Prabhu**, an electronics engineer with 28 years in automation. The company says it converts manual, paper-tracked shop floors into data-driven, error-proofed production systems. A typical example: in an auto assembly line, its systems check that the right part is picked, oriented correctly and torqued to spec. If not, they alert the operator or stop the line *(concall)*.

**Revenue mix (FY26):**

| Segment | Share of revenue | Nature | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Automation Solutions** | ~71% *(IP)* / ~78% *(implied by concall)* | Project-based turnkey work: design, build, install, commission, then AMC/retrofit | Higher, and rises on repeat or redeployed designs |
| **Trading of Automation Products** | ~29% *(IP)* / **~22%** *(concall)* | Box-selling of PLC, HMI, servo, drives and digital-picking components, mainly from Mitsubishi Electric | **~10% "on paper"** *(concall)* |

Management expects the split to stay around **70:30 to 75:25** *(concall)*. Trading gives market intelligence and leads that cross-sell into solutions.

**Product and solution portfolio *(IP)*:** automated assembly lines · robotic and cobot work cells · AI vision inspection · poka-yoke and torque control · material handling and smart conveyors · special purpose machines (SPMs) · tracking and traceability (RFID) · IoT, utility monitoring and Andon/production-control systems. The fastest-growing lines are **production control, traceability, tracking, poka-yoke, digital picking, robotics and vision** *(concall)*. A new shop typically costs a customer **₹2.5–10 Cr** *(concall)*.

**Claimed moat:**
- **Works across every shop.** Most peers serve a single shop (press, weld, paint, engine or assembly). Adisoft's IT–shop-floor layer applies to all of them: "*Can I do my peer's work? Definitely. Can my peer do the job we do?*" *(CMD, concall)*.
- **Mission-critical trust.** These systems run entire shops, and a failure causes recalls or warranty losses. Management claims **90–95% share of the target customer's spend** in its niche *(concall)*.
- **In-house design and IP.** A 49-member design team (E-PLAN, SolidWorks, AutoCAD) and a reusable solution library. Margins improve with each redeployment *(IP)*.
- **Partnerships.** All-India No. 1 Mitsubishi Electric India channel partner for the third year running, with five MEI awards in Aug-2026 *(PR)*. Also holds a 24% stake in **AIOI Systems India**, a tripartite JV with AIOI Systems Co., Japan (digital picking), consolidated as an associate *(IP, concall)*.

**Customer, industry and geography:**
- **Top customer is ~65% of FY26 revenue.** It is described as the "market leader" in both four-wheelers and two-wheelers, a relationship dating back to the company's founding *(concall)*. The company does not name it. The revenue footprint (Haryana 42%, Gujarat 23%) fits a Haryana/Gujarat-based OEM group, but that is our inference and is not disclosed.
- **Automotive plus auto OEMs are ~80% of revenue** *(concall)*. The IP's industry chart shows core "Automotive" falling from 78.6% (FY23) to 64.0% (FY26), with auto OEMs making up most of the rest. Management targets 60–65% auto over time *(concall)*.
- **Non-auto focus areas:** pharma (batch-reporting and data capture), white goods (TV/fridge/AC line QC), e-commerce and warehousing, and packaging/printing. Management plans to hire industry specialists from these sectors in FY27 *(concall)*.
- **Geography:** Haryana 42%, Maharashtra 25%, Gujarat 23%, UP 3%, others 7% *(IP)*. First exports went to Bangladesh and Bahrain in FY25–H2 FY26, following Indian OEMs' overseas plants *(IP, concall)*.

**Scale:** 950+ customers served, 1,000+ installations, 200+ employees, a 12,000 sq ft assembly unit (MIDC Bhosari), ISO 9001:2015 (June 2026) *(IP, PR)*.

### Key Observations

1. **Growth, margins and returns are excellent.** Revenue CAGR is 30%, gross margin expanded ~5 pp in three years, and ROE has stayed above 27% with almost no fixed assets. Depreciation is only ₹0.2 Cr, so this is an asset-light engineering and integration model.
2. **Cash conversion is the weak spot.** Cumulative FY23–26 CFO is only **23% of PAT**. Receivables grew 6x while revenue grew 2.2x. Management attributes this to H2 billing concentrated in Feb–Mar, with 90% collected within 45 days *(IP footnote, concall)*. On 2-Jun-2026 they said ~₹28–29 Cr was still pending *(concall)*. FY26 CFO was helped by **payables jumping ₹17 Cr**. The September half-year balance sheet will show whether the collection claim holds.
3. **Margin expansion came from mix and repeat orders.** Management credits H2-heavy, big repeat orders on redeployed designs *(concall)*. That is structurally sound, but H2-dependent.
4. **Current capacity is maxed out.** The CMD says ₹166 Cr is the "peak" for the current space and manpower *(concall)*. Growth through FY27 H1 has come without new space.

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

### DNA Shift: from a captive auto-OEM integrator in a small shed to a multi-sector Industry 4.0 platform with 4–5x capacity

1. **Capacity unlock (FY28 onward).** Moving from 12k to 70k sq ft removes the space and manpower ceiling the CMD describes. FY28 guidance is **>30% growth** *(concall)*. A modern integrated facility also helps win larger, multi-shop orders.
2. **Repeat and redeployment economics.** Every reused design lifts margin. Gross margin rose from 28% (FY23) to 33% (FY26). Management targets a **13–14% sustainable PAT margin** *(PR)* and does not expect H1 margin dilution from pre-operative hiring *(concall)*.
3. **Non-auto diversification.** Pharma (production-batch reporting), white goods (line QC data capture) and warehousing/e-commerce (digital picking, which links to the AIOI JV). The entry strategy is to lead with data capability and then expand within the customer *(concall)*. Non-auto is ~11–20% of revenue today. The target is to cut auto to 60–65%.
4. **Higher-value technology.** Robotics, AI vision, IIoT and traceability show the strongest demand *(concall)*. These are less commoditised than conveyors or SPMs.
5. **International.** Following Indian OEMs to their overseas plants (Bangladesh, Bahrain so far). Independent bidding abroad is planned *(concall)*.
6. **Balance sheet.** The IPO funds the facility, so FY27 should end debt-free *(PR)*. Interest on idle IPO cash adds to other income in the interim.
7. **Industry tailwind.** India's industrial automation market is projected at **US$29.4 Bn by FY29 (14.26% CAGR)**. India installed **9,120 robots in 2024** (6th globally), but has only **~5–7 robots per 10,000 workers** *(IP, citing Invest India / IFR)*. PLI schemes and auto capacity additions support demand.
8. **Re-rating from a low base.** At ~13x trailing and ~10x FY27E base-case P/E, Adisoft trades below its listed peers Patil Automation (23.6x) and Affordable Robotic (30.7x) *(IP)*. Adisoft also has a higher PAT margin (13.5% vs 10.1% / 5.9%) and higher ROE.

## 5. Red Flags & Risk Analysis

| # | Risk | Severity (1–10) | Detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Single-customer concentration** | **8/10** | ~65% of FY26 revenue comes from one auto OEM group *(concall)*. Management does not see it as a dependence ("it is a pull") and says it will stay "for few years". A capex pause, vendor change or in-sourcing at this one customer would hit both revenue and the H2 repeat-order margin engine. |

Receivables of ₹82.5 Cr equal ~50% of revenue (181 days at year-end), and FY24–25 CFO was negative. Management guides ~120 working-capital days. |
| 3 | **Auto-sector concentration** | **6/10** | ~80% of revenue is auto/auto-OEM *(concall)*. An auto capex downcycle (EV-transition pauses, demand slump) would hurt through both the top customer and peers. Diversification is still early (~11% non-auto by one statement). |
| 4 | **Disclosure quality and consistency** | **6/10** | Trading share is 22% in the concall vs 29% in the IP. The order book has been quoted as ₹70–75 Cr (pre-IPO), ₹40 Cr (Apr), "₹96 Cr" (Jul, really a funnel) and ₹50+ Cr (Oct) without a consistent definition. The H1 FY27 PR gives revenue only, no profit. Segment margins and customer names are not disclosed. Comparisons mix standalone and consolidated figures. |
| 5 | **Short visibility / H2 lumpiness** | **5/10** | The ~3–4 month execution cycle gives only one quarter of visibility. 60–70% of revenue and most profit falls in H2, so one delayed Feb–Mar dispatch (or an early Diwali, per the CMD) can swing the year. |
| 6 | **Facility execution and fixed-cost step-up** | **5/10** | A ~6x jump in space with heavy hiring. Timeline has already slipped Oct → Nov → end-Q3. **Overall risk profile: Medium-High.** The operating record is strong. The main concerns are concentration (one customer, one sector), cash conversion, and the transparency typical of the SME platform.

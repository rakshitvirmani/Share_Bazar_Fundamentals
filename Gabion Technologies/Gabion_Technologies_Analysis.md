## 1. Business Model Overview

**What the company does:** Gabion is an integrated geotechnical solutions company, about 18 years old. It protects infrastructure against slope failure above highways, rockfall in railway cuttings, river erosion of embankments and flooding ("river training"). It does three things under a single contract that clients would otherwise buy from three parties *(per Nov 2026 concall)*:
1. **Design:** in-house geotechnical design team. Designs have been vetted by IIT Guwahati and IIT Bhubaneswar, e.g. slope protection at Rangapahar military station in Nagaland.
2. **Manufacture:** gabions (PVC-coated GI wire mesh boxes), rockfall/slope drapery nettings, geogrids (one type made in-house), 3D polymer erosion-control mats (**sole Indian manufacturer**, *per May 2026 concall*) and geosynthetics. Geotextiles are bought in.
3. **Install:** own execution teams and a fleet of 170+ machines.

**Revenue Mix**

| Stream | FY26 | H1 FY27 | Margin Profile | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Solutions / Services** (design + supply + install; billed after installation) | ~₹54 Cr (~50%) | ~50% | EBITDA ~20%. PAT ~5 ppt higher than products | Driver of FY26 growth. Management expects it to exceed 50% from FY28 |
| **Product sales** (external supply to EPCs/contractors) | ~₹53 Cr (~46%) | ~50% | EBITDA ~10% | Sold directly to customers by regional engineer sales teams (no distributors) |

*Per May 2026 concall: the service margin is "approximately 10% higher" than manufacturing, so about 20% vs 10% EBITDA. Per Nov 2026 concall: about 5 ppt difference at the PAT level.*

FY26 manufacturing output was about 8,000–9,100 t, worth roughly ₹75 Cr of product value. About ₹25 Cr of that was used captively in Gabion's own projects *(per May and Nov 2026 concalls)*.

**Customers & Order Sources**
- **79% private / EPC clients** that list Gabion as an approved make or specialist sub-contractor on NH and railway packages: Dilip Buildcon, H.G. Infra, Rajpath, L&T, Afcons, NCC, D.H. Desai, Adani (for overseas projects). **21% direct government** (e.g. BRO's Shivalik landslide treatment on NH-07 in Uttarakhand) *(per Nov 2026 concall)*.
- **Mining / industrial:** ash-pond and slope projects for Vedanta and JSW. Standalone paid design orders from Hindalco, NCC and D.H. Desai.
- The order book is spread over **11 states**, with none above 20%. The largest are Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, Uttarakhand, Bihar, Himachal Pradesh and Chhattisgarh.

**Competitive Position**
- Management claims a **20–25% share** of the Indian gabion market *(per May 2026 concall)*.
- Competitors: Secure Matrix (Maharashtra), "Techfire" (likely Techfab), smaller players who undercut on supply, and SRM Contractors (listed, owns Maccaferri's Indian manufacturing). **SRM is both a major customer and a competitor.** Gabion supplies SRM because SRM's own manufacturing is "not going too well" *(per May 2026 concall)*.
- Gabion wins against small suppliers on quality and against large diversified players on design and execution.
- Wire-mesh gabions are **approved for the Mumbai–Ahmedabad high-speed rail (bullet train)** project. Gabions are certified to Bhutan Standards.

**Footprint:** Plants at Paonta Sahib (Himachal Pradesh) and near Guwahati (Assam), plus a plant in Nepal. Subsidiaries in Nepal and Bangladesh. Operations claimed in 29 states.

**Industry Backdrop** *(per Nov 2026 concall)*: About 12.6% of India's land area is landslide-prone. Slope protection is moving from post-failure repair to a line item in original project design. The FY27 Union Budget sets capex at ₹12.22 lakh Cr, including ₹3.10 lakh Cr for roads/highways, and raises the Power Ministry allocation 39%. About ₹77,000 Cr of NE railway projects are underway. The Indian gabion market is projected to grow 11%+ a year through FY32.

**Key Observations:**
1. **Growth has broken out after a flat FY24–FY26** (~₹100 → ₹101 → ₹116 Cr). Management blames under-built organisation, machinery and engineers, which it says are now fixed. H1 FY27 tonnage was **+80% YoY**, and revenue growth came from the Raipur–Visakhapatnam and Bengaluru–Vijayawada economic corridors and the BRO NH-07 work.
2. **Margins have not expanded.** H1 FY27 EBITDA was 14.4% against 14.2% in H1 FY26, despite 76% revenue growth. In May, management had guided +1–2 ppt for FY27. Employee cost fell from ~14% to ~10% of sales, so the gain from operating leverage was absorbed elsewhere, probably by the product-heavy mix (50:50) and raw materials (steel and polymer prices doubled during the Iran war).
3. **Working capital consumes the growth.** Net working capital is about ₹65 Cr+ on ~₹116 Cr revenue (~180+ days, *per analyst in May 2026 concall*). In May, management said "working capital will not increase despite sales almost doubling". By September, inventory was up ₹11 Cr and H1 OCF was negative. **Profit is not yet converting to cash.**
4. **Finance cost is falling** (₹2.52 Cr to ₹2.09 Cr in H1) after the IPO, but debt will rise again in H2.

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

**A. From supplier to design-led solutions company.** The solutions share of revenue rose from about 34–35% (FY24) to ~50% (FY26–FY27) and is guided above 50% from FY28. Paid standalone design orders (Hindalco, NCC, D.H. Desai) show the engineering is valued in its own right. Design wins tend to turn into supply and installation contracts. Every completion certificate qualifies Gabion for larger tenders.

**B. Post-IPO bid capacity → direct government tenders.** Higher net worth lifts tender eligibility. A dedicated bid desk has been set up. Bids of about ₹200 Cr have been submitted. FY27 order expectations are **₹150 Cr** of new work orders (₹40–50 Cr government, ~₹100 Cr private) plus ₹30–40 Cr of regular supply orders. Direct government work (21% now) is "the largest headroom" *(per Nov 2026 concall)*.

**C. Mining & industrial vertical (ash ponds).** Ash-pond systems are mandated by norms for power-plant ash disposal. Projects are live with **Vedanta and JSW**. Management claims a **₹200–300 Cr opportunity over 2–3 years** *(per Nov 2026 concall)*. This is aspirational; the vertical is only just starting.

**D. Capacity doubling plus the Assam unit.** Manufacturing was capacity-constrained ("overflowing with orders", *May 2026*). 18,000 t from Q4 FY27 removes that limit. The Assam unit gives a freight and lead-time advantage in the Northeast, where slope work is concentrated.

**E. Certifications → bullet train, exports.** CE/ETA certification for rockfall barriers is expected for the first product in FY27 and for all products by mid-FY28. That unlocks marquee projects (bullet train, metros) and exports to Europe, the Middle East, Russia and Africa. Orders for nettings in Africa and the Middle East are "almost finalising", and Indian EPCs (L&T, Adani) are already taking Gabion products to their overseas sites. Early-warning systems are being added.

**F. Policy tailwind.** Landslide and flood mitigation is becoming a built-in specification on national projects. Union Budget infrastructure capex is up 11.5%, and NE rail projects worth ~₹77,000 Cr are underway.

**G. Management's ambition.** "₹200 Cr, ₹300 Cr, ₹500 Cr" and eventually becoming a construction company that takes ₹500–1,000 Cr projects *(per May 2026 concall)*. This is a double-edged signal: big ambition, but also a possible drift into lower-margin general EPC.

## 5. Red Flags & Risk Analysis

| Risk | Severity (1–10) | Description & Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Working Capital / Cash Conversion** | **8** | NWC is about 180 days. Inventory tripled from FY24 to FY26 on flat revenue, then rose another ₹11 Cr in H1 FY27. H1 OCF was −₹5.7 Cr. Unbilled WIP (~₹15 Cr) and retention money (~₹6 Cr) depend on client measurement and certification. MSME 45-day payment rules are honoured only by top clients like Vedanta. **Mitigation:** a larger collections team; management targets about 10% WC improvement by year-end. **Watchpoint:** FY27 OCF must be positive. |
| **Guidance Credibility** | **7** | FY26 growth was 14% against IPO guidance of 20–25%. The capacity date slipped about 4–5 months. Capacity revenue potential was cut from ₹200 Cr to ₹150 Cr. Margin guidance was not met in H1. The working-capital promise was broken within one quarter. FY28 guidance was nonetheless *raised* to ₹300–350 Cr. **Watchpoint:** H2 FY27 revenue of ₹110 Cr+ is needed for the guided ₹180 Cr. |
| **Order Book Disclosure Inconsistency** | **6** | May 2026: "~₹200 Cr order book, 90% to be executed in FY27". Nov 2026: "above ₹120 Cr", after ₹53 Cr of H1 wins and ₹71.5 Cr of H1 revenue (₹200 + ₹53 − ₹72 ≈ ₹181 Cr expected). The definitions apparently differ: May included product/supply orders, Nov counts work contracts (₹110.5 Cr unbilled out of ₹179.9 Cr in 35 live contracts). **Watchpoint:** consistent order-book definition and disclosure. |
| **SME Platform: Liquidity & Governance** | **6** | BSE SME listing: large lot sizes, thin liquidity, wide spreads, half-yearly reporting only and lighter disclosure. Tax is booked only at year end, which distorts half-year comparisons. Analyst errors on the Nov call (that H1 EBITDA margin was 22%, revenue growth 26%) went uncorrected by management. **Mitigation:** possible migration to the main board once eligible. |
| **Leverage** | **5** | Net D/E is 0.86x and rising (debt +₹10–15 Cr in H2). The <1x cap leaves little room if growth needs more working capital. Finance cost is ~₹4.5 Cr a year, about 25% of EBITDA. |
| **Raw Material Volatility (steel/GI wire, PVC)** | **5** | Polymer prices doubled during the Iran war. Hedging is limited to about 3 months of forward supplier bookings. Project orders run 6–9 months. Product prices can be renegotiated under force majeure. **Mitigation:** PVC and GI-wire backward integration (FY28+). |
| **Margin Stagnation** | **5** | EBITDA has been ~14–16% for FY25–H1 FY27 despite scale. Product sales (50% of mix, ~10% EBITDA) dilute margins. Guided +1–2 ppt a year is unproven. |
| **Client Concentration / EPC Dependence** | **5** | 79% of orders come from private EPCs (Dilip Buildcon, H.G. Infra, etc.) whose own payment cycles depend on NHAI/MoRTH. A slowdown in highway awards would hit both orders and collections. SRM Contractors is both customer and competitor. |
| **Acquisition / Dilution Risk** | **4** | GI-wire backward integration via manufacturers "who want to become part of our company" suggests a share-swap or related acquisition. Ambition to become a ₹500–1,000 Cr EPC player risks diluting focus. |
| **Seasonality** | **3** | The monsoon (Jun–Sep) suppresses H1. H2 is the peak. Single-half disclosure magnifies swings. |
| **Execution / Site Risk** | **3** | Hill and NE sites, with penalty exposure for delays. Track record of 18 years and IIT-vetted designs. |

**Summary Risk Profile:** High-growth, high-risk SME. Demand and positioning are good. The key risks are **cash conversion, management credibility and SME governance/liquidity**, not demand.

## 7. Investment Verdict & Key Metrics to Monitor

### Verdict: **BUY (small/starter position)**. CMP ₹124.6, Weighted FV ₹176, +41% expected return

**Bull Case (20%): ₹336.** Gabion delivers close to guidance: ₹185 Cr in FY27 and ₹290 Cr in FY28. The design-led solutions mix rises above 50%. PVC integration and scale push EBITDA to 16%+. CE certification opens exports and marquee rail projects. PAT roughly triples to ₹28 Cr by FY28. What needs to go right: on-time capacity, direct government tender wins, and working capital under control.

**Base Case (55%): ₹168.** Revenue grows about 40% a year through FY28 (₹165 Cr, then ₹225 Cr), roughly 25–35% below management's numbers, in line with the FY26 miss and the H1 FY27 slippages. Margins improve by about 50 bps a year. PAT goes from ₹13 Cr to ₹19 Cr. Even this haircut case is worth ~35% more than today's price.

**Bear Case (25%): ₹64.** H2 FY27 disappoints. Working capital absorbs all profits, debt goes above 1x, and margins stall at 13%. The SME discount widens and the stock falls below its ₹81 IPO price.

**Bottom line:** The market is pricing Gabion at about 8.5x FY28E base EPS, well below the growth on offer, because management's numbers have repeatedly been ahead of delivery and cash flow is negative. The structural story is real: landslide and flood mitigation becoming a design standard, integrated design-manufacture-install, and capacity doubling. **H2 FY27 is the test.** Revenue of ₹90 Cr+ with positive OCF would validate the base case and justify adding.

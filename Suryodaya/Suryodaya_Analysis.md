**Tagline:** "A Bank of Smiles" — focused on inclusive finance and underserved retail customers

## 1. Business Model Overview

Suryodaya Small Finance Bank (SSFB) is a regulated small finance bank licensed by RBI, operating across three core lending segments:

### **Inclusive Finance Segment**
- **Vikas Loans** (individual unsecured lending): The bank's strategic pivot away from Joint Liability Group (JLG) model towards direct individual lending
  - Growing at 111% YoY (Q2 FY26), now ~72% of inclusive finance portfolio
  - New-to-bank Vikas loans adding to portfolio stability and yield improvement
  - Requires strict underwriting but delivers better granularity and credit pricing power
- **Target customer:** Individuals earning ₹100K–₹200K pa in semi-urban/rural markets
- **Product positioning:** Low credit limits (starting ₹10K–₹25K) with behavior-based graduation

### **Retail Assets (Secured Lending)**
- **Commercial Vehicles (CV):** Financing two/three-wheeler and small commercial vehicle operators
  - ₹1,564 Cr book as of Sept 2025 (49% YoY growth)
  - Focus on franchise model with micro-market penetration
  - PAR 30+ declining but still faces some stress; management confident on recovery
- **Mortgages:** Primarily micro-mortgages to self-employed/small business owners
  - ₹2,778 Cr book (39% YoY growth)
  - Cash flow–based underwriting, micro-markets strategy
  - Geographic diversification across select metros and Tier-II cities
- **Wheels (two-wheeler financing):** Growth segment within retail assets

### **Digital & MSME Lending**
- **Credit on UPI (Payм partnership):** Early-stage, 2.2M+ customer onboarding, attempting scale at low CAC
- **Digital MSME loans:** Secured credit cards, digital deposits partnerships
- **Digital Deposits:** ₹1,000+ Cr monthly run rate sourced through digital channels (Stable Money, Fino partnerships)
  - Nearly 30% of incremental deposit accretion
  - Enables mass-affluent acquisition at low cost

### **Deposit Franchise**
- Retail deposits dominate (82% as of Q1 FY26, 86% as of Q2 FY26)
- Digital channel increasingly important for scale and deposit stickiness
- CASA ratio at ~17.7%–21.2% — management targeting 20%+ range

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

### **Strategic Shift 1: JLG → Individual Lending**
- **Historical model** relied on group-based lending with lower credit cost and faster deployment
- **Pivot rationale:** Individual lending allows better credit pricing, customer stickiness, and granularity; reduces concentration risk
- **Impact:** Vikas loans now 72% of inclusive finance; expected to stabilize mix at 70%–80% over time
- **Driver:** Industry-wide normalization; improved collection technology and digital scoring

### **Strategic Shift 2: Unsecured → Secured Lending (Retail Assets)**
- **Retail assets now >50% of portfolio** (CV + mortgages + wheels)
- **Secured assets provide:** Lower credit cost, better recovery rates (~90% on mortgage retail), collateral cushion
- **Growth vectors:** CV and mortgage books each 30%+ YoY; management confidence in sub-2% credit cost achievable

### **Strategic Shift 3: Digital as Acquisition Engine**
- **Digital deposits:** Growing at ~₹3 Cr/day; enables mass-affluent customer entry at 35–50 bps CAC vs. 200 bps traditional
- **UPI credit:** Partnership with Payм shows scale potential; 2.2M customers onboarded in early stage
- **Implication:** Deposit franchise diversifies away from high-cost retail; supports lower COF trajectory

### **Strategic Shift 4: CGFMU Credit Guarantee Coverage**
- **100% coverage of eligible MFI portfolios** under Credit Guarantee Fund for Micro Units (CGFMU) scheme
- **Protection:** Capped 23% coverage in case of defaults; reduces stress during downturns
- **Claim timing:** Q2 claim of ₹313 Cr received; next claims expected in Q1–Q2 FY27
- **Impact on capital/profitability:** Recoveries from CGFMU offset slippage impact; allows management to report cleaner trajectory

### **DNA Element: "Bank of Smiles"**
- **Positioning:** Inclusive finance + digital convenience + retail franchise reach
- **Target:** 1% of Indian households (~1–1.5% currently penetrated per management)
- **Long-term aim:** Become primary banking relationship for self-employed, semi-urban salaried workers

## 7. Key Metrics to Monitor

### **Quarterly Metrics (Leading Indicators)**

1. **Asset Quality Trend**
   - **GNPA & NNPA ratios:** Track path to <4% GNPA, <2% NNPA by end of FY26
   - **Slippage flow:** Monitor if slippages falling toward ₹25–₹50 Cr/month (vs. ₹60–₹100 Cr current)
   - **Collection efficiency by segment:** IF should lead (99%+ target); retail secured assets (mortgages 90%+, CV 98%+)
   - **30–90 bucket recovery rate:** Indicator of portfolio stress reversal

2. **Growth Metrics**
   - **Advances mix:** % Individual (Vikas) vs. JLG vs. Retail Secured; target 70%–80% individual by FY27
   - **Deposit growth:** YoY % and digital sourcing contribution (target ₹1,000+ Cr/month inflow)
   - **New customer acquisition:** Digital channels adding 1M+ customers/quarter
   - **Secured asset originations:** CV and mortgage monthly volumes

3. **Profitability & Efficiency**
   - **Cost-to-Income ratio:** Watch for decline from 73.6% toward 65% by Q4 FY26
   - **NIMs (Net Interest Margin):** Track yield compression vs. deposit cost normalization
   - **ROE/ROA progression:** Should improve to 1.5–2% ROA by Q4 FY26 (vs. 0.6% annualized 9M)
   - **Cost of funds:** Monitor if moving below 7.5% once digital deposits scale

4. **Capital & Liquidity**
   - **Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR):** Monitor if remaining above 20% amid 20%+ advance growth
   - **Excess liquidity deployment:** Watch if ₹1,000 Cr buffer being deployed into advances or kept for provisions
   - **Equity capital plan:** Management has enabling provision to raise up to ₹1,000 Cr but no immediate intent

5. **Digital / Product Metrics**
   - **UPI Credit (Payм) users & active base:** Track if reaching 5M+ users and meaningful revenue
   - **Digital deposit run rate:** Should sustain ₹1,000–₹1,500 Cr/month
   - **Secured credit card uptake:** New product, monitor adoption and NIMs
   - **CASA ratio:** Target 20%+ by Q4 FY26; currently at 21.2%

### **Red Flags to Watch**
- **Slippages re-accelerating** above ₹100 Cr/month → asset quality not stabilizing
- **Deposit growth falling below 15% YoY** → funding squeeze
- **Cost-to-income staying above 72%** → operational leverage not kicking in
- **ROE remaining below 2% through FY27** → value destruction signal
- **GNPA trending back above 7%** → portfolio stress not over
- **Retail asset (CV/mortgage) PAR 30+ exceeding 5%** → underwriting quality concern

## Summary Thesis

Suryodaya is a **recovery story with structural growth optionality**. The bank has navigated a significant asset quality trough (GNPA 8.5% → 6.6%) and is now positioned for:

1. **NPA resolution:** CGFMU claims will resolve old-vintage stress; new business cleaner
2. **Mix shift:** Individual lending (Vikas) and secured retail assets (CV, mortgages) improving portfolio stability and ROE
3. **Digital flywheel:** Digital deposits growing at scale with low CAC; enabling deposit cost reduction and funding growth
4. **Operating leverage:** Advances growing 20%+ while cost discipline improves; cost-to-income should decline materially by FY27

**Near-term watch:** Q4 FY26 result validation of recovery narrative (GNPA <6%, slippages trending to ₹50 Cr/month, cost-to-income <70%)

**Valuation signal:** Profitability recovery path credible but not yet in market consensus; near-term volatility likely if slippages re-accelerate or digital sourcing disappoints. Conservative entry points on book value or earnings multiples likely offer better risk/reward than current levels.

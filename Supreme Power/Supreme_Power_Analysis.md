## 1. Business Model Overview

Supreme Power Equipment (SPEL) is a Chennai-based transformer maker. It began in 1994 as a partnership and became a company in 2005 (*H1FY26 call, Nov 2025*). It lists on NSE Emerge (SME) and plans to move to the main board once it becomes eligible after December 2026 (*Q1FY27 call, Aug 2026*). The Chairman & MD, Mr. Vee Rajmohan, is the only management voice on every call.

**Product segments (order book mix trend):**

| Segment | Nov 2025 OB (₹235 Cr) | Jun 2026 OB (₹588 Cr) | Aug 2026 OB (₹590 Cr) | Oct 2026 OB (₹566 Cr) |
|---------|------|------|------|------|
| Power transformers (PT, 20-200 MVA, up to 220 kV) | ₹105 Cr (45%) | ~72-73% | ₹453.83 Cr (76.9%) | ~₹440 Cr (75-78%) |
| Distribution transformers (DT) | ₹53 Cr (22%) | ~20% | ₹105.27 Cr (17.8%) | <₹95 Cr (16-17%) |
| Inverter-duty transformers (IDT, solar) | ₹76 Cr (33%) | ~6-8% | ₹30.96 Cr (5.3%) | ~₹30 Cr (5%) |

*Sources: H1FY26 call, Nov 2025; FY26 call, Jun 2026; Q1FY27 call, Aug 2026; H1FY27 call, Oct 2026*

- **The business has changed character:** In one year the company went from a DT/solar-IDT maker (with a large NLC inverter-duty order) to a power-transformer business. About 75% of the order book is now PT. In H1FY27 revenue, PT was about 60% and DT plus IDT made up the rest (*H1FY27 call, Oct 2026*).
- **Realisation:** About ₹20 lakh/MVA for DT and about ₹10 lakh/MVA for PT (*H1FY27 call, Oct 2026*).
- **Customers:** Roughly 27-34% of the order book is government (TNPDCL/TANGEDCO, TANTRANSCO, KSEB, KPTCL, NLC) and 66-73% is non-government (Karnataka/Telangana EPCs, solar developers, steel and paper plants such as Seshasayee Paper). Management wants government exposure below 50% (*H1FY26 call; Q3FY26 call; H1FY27 call*).
- **Geography:** Mostly South India. Expected mix is Tamil Nadu ~40%, Karnataka 30-40%, Kerala ~10% (*Q3FY26 call, Feb 2026*). Telangana and AP were added in FY26 and Maharashtra in FY27 (*Q1FY27 call, Aug 2026*). There are no direct exports. A Mexico order was cancelled and UK talks are under way (*H1FY26 call, Nov 2025*).
- **Subsidiary:** Danya Electric Company handles distribution transformers and acts as a backward-integration arm (*Q1FY27 call, Aug 2026*).
- **Payment terms:** Private customers pay 10-30% advance and up to 90% before dispatch. Repeat customers get 45-60 days of credit. Government pays in 60-90 days (*H1FY27 call, Oct 2026*).

**What the numbers say:**
- **Revenue is clearly accelerating** now that the Kannur plant has started commercial production (Feb 2026). Q2FY27 revenue of about ₹87 Cr is roughly 2.2× Q2FY26 and the highest quarter on record.
- **Margins have come down one step at a time.** PAT margin went from 12.4% (FY25) to 11.2% (FY26) to 10.1% (H1FY27). EBITDA margin in Q2FY27 (16.9%) is below the 18-20% management guided in August. - **Working capital worsened in H1FY27.** Inventory and receivables "shot up sharply". Management expects inventory to stay at ₹60-70 Cr and CFO to turn positive in H2FY27 (*H1FY27 call, Oct 2026*). This came after an improvement in FY26: receivable days fell from ~210 to 94 (*FY26 call, Jun 2026*).

This probably reflects EBITDA excluding other income, or revenue from operations as the base. **Margins calculated on total income may therefore be flattered.** Balance sheet, debt, CFO, ROCE and ROE were not available in the sources.

*Sources: H1FY26 call; Q3FY26 call; FY26 call; Q1FY27 call; H1FY27 call*

- **Kannur capex was ~₹95-100 Cr** (*Q3FY26 call*). It was funded with ₹30-40 Cr of term loans (₹40 Cr sanctioned, ₹30 Cr drawn), IPO proceeds, internal accruals and ₹21 Cr of warrants (*H1FY26 call, Nov 2025*).
- **Historical under-use:** In FY23-25 the company produced only 760-1,250 MVA against 2,500 MVA of capacity, i.e. 30-50% utilisation (analyst on *H1FY26 call*). Management explained this by its mix of small distribution transformers. In MVA terms, then, Unit 1 being "fully utilised" means it is limited by its product mix, not by MVA capacity.
- **Credentials gate the ramp to larger PT.** Orders for 160 MVA/220 kV and 112 MVA/~330 kV units have been won. A CPRI type test of the 160 MVA/220 kV unit is planned for Dec-26/Jan-27. Separately, 2 × 165 MVA units are on order from KPTCL, a 50 MVA TANGEDCO type test is under way and a 160 MVA TANGEDCO tender has been filed. Management expects PGCIL vendor approval within about a year (*FY26, Q1FY27, H1FY27 calls*).
- **Sanity check on capacity:** 9,000 MVA × ~₹10 lakh/MVA (PT realisation) ≈ ₹900 Cr at 100% utilisation. That is consistent with the ₹550-650 Cr "peak" at a 60-70% effective rate with a mixed product range.

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

**DNA shift: from a ₹150 Cr South-Indian DT/solar supplier to a ₹500 Cr+ power-transformer maker with 220 kV capability.**

1. **Capacity is 3.6×.** Capacity went from 2,500 to 9,000 MVA and the size ceiling from about 60 MVA to 200 MVA/220 kV. This moves SPEL into a part of the market with fewer players and 12-24 month waiting lists at the large OEMs (*H1FY26 call; Q1FY27 call*).
2. **Revenue doubles or more in two years if Kannur reaches ~80%.** Management now says ~₹550 Cr in FY28 (*H1FY27 call*), against ₹182 Cr in FY26.
3. **Credentials:** A successful 160 MVA/220 kV CPRI type test (Dec-26/Jan-27), PGCIL approval and the KPTCL 165 MVA supply history would open state transmission utilities, PGCIL-linked EPCs and eventually data-centre substations (100-200 MVA). Large OEMs' long lead times help SPEL win orders.
4. **Main-board migration:** Eligibility opens after Dec-2026 and management says it will migrate "next year" (*Q1FY27 call*). This would widen the investor base and could narrow the SME valuation discount.
5. **Geographic spread:** Entering Maharashtra, Telangana and AP reduces reliance on TN and Karnataka. Maharashtra and Hyderabad are where most data-centre load is being built (analyst on *Q1FY27 call*).
6. **Backward integration:** The tank plant (₹20-25 Cr) secures a supply-tight component and adds a little margin.
7. **Price-variation clauses:** Management cites 70-90% of the order book as covered (the figure varies by call). This limits commodity risk on long-cycle PT orders.
8. **Sector tailwind:** Management expects strong T&D, renewable and data-centre demand for 8-10 years. SPEL's capacity is a very small share of the industry: under 2% market share even at full use (*H1FY26 call*).

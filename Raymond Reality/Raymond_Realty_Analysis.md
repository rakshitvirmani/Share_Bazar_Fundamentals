## 1. Business Model Overview

Raymond Realty is the real estate arm of the Raymond group, demerged into a standalone listed company in July 2025. It started in ~2019 as an "experiment" on the group's **legacy ~100-acre textile factory land at Pokhran Road 1–2, Thane** — vacated 12–15 years ago — and has since expanded into the wider Mumbai Metropolitan Region through **asset-light Joint Development Agreements (JDAs)**, mostly society redevelopments *(per Q4FY26 concall)*.

**Two engines:**

| Engine | GDV (₹ Cr) | Status | Economics |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Owned Thane land (100 acres)** | ~25,000 | 65 acres under development (6.7 msf RERA carpet, ₹16,500 Cr potential); ₹9,400 Cr sold, ₹7,460 Cr collected; 11 towers / ~4,000 homes delivered | Zero land cost; EBITDA margin **25–26%** |
| **JDA / redevelopment (8 projects)** | ~27,000 | 4 launched (Bandra East, BKC, Wadala, Sion) = 2.8 msf, ₹11,500 Cr potential, ₹2,900 Cr sold, ₹692 Cr collected; 2 Mahim launching FY27; Kandivali + Parel (₹11,500 Cr) signed recently | 10–15% of land value as deposit; ₹300–500 Cr peak capital per project; target EBITDA **≥20%** at maturity, single digits at launch |
| **Total** | **~52,000** | Unsold-but-launched ₹15,700 Cr + unlaunched ₹24,000 Cr | |

*(All per Q1FY27 concall)*

**Brands (three-tier ladder):**
- **TenX** — aspirational 2BHK (Thane ~₹2 Cr+). TenX Habitat (3,100 homes) is fully sold and has its full Occupancy Certificate.
- **Address by GS** — premium 3BHK (₹3 Cr+ in Thane; Bandra East, Wadala, Sion, Mahim).
- **Invictus by GS** — luxury (Thane, BKC; Parel to be Address + Invictus at ₹6–20 Cr ticket sizes) *(per Q2FY26 and Q1FY27 concalls)*.

**Mix shift:** The JDA share of pre-sales went from **22% in FY25 to 54% in FY26 and 64% in Q1FY27**. That hit the FY28 50:50 target a year early *(per Q4FY26 and Q1FY27 concalls)*. Thane is now about a third of sales and has stagnated in absolute terms (see Red Flags).

**Positioning:** Management pitches "build fast, sell fast", a "manufacturing mindset" (no land hoarding), quality, ahead-of-schedule delivery and a customer focus (a Chief Customer Relationship Officer reports to the MD). Revenue is recognised on the **percentage-of-completion method**, so P&L is smoother than at peers that use completed-contract *(per Q4FY26 concall)*. 100% MMR-focused; Pune has been studied for two years, but nothing has met return hurdles *(per Q1FY27 concall)*.

### Key Observations

- **Pre-sales are running ahead of the 20% guidance.** The FY26 target was ~₹2,800 Cr and ~₹2,950 Cr was achieved. Q1FY27's +129% YoY is flattered by a weak, inventory-starved Q1FY26 base, but ₹700 Cr in a seasonally light quarter is still strong.
- **The margin recovery is real but uneven.** 9M FY26 was ~13%, then Q4 jumped to 21.5% on the high-margin Park Street retail launch and maturing projects, and Q1FY27 was back at 13% as four March launches dilute margins. Management explains three causes:
  - Common costs now allocated to the business post-demerger, where previously they sat unallocated as a segment.
  - An Ind AS treatment that books JDA rehab-area value as revenue at a presumptive 5% margin.
  - The "hockey stick" margin curve at launch.
- **Interest is now the swing factor for PAT.** Q1FY27 EBITDA rose 70% but PAT fell 19%, because finance cost rose from ~₹15 Cr to ₹47 Cr. Gross debt went from ₹380 Cr to ₹1,095 Cr YoY *(per Q1FY27 concall)*.
- **Cash flow is negative by design.** Thane releases ₹450–500 Cr/yr and early JDAs ₹100–150 Cr, but approval costs and JDA deposits exceed that. Management explicitly guides **"cash negative on an overall basis for the next two years"** *(per Q4FY26 concall)*.
- **FY25 comparisons are not like-for-like.** Pre-demerger EBITDA (~₹500 Cr) was a segmental figure that excluded unallocated costs *(per Q3FY26 concall)*. The reported ~1,600% FY26 PAT growth is a base effect of the demerger, not operating growth.

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

1. **From land monetiser to asset-light MMR developer.** In under three years, a one-location legacy land play has become a multi-micro-market brand (Bandra, BKC, Wadala, Sion, Mahim, Kandivali, Parel). JDA GDV (₹27,000 Cr) now exceeds owned-land GDV (₹25,000 Cr). This is the core re-rating argument: the business can grow without buying land.
2. **The JDA margin hockey stick in FY28.** Three of four launched JDAs are only at excavation. As they mature, management expects JDA margins to rise from single digits toward 20%, pulling the blended margin to 18–20% *(per Q1FY27 concall: "by FY28 you will see a higher margin profile on the JDAs")*.
3. **Parel (₹8,500 Cr).** A marquee South Bombay entry at ₹6–20 Cr ticket sizes. If launched well in FY28, it alone is worth ~3x FY26 pre-sales.
4. **Thane commercial development.** An already-earmarked commercial parcel is not in the ₹52,000 Cr GDV. Activating it would be a re-rating event.
5. **Mumbai tailwinds.** CY2025 was a record year with ~1.5 lakh registrations, the highest in 14 years. The repo rate is at 5.25%, buyers are shifting to branded developers, and Maharashtra policy (DCR 2034) is stable *(per Q3FY26 and Q1FY27 concalls)*.
6. **Ownership and coverage repair.** The stock fell below mid-cap scale after the demerger, which forced some institutional selling: FII+DII holding fell from ~22% to ~8%. A dedicated IR head joined in Q2FY27 to run institutional outreach, and a PE/AIF partnership at SPV level is being considered *(per Q1FY27 concall)*. Getting institutional investors back is a valuation catalyst in itself.
7. **Steady ~20% compounding.** A six-year booking CAGR of ~50% and a revenue CAGR of 84% (FY21–26). Management commits to a minimum 20% growth in pre-sales and revenue, and ROCE ≥20% *(per Q4FY26 and Q1FY27 concalls)*.

## 5. Red Flags & Risk Analysis

| # | Risk | Severity | Detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rising leverage and negative operating cash flow** | **7/10** | Net cash of ₹233 Cr (prior period cited on Q2FY26 call) → net debt of ₹824 Cr (Jun-26) within about a year. Gross debt is up ~3x YoY. Management guides cash-negative for two more years. D/E is 0.7x against a self-imposed 1.0x cap, which leaves ~₹400–600 Cr of headroom while Parel, Mahim and Kandivali all need capital. |
| 2 | **Interest is eroding PAT, and the interest guidance looks unreliable** | **7/10** | Q1FY27 finance cost of ₹47 Cr annualises to ~₹190 Cr against the CEO's off-the-cuff "₹100–120 Cr, don't hold me to it". About half of FY26 finance cost was interest on **instalment dues to government** (8–9%). This is a quasi-debt that is probably **excluded from reported net debt**, so true leverage is higher than 0.7x. |
| 3 | **Margin guidance drift** | **6/10** | Guidance went from ~20% (Oct-25) to 17–20% (Jan-26), then 16% actual FY26, then 16–18% and 17–19% for FY27. Each quarter brings a new explanation (demerger costs, Ind AS rehab accounting, launch timing). The FY27 band needs H2 margins of ~19–21% after a 13% Q1. |
| 4 | **JDA counterparty and approval risk** | **6/10** | Mahim took ~2.5 years to reach launch and slipped by about a year against Oct-25 guidance. Redevelopment depends on society consensus, on rehab delivery (partners sometimes do the rehab, with step-in rights) and on approvals. Management itself acknowledges "overheated" redevelopment bidding. |
| 5 | **Geographic concentration and real estate cyclicality** | **6/10** | 100% MMR. Demand rests on rates, sentiment and Mumbai end-users, and management itself calls the business "cyclical" and says "we don't know the timing of when the cycle will turn". Debt is being added late in a three-year upcycle. |
| 6 | **Thane stagnation and no pricing power** | **5/10** | Thane pre-sales went ₹1,950 Cr (FY24) → ₹1,800 Cr (FY25) → ₹1,360 Cr (FY26). Management admits Thane has "very marginal increase in prices over the years" because of competition from Lodha, Adani, Oberoi, Kalpataru and Piramal. All growth must come from JDAs. |
| 7 | **Parel concentration** | **5/10** | ₹8,500 Cr is ~16% of total GDV, in a first-time micro-market (South Bombay), with ~18 months to launch. An execution or pricing miss here shows up in FY29–FY32 numbers. |
| 8 | **Institutional exit and low coverage** | **5/10** | FII+DII holding fell from ~22% to ~8% within a year. An analyst explicitly raised "information asymmetry… management, leadership or corporate governance" concerns on the Q1FY27 call. Investor relations was under-resourced until Q2FY27. |
| 9 | **Revenue quality and accounting** | **4/10** | Under Ind AS, JDA rehab area is booked as revenue at a presumptive 5% margin, which inflates the top line and dilutes reported margins. ₹108 Cr of Sion revenue was recognised **before launch**, from existing residents buying extra area (genuine, but non-recurring in nature). Percentage-of-completion accounting gives management estimate-driven discretion over timing. |
| 10 | **Construction cost inflation** | **3/10** | War-related commodity effects add an estimated 3–4% to costs if prolonged. Management says this can be passed on to buyers *(per Q4FY26 concall)*. |
| 11 | **Group and promoter linkages** | **3/10** | Post-demerger, the business must be self-funding ("no further support" from the corporate). The promoters have invested ~₹1,400 Cr across Raymond businesses historically, but there is no current plan to inject capital *(per Q3FY26 concall)*. |

**Net view:** The operating story (sales, launches, brand) is de-risking. The **financial story (debt, interest, margins) is where the risk sits** for the next four to six quarters.

### What to Track Each Earnings Call

1. **Interest bridge:** management promised to disclose the full-year FY27 finance cost split between bank debt and government dues. Check whether government dues are included in net debt.
2. **Project-level margins:** whether JDA projects (Bandra East first) show margin expansion as construction progresses. This is the core of the FY28 margin thesis.
3. **Launch conversion:** percentage of launched value sold in the first 90 days for Mahim I and II, compared with Sion's slow start (4% in its first week).

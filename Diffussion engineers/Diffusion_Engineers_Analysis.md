## 1. Business Model Overview

Diffusion Engineers (founded 1982, Nagpur) is a **wear-protection and engineering solutions** company serving cement, steel, power, mining, sugar and engineering. Its model is "integrated" from metallurgy to equipment:

| Segment | ~Revenue Mix (*Q4 FY26 concall*) | What it is | Lead time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Welding consumables | ~30% | Special hard-facing / repair & maintenance electrodes and flux-cored wires (only 2–3% conventional) | ≤2–4 weeks; mostly cash & carry |
| Wear plates & wear parts | ~30% | Composite wear plates, ready-to-fit wear parts | 4–8 weeks |
| Heavy engineering | ~30% | Roller-press rolls (HPGR), VRM rollers, steel-mill rollers, fans, separators, air heaters, wagon tipplers, crushers (assemblies up to 500 t) | 3–10 months |
| Industrial services | ~10% | On-site cladding/overlay, hot kiln alignment, breakdown repair (50–60 technicians) | — |

**Business characteristics:**
- **~70–75% of revenue comes from opex**, i.e. maintenance and spares for the installed base. About 25–30% comes from new-project capex (*Q2 FY26, Q2 FY27*).
- **More than 80% of revenue is from repeat customers.**
- **Customer concentration:** the largest customer is under 15% of revenue, and the top 10 are about 60–70% (*Q3 FY26, Q1 FY27*).
- **Roller-press rolls are the breakout product:** about ₹60–70 Cr in FY26 (~+100% YoY), with "little less than ₹100 Cr" expected in FY27 (*Q2 FY27*).
- **Exports** are about 12–13% of revenue, covering 35–53 countries. Subsidiaries in **Philippines and Singapore** (profitable), **Turkey** (now breakeven) and **UAE** (live from Q2 FY27).
- **Associate:** LSN Diffusion (UK), which makes thermal spray powders.
- **Strategic minority stake:** 10% of **Tejorup Sunmay Systems**, a VSHORADS (very short-range air defence) missile developer, valued at about ₹40–45 Cr. The stake comes with manufacturing rights after prototype approval.
- **Defence** is about 1.5–2% of revenue: T-90 armour welding wires and a ₹7.5 Cr flux-cored wire (FCW) order.
- **Railways:** Vande Bharat development LOIs, plus a loco-shell manufacturing qualification received in Q2 FY27.

**Competitors:**
- Consumables and wear plates: Ador Welding (incl. ex-Ador Fontech), ESAB/EWAC Alloys.
- Heavy engineering: ISGEC, ThyssenKrupp, Walchandnagar.
- Global: Welding Alloys, Castolin Eutectic.

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

### 4.1 Doubling heavy-engineering capacity into a strong order book
- The order book is up from ₹100 Cr to ₹227 Cr in 18 months. Customers are now "loading us with more orders" as they see the new shop floor, and some are asking to pull deliveries forward (*Q1/Q2 FY27*).

### 4.2 Shift from consumables to ready-to-fit parts and equipment
- Skilled welding labour is scarce at customer sites, so plants increasingly buy finished wear parts or complete rollers instead of electrodes.
- A complete roller order is many times the value of the electrodes used to repair it.
- Heavy engineering grew from ~₹55 Cr to ~₹125–130 Cr over four years, versus consumables from ₹75 Cr to ₹110 Cr (*analyst data, Q1 FY27*).

### 4.3 Product pipeline beyond roller-press rolls
- **VRM rollers:** the first order has been executed. The installed mill base is much larger than for roller presses.
- **Steel-mill rollers:** supplied to SAIL (Bokaro, Bhilai) and now JSW and JSPL.
- **Shredder parts** for alternative fuel, and new rotor and rotary-air-feeder designs.
- These new products are 1–2% of revenue today. Management expects **10–15% of an enlarged revenue base in 2–3 years** (*Q2 FY27*).

### 4.4 Margin recovery and operating leverage
- Management guides that quarterly margins return to the "original levels" (~14–15%) **by Q4 FY27** as price increases flow through. It targets **15–16% EBITDA** over the medium term from scale, backward integration (strips, wire line) and mix.

### 4.5 International service model
- The Philippines asset-light service model is being copied in UAE and Turkey.
- New wins in Ghana and Nigeria. A large US order was "on hold because of tariffs, expected any time now" (*Q2 FY27*).
- Exports are targeted at about 15% of revenue.

### 4.6 Optionality: defence (Tejorup) and railways
- Tejorup's DRDO project sanction order (PSO) covers **200 launchers and 1,200 rockets** after prototype approval. Diffusion is the intended manufacturing and integration partner. No revenue is expected in FY27 or "nothing significant" in FY28 (*Q4 FY26*).
- Railways: Vande Bharat development orders (LOIs, RITES inspections) and a loco-shell qualification. Revenue is expected in 9–12 months.

## 7. Key Metrics to Monitor

### Investment Verdict

**Weighted Fair Value: ₹427 | CMP ₹448 | Rating: HOLD.** ACCUMULATE between ₹384 and ₹427; BUY below ₹384.

- **Bull (25%, ₹643):** capacity doubling meets the cement and steel capex boom, and margins hit 16%. Roller, VRM, export and rail wins compound, giving ~30% revenue growth into FY28.
- **Base (50%, ₹436):** 20–25% growth with margins recovering to ~14.75%. EPS growth trails revenue in FY28 as depreciation rises and treasury income falls. The stock is fairly valued today.
- **Bear (25%, ₹193):** sticky input inflation plus a capex slowdown keep margins at ~12.5%, receivables balloon and the multiple compresses.

**Bottom line:** this is a good franchise with a niche, integrated wear-protection moat, and revenue momentum has clearly inflected. But the margin story hasn't delivered yet. FY28 earnings face a depreciation and treasury-income air pocket, and the stock already prices the base case. Hold existing positions. Add only below ₹427, or on proof of the Q4 FY27 margin recovery to ≥14.5%.

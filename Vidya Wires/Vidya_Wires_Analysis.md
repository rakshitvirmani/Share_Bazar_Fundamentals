## 1. Business Model Overview

Vidya Wires (incorporated 1981, Anand, Gujarat) makes **winding and conductivity products**: insulated copper and aluminium conductors that go into transformers, motors, generators, switchgear and solar modules. It claims to be the **4th-largest Indian player, with 5.7% installed-capacity share**, and targets #3 and ~11% after expansion (*Q2 FY26 concall*). It sells 8,500+ SKUs, from 0.07 mm to 25 mm.

**Economics:** this is a **pure conversion business**. Copper and aluminium are booked back-to-back against the customer's LME booking, and FX is hedged as well (*Q2 FY26*). Material is ~92–93% of revenue, so:
- **Revenue moves with copper prices.**
- **Profit moves with volume × EBITDA per kg.**
- Gross margin is only ~7–8%, EBITDA ~4–5% and PAT ~3%.

**Backward integration:** in-house oxygen-free copper rod covers 35–40% of rod needs. About 26% of power comes from captive solar and wind.

**Credit rating:** CRISIL A-/Positive.

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

### 4.1 Doubling capacity in a demand-rich market
- Transformer demand is strong: India's generation capacity is set to go from 442 GW to ~900 GW by 2032, with ₹9.16 trn of transmission capex, 500 GW of non-fossil capacity by 2030, and data-centre load on top.
- The old plant ran at ~90% for two years, so it was capacity-constrained, not demand-constrained. If ALCU fills to ~80%, volumes rise from ~18k MT to ~29k MT by FY28, about **+60%**.

### 4.2 Mix upgrade, higher EBITDA per kg
- ALCU products are higher value-add: aluminium (~3.5x more processing per kg than copper), CTC, PV ribbon, multi-paper-covered conductors, copper busbars and foils.
- EBITDA/kg has gone from ~₹38 (FY25) to ~₹47.5 (FY26) to ₹59 (H1 FY27, management figure). Management targets **₹60–65/kg** (*Q2 FY27*). For comparison, peers in similar products reportedly run around ₹66/kg (*Q4 FY26 Q&A*).

### 4.3 CTC: entry into large power transformers
- India's CTC demand is about 30,000–40,000 MT and growing (*Q4 FY26*). Phase 1 adds 3,000 MT, starting with mid-size transformers. HVDC-grade approval would take another 6–9 months after that (*Q2 FY27*).

### 4.4 Exports to 25% of revenue
- Exports are currently ~12% (they were 18–19% in some years). Export margins are higher. ALCU has made its first export shipments, to Israel and Nepal, and PV ribbon has a US customer (*Q2 FY27*).

### 4.5 New end-markets: data centres, EV, rail
- First orders have come from data-centre applications (copper busbars). EV strips are on regular orders.

### 4.6 Operating leverage from fixed costs already in place
- ALCU's full fixed cost is already in the H1 FY27 P&L while its volume is still small. Management says "most of the extra contribution will come to profit" once volumes scale (*Q2 FY27*).

## 7. Key Metrics to Monitor

### Investment Verdict

**Weighted Fair Value: ₹87 | CMP ₹77.2 | Rating: BUY (marginal, starter position).** ACCUMULATE up to ₹87; REDUCE above ₹91.

- **Bull (20%, ₹138):** ALCU fills fast, CTC and aluminium approvals land, EBITDA/kg reaches ₹65 and exports scale. Doubled capacity in a strong transformer cycle drives a ~2.3x PAT jump by FY28.
- **Base (55%, ₹90):** the capacity fills about a year behind the original plan. PAT rises from ₹58 Cr to ~₹107 Cr by FY28, and a 20x multiple gives modest upside from here.
- **Bear (25%, ₹40):** volumes stay near ~20k MT, ALCU fixed costs drag, and margins compress. At 14x on ~₹3.2 EPS the stock roughly halves.

**Bottom line:** the valuation is undemanding (~15x FY28E), the balance sheet is clean after the IPO, and the conversion model is fully hedged. But the investment case rests on one thing, ALCU volume, and management has so far given neither the numbers nor the timelines to back it. Take a small position near ₹75–78 and add only after Q3 FY27 (Feb 2027) shows real volume growth and CTC has started. Cut the position if H2 volumes disappoint again.

## Key Indicators Supporting the Bottom

### 1. Historical Correction Comparison (BSE SME IPO Index)

Every major correction in the SME IPO Index has been followed by massive rallies:

- Dec 2014 to Mar 2016: 41% fall in 455 days, followed by 177% rally in 645 days
- Jan 2018 to Apr 2020: 37% fall in 819 days (including COVID), followed by 1300% rally in about 1 year (13x the index)
- Feb 2022 correction: 42% fall in 154 days (Russia-Ukraine war), followed by 900% rally in 924 days
- Current correction (Jan 2025 onwards): 39% fall in 448 days already. This is comparable to or worse than previous corrections, suggesting we are near the end of the pain

### 2. Crude Oil Correlation

Historically, when crude oil tops out, the SME Index bottoms out almost simultaneously. Example: May-June 2022, crude oil peaked and SME Index hit bottom in the same timeframe. Currently, crude oil has spiked due to Iran tensions, but Harsh expects the Strait of Hormuz situation to normalize quickly because 30-40 countries are diplomatically pressurizing Iran to open it, and Trump needs to end the war badly because US Treasury bond yields are spiking dangerously.

### 3. India VIX (Volatility Index)

Market typically bottoms when VIX touches the 28-32 zone. During the Russia-Ukraine war, VIX hit 32 and the market reversed. Currently, VIX is near the lower band of this zone (28-29) and may touch 31-32 with one more bout of panic (Monday-Tuesday), after which things normalize. When VIX starts coming down from 32 and drops below 26-27, that is a confirmed signal that the rally has begun.

### 4. MSCI India ETF (Blackstone iShares)

This ETF, which tracks 164 Indian companies and represents how FIIs invest in India, is touching its long-term trendline support. Historically, every time this trendline is tested, a strong reversal follows (except during COVID black swan). Only 4% correction is left to reach the trendline, aligning with all the other indicators.

### 5. USD-INR Positive Signal

USD-INR has fallen from 95+ to around 92. If it stabilizes near 90 or below, FII inflows will restart. Level 90 on USD-INR is the trigger for FII buying to turn positive. DIIs are already buying through SIPs, so the liquidity problem will get resolved from both sides.

### 6. SME IPO Index vs Nifty Relative Strength

The ratio chart of BSE SME IPO / Nifty is forming a base after underperforming since August. If this ratio breaks above 3.45, it signals that SME companies will start outperforming large caps again, similar to the March-July 2025 period.

## Valuation Snapshot

### Nifty PE

Nifty PE has bottomed around 19-20 levels historically (March 2023 at 20.4, June 2022 at 18.9, March 2025 at 19.9). A further 5% correction would take PE to 18.9, which is the hard floor. Below 18 only happens during black swan events like COVID (went to 17.2). These are extremely attractive valuations for a 1-year horizon.

### BSE SME IPO Index PE

Currently around 9-10 PE at the index level. This is dirt cheap. Even individual stocks within this space are far more attractively valued than they were at any point in the last bull run.

### Micro Cap 250 Index

PE looks optically high at 24x but that is misleading. The top 10-15 companies (which carry 50%+ weightage) are trading at inflated PEs (50-97 PE). If you strip them out, the remaining 240 companies trade at significantly lower PEs. The more useful metric is Price-to-Book: currently at 2.7-2.8, and bottoming levels historically are 2.2-2.4 (March 2023 bottom was 2.1-2.3). We are right at the bottom zone. P/B historically tops at 3.6-3.8 during bull market peaks. From 2.5 to 4.5 P/B rerating alone, the index doubles.

### Gold vs Equity Rotation

Nifty/Gold Bees ratio trades between 175 and 400-450. Currently near the lower channel, meaning gold is overvalued relative to equity. Historically, gold and Sensex play a catchup game. Gold has run up to 1.49 lakh while Sensex is at 73,000, so expect gold to consolidate for 6-8 months while equities catch up. This is a time to rotate from gold/silver into equity, not the reverse.

## Strategies

### Strategy 1: Deploy Cash Aggressively Near Support Levels

Harsh is deploying his remaining 30% cash into the market. His approach is to watch for a 4-5% dip (likely Monday-Tuesday due to geopolitical uncertainty) and use that to go fully invested. He is so bullish at these levels that he would even take 5-10% leverage if Nifty touches 21,642, provided his emergency fund covers 2-3 years. After deployment, the plan is to hold for 8-10 months minimum to reap rewards.

### Strategy 2: Focus on Relative Strength for Stock Selection

Compare your target stock against the Micro Cap 250 or BSE SME IPO index. When the index recovers by 5%, see how much your stock recovers. If it recovers 15-20% when the index recovers 5%, that stock has strong relative outperformance and is very likely to make a fresh all-time high when the bull rally starts. More than 72% probability that such stocks will make new highs when the index does.

### Strategy 3: Screen for Revenue Doublers and Profit Triplers

The single most powerful filter: find stocks that can double their revenue and triple their profits in the next 2 years. But critically, these should NOT be EPC (construction) companies. Stocks that meet these two conditions can more than triple in 1-1.5 years because valuations are already at rock-bottom levels.

### Strategy 4: Avoid Government-Dependent EPC Companies

Stay away from road construction, infrastructure EPC, and any companies heavily dependent on government spending. Reasons: government spending is not picking up, payment delays of 2-3 months are being reported by multiple EPC managements, and with bad market conditions these companies can't raise funds either, creating severe cash flow problems.

### Strategy 5: Favour Niche Moat Companies in SME Space

Companies like Pricol (CCTV manufacturing) and P Electrical (GIS - Gas Insulated Switchgear manufacturing) are near all-time highs even in this carnage because they do something no other SME company does. Look for companies with a clear niche or moat that differentiates them.

### Strategy 6: Don't Marry Your Losers

If a stock is down 20-30% and H2 results don't meet your expectations (not all-time high revenue, not all-time high profit), switch immediately to a stock with better prospects. Don't wait for that specific stock to recover your loss. Money can be made from any stock.

### Strategy 7: Use ROC Indicator for Exit Timing

When the ROC (Rate of Change) indicator reaches 100, that is the signal to exit equity or drastically reduce allocation. At that point, shift 80% to bonds, gold, or other asset classes. Keep only 20% in equity. This is a multi-asset approach, not a permanent equity-only strategy.

### Strategy 8: Nifty Futures for Leveraged Upside (Advanced)

Harsh has taken a Nifty June Futures position at 22,300 levels with just 1% portfolio allocation (which becomes ~10% with leverage). Max loss scenario: if Nifty drops 3%, he loses only 0.2-0.3% of total portfolio. If things go wrong, he rolls futures to the next month and holds. Historical data shows 35%+ return from these levels within a year, so rolling futures is a viable strategy. MTF (Margin Trading Facility) is NOT recommended because interest charges make profitability very difficult.

### Strategy 9: Micro Cap 250 Over Small Cap 250 for Next 12 Months

Between the two indices, Harsh favours Micro Cap 250 for the next 12 months. Reason: both technically and fundamentally, Micro Cap 250 has better valuations and stronger relative strength. However, there is no ETF for this index, so you have to fundamentally screen individual stocks.

### Strategy 10: Be Patient, the Rally Will Be Worth It

Expected rally duration: at least 1.5 years, possibly 2-2.5 years since we are bouncing from a hard reset zone (not a soft reset). BSE SME IPO Index needs to rally 65% just to reach its previous all-time high. This could happen in 8-10 months (by Dec 2025 to Feb 2026). Stock-specific returns during this rally can be 100-300% given that a 65% index recovery typically translates to 2-3x in individual quality stocks.

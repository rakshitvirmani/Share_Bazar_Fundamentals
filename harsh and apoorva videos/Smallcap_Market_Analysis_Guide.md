# Step-by-Step Guide to Analyse the Smallcap Market

Based on the framework shared by Investing with Harsh & Apoorva, here is a structured approach to analysing the Indian smallcap market and timing your entries.

---

## Step 1: Pick the Right Benchmark

Do not compare your portfolio returns against Nifty 50. If you hold smallcap stocks (market cap 5,000 Cr to 60,000 Cr), your correct yardstick is the Nifty Smallcap 100 Index or Nifty Smallcap 250 Index. Nifty 50 is dominated by 7-8 large companies (Reliance, HDFC, Airtel, TCS, ICICI, SBI etc.) that control ~50% of its weightage. Your smallcap portfolio will naturally diverge from Nifty 50's performance.

---

## Step 2: Use a Log Chart (Not Linear) for Long-Term Trend Analysis

Open the Nifty Smallcap 100 index on a weekly timeframe and switch the price scale to logarithmic. On a regular (linear) chart, you see absolute price change — a Rs.100 move looks the same whether the stock went from 100 to 200 or from 900 to 1000. On a log chart, you see rate of change — a 100% move (50 to 100) looks the same as another 100% move (500 to 1000). This is critical for identifying long-term trends and support/resistance zones that actually matter.

---

## Step 3: Identify the Long-Term Trendline Support Zone

On the log chart, draw a trendline connecting the major historical bottoms of the Nifty Smallcap 100 Index. Historically, the index has bounced from this support zone every time — 2008 correction, 2013 correction, COVID crash (briefly broke it but quickly recovered), 2022 correction, and 2023 correction. Check where the current index level sits relative to this trendline. If the gap is large, a significant correction is still needed before the next major rally.

---

## Step 4: Track the 150 EMA on the Weekly Chart

Add the 150-period Exponential Moving Average (EMA) on the weekly timeframe chart. This acts as a strong intermediate support level. Historically, multiple rallies have started from the 150 EMA level — March 2023, 2022 rally, and so on. If the index is near the 150 EMA and fundamentals are favourable, a short-term bounce (soft reset of ~10%) can happen from here even without a full trendline test.

---

## Step 5: Assess the Two Correction Scenarios

Based on the trendline and 150 EMA, evaluate which scenario is more likely:

Scenario A — Soft Reset (~10% correction): The index finds support around the 150 EMA, bounces back, completes a cup-with-handle formation, and breaks out to new highs (target around 26,000-27,000 levels). This rally will not be as ferocious but provides decent returns. A hard reset is merely postponed and will come later (possibly in 2027).

Scenario B — Hard Reset (~20% correction): A significant bad news event (unfavourable budget, geopolitical tension, global economic shock) pushes the index down to the long-term trendline support (~14,000 levels). This is a brutal correction but historically, every hard reset has been followed by an extremely powerful multi-year bull rally delivering 100-500%+ returns at the index level.

---

## Step 6: Study the Nifty 50 / Nifty Smallcap Ratio Cycle

Search for "Nifty 50 / CNX Smallcap 100" ratio chart. This ratio historically oscillates between two zones — roughly 1.15 to 1.25 at the bottom and 1.95 to 2.2 at the top.

When the ratio reaches the upper zone (1.95-2.2), it means largecaps are overvalued relative to smallcaps. This is when the smallcap rally triggers. When it reaches the lower zone (1.15-1.25), it means smallcaps are overvalued relative to largecaps and a top is forming.

Track this ratio. The smallcap bull rally will start when this ratio approaches or reaches the 1.95-2.2 zone. A ratio of ~2.0 essentially means Nifty 50 has become 2x relative to smallcap. If Nifty 50 reaches 29,000 and smallcap drops to ~14,000, this ratio gets fulfilled and both triggers (trendline support + ratio) overlap — creating a very high-conviction buy signal.

---

## Step 7: Track the USD-INR Correlation

Open the USD-INR chart alongside the Nifty Smallcap chart. Historically, whenever USD-INR reaches a top (i.e., the Rupee depreciates to a resistance level), smallcap rallies have started shortly after. This does not tell you how much the index will fall, but it gives you a rough timing signal for when the next rally is likely to begin.

---

## Step 8: Monitor the Smallcap / Gold Ratio

Search for "CNX Smallcap / Gold Bees" ratio. This ratio moves between roughly 90 and 313 historically. When the ratio hits the lower zone, smallcaps are deeply undervalued relative to gold and life-changing bull rallies follow. When it tests intermediate support levels, good (but not generational) rallies occur. Currently, if this ratio has broken intermediate support, it signals that a deeper correction is needed before the next major rally. The bottom zone is where the truly generational wealth-creating opportunities lie.

---

## Step 9: Check the ROC (Rate of Change) Indicator

The ROC indicator helps you understand when the market's rate of change is hitting zero (bottoming out) versus when it signals overvaluation or undervaluation. Study how ROC has historically triggered at major bottoms of the smallcap index. When ROC reaches specific levels at the bottom, it aligns with the other triggers above. (Refer to dedicated ROC analysis for detailed level understanding.)

---

## Step 10: Evaluate Valuations Using PE Ratio

Check the current PE of the Nifty Smallcap 100 index on the NSE website. Run a simple scenario analysis:

Calculate base EPS: Current Index Level / Current PE.

Project new EPS forward by 3-4 quarters assuming a per-quarter earnings growth rate (e.g., 3-4% per quarter, i.e., 10-16% cumulative).

Divide the anticipated index level (after correction) by the new EPS to get the forward PE.

For reference, during the last bull rally trigger in March 2023, the PE was around 16-17. At the peak, it reached 35-36. For a healthy bull rally to have significant upside, the PE needs to come down to the low-to-mid 20s. In the hard reset scenario (index at ~14,000 with 4 quarters of 4% earnings growth), the PE drops to ~21.8 — very favourable for a sustained rally.

---

## Step 11: Formulate Your Capital Allocation Strategy

Until there is clarity on which scenario plays out, follow this approach:

Allocate 50-60% of your capital to largecap stocks in sectors showing relative strength (sectors making new all-time highs even in a bearish broader market). Nifty 50 already has a rally underway and is likely to continue growing.

Limit smallcap exposure to 20-30% of capital, and only in stocks where you have high conviction, the company is fairly valued, and future prospects look solid. This is not the time to be super aggressive in smallcaps.

Use the December-to-March period for tax loss harvesting — book losses in underperforming positions to offset gains and revamp your portfolio.

Keep strict stop losses on all trading positions.

Build cash reserves so you are ready to deploy aggressively when the hard reset trigger fires.

---

## Step 12: Wait for the Triggers to Overlap

The strongest buy signal comes when multiple triggers align simultaneously:

1. Nifty Smallcap 100 index reaches the long-term trendline support zone
2. Nifty 50 / Smallcap ratio reaches the 1.95-2.2 zone
3. USD-INR tops out
4. Smallcap / Gold ratio hits the lower zone
5. ROC indicator signals a bottom
6. PE valuations drop to the low 20s or below

When 3 or more of these overlap, you have an extremely high-conviction setup for a sustained multi-year bull rally. This is the time to go aggressive in quality smallcaps and sectoral themes. Be patient — the hard reset followed by a generational rally is worth the wait.

**Which scenario is tracking:** On the single quarter available, **the Base case is being exceeded and the business is tracking between Base and Bull.** Revenue growth (+103.5% YoY) is far above every old scenario, but this is off a seasonally tiny Q1 (Q1FY26 was just 10.3% of FY26 revenue) — management explicitly said H1 is soft and H2 is the key execution period. **What surprised (positive):**
1. **Margins held while peers compressed.** An analyst noted most transformer peers reported Q1 margin declines due to transformer-oil/copper inflation; Kanohar *expanded* EBITDA margin to 28.3%. Management attributes this to IEEMA price-variation clauses on most contracts, and states there were no one-offs in Q1 *(per Q1FY27 concall)*.
2. **Q2 order inflow of ₹867.3 Cr** (including a ₹250 Cr+ order from a central PSU) — nearly 2.6x the Q1 inflow and ~1.3x FY26 full-year revenue in a single quarter *(per Q1FY27 PPT, slide 9)*.
3. **400 kV mix jumped to ~70%** of Q1 revenue (from 10.3% in FY24 and 52.2% in FY26).
4. **FY28 visibility**: management guided "similar year-on-year growth" in FY28 as FY27 (~40–45%) and said the FY27-end order book should be adequate to cover projected FY28 and FY29 revenue *(per Q1FY27 concall)*.

**What surprised (negative / incremental caution):**
1. **Capacity is the binding constraint by FY28.** At ~45% growth in FY28, utilization of the existing 19,200 MVA approaches ~90% (management agreed "it will be in that range"); the +18,000 MVA brownfield expansion is targeted "by the end of FY28" for FY29 *(per Q1FY27 concall)*. Any slippage caps FY29 growth. Management also said not to expect FY29 growth above the 40–45% range even with doubled capacity.
2. **Competition is acknowledged.** Management expects new entrants into 400 kV and Scott transformers (qualification takes 2–4 years) and conceded "some short-term supply-demand mismatch and short-term margin pressure" beyond the 3–4-year visibility window.
3. **Working capital guidance is 110–120 NWC days** — worse than the 107 days reported for FY26 *(per Q1FY27 concall)*.
4. **Disclosure gaps**: no segment EBITDA, no bid pipeline, no Q2 order-book closing figure, and no quarterly cash flow.

**Correction to prior version:** The old note described the IPO capex line as "₹64.18 Cr of a planned ₹641.83 Cr capex line item". ## 1. Business Model Overview

Kanohar Electricals is a third-generation, family-promoted (Singhal family, since 1972) manufacturer of power transformers, headquartered in Meerut, Uttar Pradesh. *Per RHP, "Our Business – Overview," page 244*, the company describes itself as "one of the leading domestic players in transformer manufacturing in terms of revenue in Fiscal 2026," and is one of only **five companies in India** with short-circuit test certification for 500 MVA/400 kV transformers (used in power transmission), one of **four** RDSO-certified manufacturers of 100 MVA/132 kV Scott transformers for Indian Railways, and one of **two** certified for 100 MVA/220 kV Scott transformers (Kanohar was the first Indian company to get RDSO approval at the 220 kV rating *(per Q1FY27 concall)*). It has completed 200+ successful short-circuit tests at government labs.

**Two business segments** *(per RHP, page 244)*:

| Segment | FY26 Revenue (₹ Cr) | % of FY26 Revenue | % of Q1FY27 Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transformer Manufacturing Business | 545.51 | 83.43% | ~85.2% |
| EPC Business (substations + transmission lines) | 107.50 | 16.44% | ~14.8% |
| Other operating revenue (scrap) | 0.83 | 0.13% | — |
| **Total** | **653.84** | **100%** | **137.1 Cr** |

*Q1FY27 split per Q1FY27 PPT, slide 7, and concall (transformer 84.9%, EPC 14.81%).*

- **Transformer Manufacturing**: Power transformers (57.09% of FY26 revenue), Scott transformers for railways (25.29%), traction transformers (0.92%), distribution transformers (0.13%). Shunt reactors had nil FY26 revenue as orders were booked for FY27 execution; management expects shunt reactors to settle at **10–15% of revenue on average** and they are 13.1% of the June-26 order book *(per Q1FY27 concall)*. Distribution transformers are lower-margin and not a focus. Management says 400 kV power transformers, Scott transformers and shunt reactors carry **broadly similar margin profiles**; transformer margins are higher than EPC.
- **Voltage mix (Q1FY27)**: 400 kV 70.16%, 132–<220 kV 11.38%, 220–<400 kV 3.19%, <132 kV 0.23% of revenue. **End-user mix (Q1FY27)**: Power Transmission 85.18%, Railways 11.53%, Renewable Energy 2.84%, Power Distribution 0.23% *(per Q1FY27 concall)*.
- **EPC Business**: Turnkey installation of air/gas insulated substations (entered 2013) up to 400 kV class, and transmission lines up to 400 kV (entered 2021). Enables "single window" bids combining equipment supply + installation, which widens the addressable market as utilities bundle transformers into substation packages.
- **GIS**: Manufacturing tie-up with Chung-Hsin Electric and Machinery Manufacturing Corporation (CHEM), Taiwan, since 2017 (valid to Nov 2027, auto-extendable) — but **zero revenue from GIS in the last three fiscals**.
- **Exports**: Past experience (US, West Africa, Southeast Asia), but management is focused on the domestic market for the next 3–4 years *(per Q1FY27 concall)*. Inverter-duty transformers are not a focus area.

**Manufacturing facilities** *(per RHP, "Our Business," page 261)*: Two plants, both in Meerut, UP —
- **Rithani Manufacturing Facility** (commenced 1983): up to 25 MVA, 66 kV — legacy, small-scale unit.
- **Gangol Manufacturing Facility** (commenced 1997): ~18,000 MVA, up to 500 MVA, 400 kV (equipped for 765 kV) — the growth engine, backward-integrated (in-house tanks, radiators, control panels, insulation components).

**Customer base**: Predominantly government/PSU — 93.62% of the FY26 order book and 85.37% of FY26 revenue came from government tenders *(per RHP)*. In **Q1FY27, 99.6% of revenue came from government entities** (Central Government 58.8%, State Government 40.8%, Private 0.4%), and two states — Gujarat (44.8%) and Haryana (22.9%) — made up 67.7% of Q1 revenue by geography *(per Q1FY27 PPT, slide 18)*. The June-26 order book is 88.4% government / 11.6% private *(per Q1FY27 PPT, slide 9)*. Key named customers include POWERGRID (₹568.67 Cr order won June 2025; ~24-month execution *(per Q1FY27 concall)*), Blue Star, BNC Power Projects, and Bhutan Power Corporation. **Top 10 customers = 93.16% of FY26 revenue** (top 2 ~48%).

**Employees**: Over 526 as of March 31, 2026, with regional offices in Delhi NCR, Mumbai, Kolkata, Bangalore and Chennai.

*Per RHP, Risk Factor #3 (page 21), "Objects of the Offer" (page 131), Q1FY27 PPT slides 10 & 17, and Q1FY27 concall.*

| Facility | FY24 Capacity (MVA) | FY25 Capacity (MVA) | FY26 Capacity (MVA) | FY26 Utilization | Planned (end-FY28) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rithani Unit | 1,200 | 1,200 | 1,200 | **0.25%** | 1,200 |

**Utilization trend**: 16.46% (FY24) → 29.70% (FY25) → 45.99% (FY26) → ~36% annualized in Q1FY27 (1,725 MVA; seasonally weak quarter). Management's own math: ~45% growth in FY28 takes utilization of the current 19,200 MVA to **~90%**, so **existing capacity is sufficient through FY28** and the new capacity is needed for FY29 *(per Q1FY27 concall)*.

**Planned expansion (IPO-funded)** *(per Q1FY27 PPT, slide 10)*:

| Use of Net Proceeds (₹ Cr) | Total | FY27E | FY28E |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capex — new machinery at Gangol (+18,000 MVA), automated backward integration (radiators), office building, sustainability (solar ~2,500 kW, EVs) | 64.2 | 24.2 | 40.0 |
| Incremental working capital | 155.0 | 155.0 | — |
| General corporate purposes | 55.5 | 27.7 | 27.7 |
| **Total** | **274.7** | **206.9** | **67.7** |

- **Timing**: brownfield +18,000 MVA to "come up by the end of FY28," in place for FY29. The new capacity will be **suitable for 765 kV** *(per Q1FY27 concall)*.
- **Capital efficiency**: ₹64.2 Cr to double MVA capacity is very low capex intensity, because the expansion uses existing land, test labs and infrastructure at Gangol.
- **765 kV**: technology developed in-house (no tie-up needed); the company is ready to build a prototype. No Indian player has yet completed a 765 kV short-circuit test. Management says it is realistically a **3–4 year journey** (win first orders, supply, demonstrate field performance) before Kanohar can bid for regular 765 kV tenders *(per Q1FY27 concall)*. 765 kV is therefore a post-FY29 lever, not a FY27–FY29 driver.
- **Rithani** remains effectively idle (0.25% utilization); still no stated consolidation or write-down plan.

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

### Status of catalysts from the prior analysis

| # | Catalyst | Status (Nov 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mix-shift toward >400 kV transformers | **Materialized / strengthening** — 10.3% (FY24) → 52.2% (FY26) → **~70% (Q1FY27)**; ~70% of the order book is power transformers, mostly 400 kV |
| 2 | Order-book inflection | **Materialized** — ₹1,818 Cr (Mar-26) → ₹2,026 Cr (Jun-26); **₹867.3 Cr inflow in Q2FY27** incl. a ₹250 Cr+ central-PSU order |
| 3 | India T&D capex supercycle | **Strengthened** — Green Energy Corridor III (₹1.85 lakh Cr, entirely intra-state transmission) announced; state utilities (Maharashtra, Gujarat) launching large programmes; intra-state is mostly 400 kV, the company's sweet spot |
| 4 | Capacity doubling funded by IPO | **On track** — target end-FY28; FY28 utilization to reach ~90% of the current base first |
| 5 | Backward integration | **Intact** — tanks, radiators, control panels, insulation; no plans to integrate further |
| 6 | Railways & renewables diversification | **Intact / building** — Railways 11.5% and RE 2.8% of Q1 revenue; shunt reactors 13.1% of order book |
| 7 | Fresh-listing catalyst | **Materialized** — listed Sept 16; maiden concall drew broad institutional participation (Union AMC, Mirae, Quantum, Trust MF, Motilal, Nuvama etc.) |
| 8 | 765 kV entry | **Delayed vs. implicit expectation** — 3–4 year journey; prior Bull shifter ("765 kV order win") is unlikely before FY29 |

### Catalysts

1. **Qualification moat in 400 kV**: Only five Indian companies hold 500 MVA/400 kV short-circuit certification; a new 500 MVA certification takes **18–24 months** concept-to-certificate, plus field-performance history *(per Q1FY27 concall)*. Management estimates the 400 kV transformer TAM at roughly **₹4,000–6,000 Cr/year** and ~**60,000–70,000 MVA** last year, growing **10–15%** a year. If ~70% of FY27E guided revenue's manufacturing portion is 400 kV (₹950 Cr × ~85% × ~70% ≈ ₹565 Cr), Kanohar would hold roughly 9–14% value share of that TAM (my estimate, not management's).
2. **Order-book cover**: ₹2,026 Cr at June-26 = ~2.1x FY27E guided revenue, executable over 18–24 months; tenders being bid now are mostly for **FY29–FY30 execution**. Management says the FY27-end order book should broadly cover FY28 and FY29 projected revenue, and that the order book and current tendering carry margins **similar to FY26**.
3. **GEC-III and intra-state transmission**: a new demand layer on top of inter-state (ISTS) ordering; the bulk of intra-state work is at 400 kV.
4. **Railways — Scott-connected transformers**: India has electrified ~99.6% of broad gauge; future work (high-speed rail, DFCs, Vande Bharat, heavy haul) uses the 2x25 kV AC system, which needs Scott transformers. Per a Railways conference cited by management, ~1,000 traction substations (≈2,000 transformers) are envisaged over 5–10 years, an opportunity of **~₹20,000 Cr** *(per Q1FY27 concall)*. Kanohar is one of two certified at 100 MVA/220 kV; Scott margins are broadly similar to 400 kV.
5. **Shunt reactors** for renewable grid integration: management guides 10–15% of revenue on average.
6. **Commodity pass-through**: Most contracts carry the IEEMA price-variation clause, which protected margins in a quarter when peers were squeezed by copper and transformer-oil inflation.
7. **Operating leverage + capital-light expansion**: ₹64.2 Cr of capex to double capacity, on top of a base that reaches ~90% utilization in FY28.
8. **Long-term option — 765 kV**: technology ready in-house, new capacity 765 kV-capable; a FY30+ lever.

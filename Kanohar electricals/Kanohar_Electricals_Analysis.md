## 1. Business Model Overview

Kanohar Electricals is a third-generation, family-promoted (Singhal family, since 1972) manufacturer of power transformers, headquartered in Meerut, Uttar Pradesh. *Per RHP, "Our Business – Overview," page 244*, the company describes itself as "one of the leading domestic players in transformer manufacturing in terms of revenue in Fiscal 2026," and is one of only **five companies in India** with short-circuit test certification for 500 MVA/400 kV transformers (used in power transmission), one of **four** RDSO-certified manufacturers of 100 MVA/132 kV Scott transformers for Indian Railways, and one of **two** certified for 100 MVA/220 kV Scott transformers.

**Two business segments** *(per RHP, page 244)*:

| Segment | FY26 Revenue (₹ Cr) | % of Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Transformer Manufacturing Business | 545.51 | 83.43% |
| EPC Business (substations + transmission lines) | 107.50 | 16.44% |
| Other operating revenue (scrap) | 0.83 | 0.13% |
| **Total** | **653.84** | **100%** |

- **Transformer Manufacturing**: Power transformers (57.09% of manufacturing revenue in FY26), Scott transformers for railways (25.29%), traction transformers (0.92%), distribution transformers (0.13%). Shunt reactors had nil FY26 revenue as orders are booked for FY27 execution.
- **EPC Business**: Turnkey installation of air/gas insulated substations (entered 2013) up to 400 kV class, and transmission lines up to 400 kV (entered 2021). Enables "single window" bids combining equipment supply + installation.
- The company also has a **gas insulated switchgear (GIS)** manufacturing tie-up with Chung-Hsin Electric and Machinery Manufacturing Corporation (CHEM), Taiwan, since 2017 (valid to Nov 2027, auto-extendable) — but has generated **zero revenue from GIS in the last three fiscals** as no GIS tenders were awarded to the company.

**Manufacturing facilities** *(per RHP, "Our Business," page 261)*: Two plants, both in Meerut, UP —
- **Rithani Manufacturing Facility** (commenced 1983): up to 25 MVA, 66 kV — legacy, small-scale unit.
- **Gangol Manufacturing Facility** (commenced 1997): up to 500 MVA, 400 kV (design-capable up to 765 kV) — the primary growth engine, backward-integrated (in-house transformer tanks and radiators).

**Customer base**: Predominantly government/PSU — 93.62% of the FY26 order book and 85.37% of FY26 revenue came from tenders floated by government entities *(per RHP, "Our Business" and MD&A)*. Key named customers include Power Grid Corporation of India Limited (POWERGRID) — order book of ₹573.26 Cr as of March 31, 2026, following a single ₹568.67 Cr order won in June 2025 — plus Blue Star Limited, BNC Power Projects Limited, and Bhutan Power Corporation Limited (export customer). **Top 10 customers accounted for 93.16% of FY26 revenue** (top 2 alone ~48%).

**Employees**: Over 526 as of March 31, 2026, with regional offices in Delhi NCR, Mumbai, Kolkata, Bangalore and Chennai.

*Per RHP, Risk Factor #3 (page 21) and "Objects of the Offer" (page 131).*

Utilization trend: 16.46% (FY24) → 29.70% (FY25) → 45.99% (FY26) — capacity utilization has nearly tripled in two years, but Rithani (the legacy 1983 unit) has been essentially idle (6.66% in FY24, then just 0.25% for two straight years), representing ~1,200 MVA of stranded/dormant capacity that the company has not consolidated or written off.

**Planned expansion**: A portion of IPO Net Proceeds (₹64.18 Cr of a planned ₹641.83 Cr capex line item) is earmarked for new machinery/equipment at Gangol, expected to add **+18,000 MVA of annual installed capacity** (effectively doubling total capacity to ~37,200 MVA), automating backward-integration (radiator manufacturing), civil construction of an office building, and sustainability capex (solar power plants, electric trucks/forklifts at Gangol). Deployment schedule: ₹24.18 Cr in FY27E, ₹40.0 Cr in FY28E *(per "Objects of the Offer," page 132)*. Separately, ₹155 Cr of Net Proceeds is earmarked for incremental working capital to support order-book conversion.

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

1. **Mix-shift toward high-voltage transformers**: Revenue share of >400 kV transformers rose from 10.30% (FY24) to 52.17% (FY26) — the highest-margin, most technically-differentiated segment, where only five Indian manufacturers hold the requisite short-circuit certification, limiting competitive intensity *(per RHP, "Strengths," page 249)*.
2. **Order book inflection**: Order book grew from ₹595.85 Cr (FY24) to ₹1,818.32 Cr (FY26) — a 74.69% CAGR and ~2.8x FY26 revenue, providing strong near-term revenue visibility. The single ₹568.67 Cr POWERGRID order (June 2025) alone equals ~87% of FY24's entire revenue.
3. **India's T&D capex supercycle**: Per the CARE Report commissioned for the RHP, India's transformer market is projected to grow from USD 4.94 bn (CY25) to USD 6.85 bn (CY30, 6.7% CAGR), with extra/ultra-high-voltage segments (400 kV+) growing fastest (8.8–9.3% CAGR). India is targeting ~143,000 circuit-km of new transmission lines by FY32 and 594 GW renewable capacity by FY32, both structurally transformer-intensive.
4. **Capacity doubling funded by IPO proceeds**: +18,000 MVA planned by FY28, layered on top of a base that is itself only 46% utilized — significant operating leverage if order momentum sustains.
5. **Backward integration**: In-house transformer tank and radiator manufacturing (vs. outsourcing) supports both margin and lead-time advantages over less-integrated peers.
6. **Railway electrification and renewable energy demand**: Traction/Scott transformer and shunt-reactor order book provides diversification beyond pure power-transmission cyclicality; renewable-linked shunt reactor orders booked in FY26 are due to convert to revenue in FY27.
7. **Fresh listing catalyst**: Re-entry into public markets after a 16-year gap (delisted 2010) brings visibility, access to growth capital, and potential index inclusion over time as market cap and free float season.

### Investment Verdict

**Weighted Fair Value: ₹818/share | CMP: ₹790 (Sept 21, 2026) | Rating: ACCUMULATE**

At CMP ₹790, Kanohar Electricals trades at a ~3.4% discount to its probability-weighted FY27E-based fair value of ₹818/share, placing it in the ACCUMULATE band on the entry-point framework.

**Bull Case (25% probability) — what needs to go right**: Order book conversion accelerates, EBITDA margin holds near the FY26 peak of 27–28% as the >400 kV mix keeps rising, new Gangol capacity comes online on/ahead of schedule and quickly ramps utilization past 55%, and the company continues to win large PSU/POWERGRID orders without customer-concentration blowing up. This would justify a re-rating toward ₹1,480/share (55x FY27E P/E), still below current sector-average multiples.

**Base Case (50% probability) — most likely path**: Growth decelerates from FY25/26's hyper-growth pace (63%/45% YoY) to a still-strong ~40% in FY27E as the ₹1,818 Cr order book converts, with EBITDA margin normalizing modestly to ~25%. This supports a fair value near ₹788/share (40x FY27E P/E) — broadly consistent with the current market price.

**Bear Case (25% probability) — what could go wrong**: Government tender delays, unhedged raw-material cost inflation, and/or a stumble in one of the top-2 concentrated customer relationships cause growth to decelerate sharply (~20% YoY) and margins to revert toward FY25 levels (~18-20%), triggering a de-rating toward the peer-low multiple (TRIL's 32x, discounted further to 22x for small-cap/newly-listed risk) — implying a fair value of only ₹218/share, a >70% downside from CMP.

**Rating: ACCUMULATE.** The underlying business — a scaled, technically-differentiated, low-leverage transformer manufacturer riding a genuine multi-year T&D capex supercycle with 2.8x revenue order-book cover — is fundamentally attractive, and the current price is close to fair value on a probability-weighted basis with modest (~3.6%) implied upside. However, the extreme customer concentration (93% in top 10, ~48% in top 2), recurring governance/compliance lapses, unhedged commodity exposure, weak operating cash conversion relative to reported profit, and a stranded/idle legacy facility (Rithani) all warrant a measured, staggered accumulation approach rather than aggressive buying — and position sizing should account for the stock's five-day trading history and likely high near-term volatility.

## 7. Key Metrics to Monitor

Track each quarter (post-listing quarterly results, exchange filings, investor presentations):

1. **Order book & book-to-bill ratio** — watch for continuation of the ₹1,818 Cr (FY26) order-book base; net new order inflow vs. revenue recognized each quarter.
2. **Revenue mix of >400 kV transformers** — currently 52.17% of FY26 revenue; a proxy for margin sustainability (higher voltage = higher value-add, less competition).
3. **EBITDA margin trend** — FY26 was 27.59%, up sharply from 20.73% (FY25) and 11.23% (FY24); watch for reversion below 20–22% as a bear-case signal.
4. **Capacity utilization** — Gangol was 49.04% in FY26 (target: track progress toward the planned +18,000 MVA expansion and whether utilization keeps climbing); Rithani remains at 0.25% — watch for any consolidation/write-down decision.
5. **Net Working Capital days** — improved from 124 (FY24) to 107 (FY26); a rise back above 120–130 would signal cash-conversion stress.
6. **Customer concentration** — top 10 = 93.16% of FY26 revenue, top 2 ~48%; any single-customer loss or slowdown (especially POWERGRID, ₹573 Cr order book) is a material risk event.
7. **Raw material cost trends** — copper, CRGO steel and transformer oil prices (company runs no hedging); cost of materials was 75.9% of FY26 total expenses.
8. **POWERGRID and other large-PSU order execution/timelines**, and progress on 765 kV (ultra-high-voltage) transformer qualification/bids.
9. **Resolution of the six pending RoC adjudication applications** and whether the FY27 annual report/auditor's report carries fresh CARO adverse observations.
10. **Promoter (K Sons Family Trust) holding** post-listing lock-in — any further OFS/stake sale post lock-in expiry.
11. **EPC segment execution** — historically volatile (48% of FY24 revenue down to 16% in FY26); watch for re-acceleration as the FY26 order book (10.8% EPC share) converts.
12. **Dividend policy** — no dividend paid to date; any initiation would be a signal of maturing free cash flow generation.

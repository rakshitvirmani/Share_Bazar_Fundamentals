## 1. Business Model Overview

Innova Captab Limited is a Baddi-headquartered pharmaceutical manufacturer operating a dual-engine model: CDMO (contract development and manufacturing) for domestic and regulated-market clients, and Branded Generics (trade generic pharma for chemist/stockist distribution). The company operates across three legal entities consolidated under the listed parent: **Innova Captab Limited** (CDMO + domestic branded generics), **Univentis Medicare** (100% subsidiary, Branded Generics), and **Sharon Bio-Medicine** (100% subsidiary, regulated-market CDMO via Europe/Canada/UK/Australia).

**Business Verticals (from Q1FY26 onward):**

| Vertical | FY26 Revenue | FY26 % | Revenue Model | Margin Profile |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CDMO | ₹1,133 Cr | ~69.5% | Cost-plus; API is pass-through | Ex-Jammu: 18–19% EBITDA; blended 15–16% |
| Branded Generics | ₹497 Cr | ~30.5% | Trade generic (no MR force); chemist/stockist | 15–18% EBITDA |

**CDMO Model Detail:**
- API sourced and passed through to customer at cost; conversion charges are Innova's revenue
- ~350+ active CDMO clients; domestic pharma companies, regulated-market MNCs via Sharon
- Gross margin is partially optical — lower API prices = lower revenue but higher gross margin % on conversion
- Regulated market CDMO: Sharon Bio-Medicine serves Europe, Canada, UK, Australia (~₹240 Cr FY26)
- UK-MHRA approval on Baddi plant; Kathua Jammu plant received PIC/S certification (Nov 2025) — opens regulated market supply from Jammu

**Branded Generics Model:**
- ~1,500+ SKUs; sold through chemist/stockist distribution without ethical MR force
- 30+ countries export reach; 18 therapeutic categories
- Growing share of chronic molecules (cardiac, diabetology, CNS) layered on top of anti-infective base
- Rapid 51% FY26 growth driven by distribution expansion and new therapeutic category entry

**Manufacturing Infrastructure:**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baddi Main | Baddi, Himachal Pradesh | Operational; 55–60% utilized | Cephalosporins (dry powder, injectables), general formulations | UK-MHRA approved (H1FY26); Schedule M compliant |
| Kathua Jammu | Kathua, J&K | Commissioned Jan 14, 2025; ramping | Cephalosporins + general; β-lactam + non-β-lactam blocks | PIC/S certified (Nov 2025); ₹480 Cr invested |

| Sharon sites | Multiple | Operational | Regulated-market CDMO | EU GMP |

**Jammu Economics (critical to understand):**
- ₹480 Cr greenfield capex; funded via QIP + debt
- GST incentive scheme: 5% refund on domestic sales (reduced from 12% in Q2FY26 due to GST rate rationalization)
- At ₹5% incentive and ₹75 Cr maximum cap: requires ~₹1,400 Cr revenue to hit cap (was ₹650 Cr at 12% rate)
- Jammu EBITDA breakeven: ~₹60–65 Cr/quarter revenue
- Depreciation from Jammu: ~₹24–25 Cr per annum; bites PAT growth even as EBITDA grows
- Peak capacity potential: ₹1,400 Cr revenue at 65–70% utilization (3–5 year horizon)

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

### A. Jammu Operating Leverage — The Central Thesis

The ₹480 Cr Kathua Jammu greenfield plant is the single most important driver of Innova's next 3–5 year earnings. The economics are asymmetric:

- Fixed costs (depreciation ₹24–25 Cr/year, interest on capex) are already fully loaded in the P&L
- EBITDA breakeven achieved at ~₹60–65 Cr/quarter (Q1FY26 was the threshold quarter)
- Every incremental ₹10 Cr of Jammu revenue at current scale flows through at very high margins (~25–30% incremental EBITDA)
- From ₹300 Cr (FY26) to ₹1,400 Cr theoretical peak = ₹1,100 Cr of incremental high-margin revenue ahead
- Timeline: management guides 3–5 years for peak utilization; requires customer audits, regulatory certifications (PIC/S done), market development

This is a textbook greenfield ramp-up: front-loaded pain (high depreciation + low utilization), back-loaded reward (high incremental EBITDA on fixed cost base already absorbed). *Per Q4FY26 concall*

### B. PIC/S Certification + UK-MHRA — Regulated Market Unlock

Two critical regulatory milestones achieved in H1FY26:
- **UK-MHRA approval for Baddi plant** — enables supply to UK, European partner countries
- **PIC/S certification for Kathua Jammu** — enables supply to all PIC/S member countries (40+ countries incl. Canada, Australia, Singapore, EU members)

Significance: CDMO clients in regulated markets (EU, Canada, Australia, UK) can now use Jammu as their manufacturing source. These are higher-value, more sticky relationships vs. domestic CDMO. Sharon Bio-Medicine (~₹240 Cr) already serves this segment; Jammu PIC/S opens a second regulated-market-capable facility. *Per H1FY26 concall*

### C. Branded Generics Scaling — New Distribution Engine

Branded Generics grew 51% in FY26 — the fastest-growing segment. Drivers:
- Adding new therapeutic categories beyond anti-infectives: cardiac, diabetology, CNS
- Distribution expansion into new geographies and stockist relationships
- Univentis Medicare subsidiary as focused execution vehicle
- 30+ country export reach in generics
- No ethical MR force required — trade generic model scales with distribution width

At ₹497 Cr (FY26) and 51% YoY growth, Branded Generics is on track to approach or exceed ₹700 Cr by FY27. *Per Q4FY26 concall*

### D. Sharon Bio-Medicine Synergies

Sharon (~₹240 Cr FY26) provides:
- Regulated market (EU GMP) manufacturing relationships — higher margin, more stable
- International CDMO client base that can be transitioned to Jammu (PIC/S now certified)
- Geographic revenue diversification (Europe, Canada, UK, Australia)
- Margin accretive to blended EBITDA (regulated market conversion = better pricing)

### E. Schedule M Compliance Advantage

India's revised Schedule M (Good Manufacturing Practices) deadline has passed or is imminent. Many smaller CDMO players (the 600+ unit pharma manufacturing cottage sector in India) are non-compliant and unable to upgrade without material capex. Innova is fully compliant across all plants, creating a structural consolidation opportunity as large pharma clients consolidate CDMO supply chains toward compliant, large-scale manufacturers.

### F. API Cost Pass-Through = Margin Insulation

Innova's CDMO model is cost-plus with API as a pass-through. When API prices fell 20% (potassium clavulanate from ₹18,500 → ₹13,000/kg in Q1FY26), revenue appeared softer but conversion margins were preserved. As API prices recover (management noted reversal from April 2026 due to Middle East conflict disruptions to supply chains), CDMO revenue will mechanically re-accelerate even without volume growth. This asymmetry protects downside and amplifies upside.

## 5. Red Flags & Risk Analysis

| Risk | Detail | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| **Jammu Ramp Slower Than Guided** | Original FY26 Jammu guidance: ₹400 Cr. Actual: ₹300 Cr. Customer validation/audit timelines are outside Innova's control. Regulated market clients require rigorous qualification — timelines are unpredictable. If ramp continues at current pace, peak utilization could take 6–8 years vs. management's 3–5 year target. | 7/10 |
| **GST Incentive Erosion** | J&K GST incentive cut from 12% → 5% in Q2FY26 (government GST rate rationalization). Max incentive remains ₹75 Cr/year but requires ~₹1,400 Cr Jammu revenue (was ₹650 Cr at 12%). At FY26's ₹300 Cr Jammu revenue, incentive realization is minimal. Full ₹75 Cr incentive is years away. Future rate changes are a policy risk. | 6/10 |
| **API Price Volatility** | Cephalosporins and amoxicillin/clavulanate are major API categories. China and India dominate global supply. COVID-era oversupply pushed prices down 20%+ (FY25-FY26 headwind). Middle East conflict and supply chain disruptions are reversing this from April 2026. While cost-plus model insulates conversion margin, low API prices suppress top-line and capacity utilization optics. | 5/10 |
| **PAT Growth Lagging Revenue Growth** | FY26: revenue +31%, PAT only +10%. Jammu depreciation (₹24–25 Cr/year) + interest charges are structural P&L headwinds until utilization scales. This lag will persist for 2–3 more years. Investors expecting earnings growth to match revenue growth will be disappointed near-term. | 6/10 |
| **Geopolitical Risk — Jammu Location** | Kathua, Jammu is near the India-Pakistan border. India-Pakistan tensions (escalated April-May 2026) create operational risk. Management confirmed no operational disruption as of Q4FY26 concall, but this is a tail risk specific to this plant location that other CDMO competitors don't carry. | 5/10 |
| **New Baddi Block Capex Timing** | ₹150–170 Cr new Baddi block announced for FY27–28. If Jammu is still underutilized while new Baddi block is added, fixed cost base grows again before earnings catch up. Timing of when to deploy this capex (vs. filling Jammu first) is a capital allocation judgment call. | 5/10 |
| **CDMO Competitive Landscape** | Domestic CDMO market has multiple capacity additions: Leeford Healthcare and others expanding. Innova's advantages are regulatory approvals + scale + track record, but pricing pressure from competitors is real. New entrants adding Schedule M-compliant capacity could compress conversion margins over time. | 5/10 |
| **Working Capital Expansion** | WC requirements grow with revenue, especially in CDMO (API procurement lead times + customer payment cycles). As Jammu ramps and Branded Generics scales, absolute WC will expand. Monitor short-term debt trajectory. | 4/10 |
| **Single-Segment API Concentration** | Significant cephalosporin concentration (Baddi and Jammu both have β-lactam blocks). A structural shift away from cephalosporin prescriptions (antibiotic stewardship, resistance concerns) or a disruptive new entrant in ceph API supply could materially impact volumes. | 4/10 |

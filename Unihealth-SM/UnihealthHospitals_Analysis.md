## 1. Business Model Overview

UniHealth Hospitals Limited (formerly UniHealth Consultancy Limited) is a Mumbai-based integrated hospital operator with a unique India-Africa presence. The company has transitioned from a hospital management consultancy into a direct hospital operator and is building a hub-and-spoke model that links Africa patient referrals into India tertiary care facilities.

**What they do:** Own and operate hospitals (asset-light lease model in India; owned assets in Uganda), manage hospitals under O&M (Operations & Management) contracts, and generate medical value travel (MVT) referral flows from Africa networks to India.

**Revenue Mix (FY26):**
| Geography | Revenue Share | EBITDA Profile | Model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Uganda | ~90% | High — 40%+ | Owned hospital; 120–150 beds; tertiary care |
| India | ~10% (scaling) | Ramp-up phase | Asset-light lease; Navi Mumbai (52 beds, Oct 2025) + Nashik (200 beds, Jul 2026) |
| Nigeria | Exited | — | Fully exited April 2026 |
| Tanzania | Pipeline | — | O&M contract; 20-bed license imminent; 100-bed Dar es Salaam in agreement |

**Uganda Operation (Core Business):**
- Kampala-based hospital serving Uganda Ministry of Defense (UPDF), private patients, and international patients
- Strong brand recognition in Uganda built over 15+ years
- Average Revenue Per Occupied Bed (ARPOB): ₹40,000+/day (H1FY26), targeting ₹55,000–60,000
- Occupancy: 72% on 120 beds (H1FY26); expanding to 150 beds
- Uganda government pays in USD — natural USD revenue stream for an INR-listed company
- 10-year income tax holiday (July 1, 2024 – June 30, 2034) — structurally zero tax in Uganda for the decade

**India Model (Asset-Light Expansion):**
- No land or building purchase; lease-based entry
- Capex limited to equipment + fit-out (~₹2–3 Cr per small facility)
- Navi Mumbai: 52-bed multispecialty; soft-launched Oct 2025; ICU added Feb 2026
- Nashik: 200-bed facility; commissioning July 2026
- Medical Value Travel: Africa patients referred to India facilities within the group — India facilities bill at international patient rates (meaningfully higher than domestic rates); zero referral fee

**Consultancy / O&M Contracts:**
- Managing ~1,300 beds across Africa under consultancy/management contracts (as of H1FY25)
- Tanzania: O&M model with no asset ownership; 20-bed initial license pending; 100-bed Dar es Salaam facility in agreement stage
- Pure fee income; no balance sheet risk on managed assets

**Key Differentiator:** No other Indian hospital chain has African operational presence with a captive patient referral pipeline into India. The India-Africa hub-and-spoke is genuinely unique.

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

### A. Uganda ARPOB Re-Rating — The Margin Engine

The most significant financial development is the Uganda ARPOB jump from ~₹25,000/day (FY25) to ₹40,000+/day (H1FY26) — a 60%+ step-up without any bed count increase. This is pricing power, not volume. Management is targeting ₹55,000–60,000/day by FY27, which would be another 40% increase. If achieved, Uganda alone could generate ₹90–100 Cr EBITDA at current occupancy.

Drivers: improved case mix (higher complexity/higher billing), growing international patient share, improved insurance mix, and the natural USD billing advantage.

### B. India Ramp — The Growth Kicker

India was nil revenue in FY24, 15% in H1FY26, targeting 50% of group revenue by FY27. This is coming from:
- **Navi Mumbai (52 beds):** Operational Oct 2025; already showing ₹32,500 ARPOB (comparable to Uganda); ICU capability from Feb 2026 enables higher-acuity cases
- **Nashik (200 beds):** Commissioning July 2026; largest India facility; full ramp over 9–15 months post-launch
- **Medical Value Travel:** Africa patients referred into India units bill at international patient rates (2–3x domestic rates); zero incremental cost to acquire

India hospitals are loss-making or breakeven in first 6–12 months (greenfield operating leverage); the inflection comes after beds are consistently above ~50–60% occupancy. Nashik at scale is the transformational India facility.

### C. Nigeria Exit — Strategic Focus Unlock

Nigeria was contributing 8% of revenue (H1FY25) but consuming disproportionate management bandwidth with poor economics. Full exit completed April 2026. This removes:
- Low-margin revenue diluting group EBITDA
- Management attention cost
- Geopolitical/currency risk (Nigerian Naira volatility)

Capital and management now concentrated on Uganda (high EBITDA), India (high growth), Tanzania (new market entry).

### D. Uganda 10-Year Tax Holiday (Jul 2024 – Jun 2034)

Uganda granted a 10-year income tax exemption starting July 1, 2024. For a company generating 90% of EBITDA from Uganda, this is a structural suppressor of the effective tax rate for the entire decade. As Uganda profitability grows, the group effective tax rate stays near zero on the Uganda component. The holiday ends June 2034 — meaningfully post any near-term investment horizon. *Per H1FY26 concall*

### E. Uganda Debt-Free Status

Uganda operations fully repaid all bank debt as of September 30, 2025. A debt-free, cash-generative Uganda hub means:
- All future Uganda EBITDA converts directly to PAT (no interest cost)
- Uganda free cash flow available to fund India expansion without equity dilution
- Balance sheet de-risked going into the scale-up phase

### F. Tanzania Entry — Third Africa Hub

Tanzania is the next geography:
- **Stage 1:** Small 20-bed facility under O&M license (imminent)
- **Stage 2:** 100-bed Dar es Salaam hospital (in agreement stage); Dar es Salaam is East Africa's primary commercial hub
- O&M model means zero balance sheet risk; Tanzania generates management fee income + positions UNIHEALTH for eventual hospital ownership
- East Africa hub-and-spoke: Kampala (Uganda) → Dar es Salaam (Tanzania) → potential Nairobi / Kigali in long term

### G. Medical Value Travel — Structural Revenue Multiplier

The India-Africa integration thesis: Africa patients (Uganda, Tanzania, Nigeria networks) requiring tertiary care beyond local capability are referred to UNIHEALTH India facilities. These patients:
- Bill at international patient rates (₹50,000–80,000/day ARPOB potential vs ₹27,000 domestic average)
- Require no separate marketing spend — captive referral from existing Africa relationships
- Create fully unique economics not available to any domestic Indian hospital chain

As India bed capacity scales from 52 (Navi Mumbai) to 252+ (post-Nashik), the MVT pipeline from Africa becomes a meaningful incremental revenue layer.

## 5. Red Flags & Risk Analysis

| Risk | Detail | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| **Uganda Government Receivables — Structural Cash Trap** | Ministry of Defense (UPDF) is the single largest payer in Uganda. Payment cycles are 9–13 months. As of H1FY26, ₹112 Cr total receivables of which ₹82 Cr is MoD (Uganda). Debtor days are 240–250 days structurally. Even with healthy EBITDA, cash conversion is severely delayed. Growth consumes more working capital, compounding the problem. Target is 150 days medium-term — unclear how achieved without MoD payment terms improving. | 8/10 |
| **Single-Hospital Revenue Concentration (Uganda)** | Uganda is ~90% of group revenue. A political disruption, regulatory change, or hospital-level incident in Uganda could wipe out the majority of group earnings. Uganda elections in late 2025 already caused a visible H2FY26 revenue dip. One country, one hospital, one government payer = extreme concentration risk. | 8/10 |
| **India Greenfield Ramp Risk** | Nashik (200 beds) is the biggest India bet — commissioning July 2026. Greenfield hospitals take 9–15 months to turn cash-flow positive. During ramp: fixed costs (staff, lease, utilities) without revenue. Navi Mumbai (52 beds) showed slow build in first 2.5 months (₹3 Cr). Nashik will burn cash through H1FY27 before contributing meaningfully. If ramp disappoints, FY27 guidance misses badly. | 7/10 |
| **Uganda ARPOB Sustainability** | H1FY26 ARPOB of ₹40,000+/day vs FY25's ₹27,000 is a 48% jump. Management is guiding another 40% jump to ₹55,000–60,000 by FY27. Two consecutive 40%+ ARPOB increases requires sustained case mix upgrade and pricing ability. If Uganda ARPOB plateaus at ₹40,000–42,000, the entire FY27 financial model needs revision. | 7/10 |
| **H2FY26 Seasonal Weakness — Recurring Pattern** | H2FY26 revenue (~₹67 Cr) was materially weaker than H1FY26 (₹70 Cr) on an absolute basis. Uganda elections + December Christmas gap are cited as reasons. If this is a recurring seasonal pattern (elections are periodic; Christmas is annual), H2 of every year will be softer than H1. Investors expecting linear quarterly progression will be disappointed. | 6/10 |
| **Receivables / Cash Crunch Risk** | ₹112 Cr receivables at H1FY26 on ₹137 Cr full-year revenue = 82% of annual revenue stuck in receivables. Cash flow from operations may be deeply negative despite high EBITDA margins. Uganda being debt-free is positive, but India expansion requires cash. If Uganda MoD delays payment further, funding India growth from internal accruals becomes constrained. | 7/10 |
| **Small Management Team / Execution Bandwidth** | Simultaneously managing: Uganda scale-up (ARPOB push + 30-bed expansion), Navi Mumbai ramp, Nashik commissioning + ramp, Tanzania O&M entry, Tanzania 100-bed negotiation, and MVT program launch. Each initiative is full-time work. Small listed company management teams running this many initiatives simultaneously face high execution failure risk. | 6/10 |
| **Tanzania Regulatory / Execution Risk** | Tanzania license not yet received as of June 2026. Regulatory timelines in Africa are uncertain. The 100-bed Dar es Salaam deal is still "in agreement stage" — not signed. Timeline slippage in Tanzania is likely; investors should not embed Tanzania revenue before FY28. | 5/10 |
| **USD Revenue but INR Reporting — Currency Tailwind/Risk** | Uganda revenues are USD-denominated; reported in INR. A stronger INR vs USD reduces reported EBITDA without any operational change. Conversely, INR depreciation inflates reported financials. The company does not appear to actively hedge. | 4/10 |
| **Low Liquidity / Market Cap** | Sub-₹500 Cr market cap with limited analyst coverage and half-yearly reporting. Institutional investors cannot take meaningful positions. Thin float means large price moves on small transactions. Half-yearly reporting (vs. quarterly) creates a 6-month information gap between data points. | 5/10 |

- Uganda alone: 150 beds × 75% occupancy × ₹60,000/day × 365 = ₹246 Cr Uganda revenue

- Uganda ARPOB stays at ₹40,000–42,000; occupancy dips due to elections
- Nashik ramp slower than modeled (12–18 months to breakeven)
- MoD receivables balloon further; cash flow deeply negative
- FY27 revenue: ₹180–200 Cr; EBITDA: ₹65–75 Cr; PAT: ₹25–35 Cr
- No rerating without receivables resolution + India proof point

## 1. Business Model Overview

AVTL is a pure-play, fee-for-service **tank storage infrastructure** company — it does not take title to, trade, or hold inventory risk in any product it stores. Revenue is earned purely as a **"throughput charge"** (a per-metric-ton or per-CBM fee charged on volume moving through its terminals), not a rental/occupancy fee — *"occupancy has got no relevance here... revenue generating occupancy is always close to 100%"* *(per Q1 FY26 concall, Aug 2025; reaffirmed across all five calls)*.

**Two (soon three) reporting segments:**
- **Liquid Terminaling** — storage/handling of petroleum, petrochemicals, chemicals and vegetable oils. Highly flexible: product can be switched within ~48 hours, and the company can handle imports, exports and coastal trade, which management repeatedly cites as insulating the business from tariff/trade-flow shocks. Liquid realization is highly location/mix-dependent — JNPA alone realizes **~₹6,000/CBM/year vs a blended average of ~₹3,000/CBM** *(per Q1 FY27 concall, Aug 2026)*.
- **Gas (LPG) Terminaling** — cryogenic refrigerated storage, standardized pricing (**~₹1,175–1,200/ton across all terminals** — *"LPG is a volume-driven game... tariff is same across all terminals"*, per Q1 FY27 concall). Gas terminals take 5–7 years to reach full utilization (start at 25–30%, scale ~20–25%/year) but have a 40-year asset life.
- **Ammonia (new, third vertical)** — India's first independent third-party ammonia terminal, commissioned at Pipavav in **August 2026** (36,000 MT static capacity), backed by a 15-year take-or-pay with Hindustan Zinc. Realizations are cited as **2.5–3x LPG** on a per-ton basis, though turnaround is roughly one-third that of LPG. Japan's **ITOCHU Corporation** took a 10% stake in the ammonia subsidiary (Aegis Terminal Pipavav Limited) in FY26, with an option to raise it to 25% over three years — the company's first strategic (non-parent) equity partner at the asset level.

**Construction model:** all physical infrastructure is built by parent **Aegis Logistics Limited** (leveraging its in-house EPC efficiency/cost advantage) and then transferred to AVTL at a markup captured as "other comprehensive income" (~20–22% margin, netted out on consolidation) — see Red Flag #3.

**Network (as of Aug 2026):** 6 operational ports on both coasts (JNPA/Maharashtra, Haldia/West Bengal, Kandla & Pipavav/Gujarat, Kochi/Kerala, Mangalore/Karnataka), connected via a mix of pipeline (JLPL, KGPL, Uran-Chakan, and the upcoming Mangalore-Hassan-Cherlapalli), rail and road evacuation. Since JV formation (Nov 2021), liquid storage capacity has grown **3.75x** and LPG static capacity **4.5x** *(per Q4 FY26 concall, Jun 2026)*.

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

- **Project GATI** — a deliberate, capacity-ahead-of-demand national build-out strategy ("we follow demand, no flag planting" — but building fast once demand is visible), executed with JV-parent construction cost/speed advantages.
- **JV moat**: Aegis Logistics (India market/distribution knowledge) + Royal Vopak (global AAA-ESG operator, 400 years, 23 countries) — cited repeatedly by management and by multinational customers as a safety/ESG differentiator that wins contracts.
- **Debt-light, then measured re-leveraging**: IPO proceeds fully extinguished ~₹2,016 Cr of bank debt (Jun 2025); subsequently re-levered in a disciplined way via listed NCDs (₹1,690 Cr in FY26) rather than bank debt, targeting a 0.6x gearing ratio capped at 3.5x EBITDA.
- **Multi-modal evacuation build-out is the single biggest near-term throughput lever** — each pipeline hookup (JLPL — live; KGPL — guided H1 FY27; Haldia-Panagarh — Oct/Nov 2026; Mangalore-Hassan-Cherlapalli — FY28) and each VLGC-compliant jetty (Kandla — live Dec 2025; Pipavav — CY2026) has historically preceded a step-up in utilization on these calls.
- **Diversification into Ammonia** — first-mover in India's independent third-party ammonia terminaling, riding the green-hydrogen/DAP-fertilizer and specialty-chemicals demand wave; ITOCHU's minority stake is an external validation signal.
- **East Coast entry via HALPG/Haldia** — converts AVTL from a West-coast-concentrated operator to a pan-India platform, anchored by an exclusive HPCL agreement running to 2038.
- **New-port optionality**: Vadhavan (₹20,000 Cr non-binding MoU) plus an unnamed 8th port, with a stated ambition of 8–12 ports by 2030 (from 6–7 today) — management also flags newer categories (inland depots, strategic storage for the government, industrial terminals, jetties) beyond the traditional port-terminal model.
- **"Flight to quality" thesis** — management explicitly frames much of the industry's growth as market-share transfer from small, inefficient, less-compliant operators to scaled, safety-compliant players like AVTL, rather than pure category growth.
- **Growing take-or-pay/long-tenor revenue visibility** — HPCL (to 2038), Hindustan Zinc (15 yrs), unnamed conglomerate (15 yrs, 0.5 mn MT/yr) — a rising share of capacity is contractually locked in versus the historically pure-spot/open-source model.
- **Segment mix shift toward Gas** — management guides the long-run mix toward **55–60% Gas / 40–45% Liquid** (from ~48/52 in FY26) as recently-commissioned cryogenic LPG and ammonia capacity matures through its 5–7 year utilization ramp.

## 5. Red Flags & Risk Analysis

*Severity: 10 = highest risk.*

| # | Risk | Severity | Detail |
|---|---|:---:|---|
| 1 | **Valuation is extremely stretched vs. fundamentals** | **8/10** | TTM P/E of ~104–115x against a company growing PAT at 20–35% even in a bull scenario implies a PEG well above 3x. The probability-weighted fair value derived in Section 6 (~₹180/share) is ~40% below the CMP used in this note (~₹297.5). See Section 6/7 for full workings. |
| 2 | **Geopolitical/Middle East supply disruption (Strait of Hormuz)** | **6/10** | National LPG import volumes fell as much as ~50% at the trough (Mar 2026) due to shipping disruption from the Iran-related conflict, recovering to only ~80–85% of normal by July 2026. AVTL's Gas segment revenue actually **declined 3.5% YoY in Q1 FY27** as a direct result — the first segment-level YoY decline disclosed across the five quarters reviewed. Management says AVTL was relatively insulated (diversified sourcing, no ships stuck in the Strait) but the macro dependency on LPG imports remains structural. |
| 3 | **100% of construction is outsourced to, and marked up by, the parent** | **6/10** | All AVTL infrastructure is built by parent Aegis Logistics Limited and transferred to AVTL at a development margin of **~20–22%**, captured (and eliminated on consolidation) via "other comprehensive income." This is efficient for speed/cost, but it is a structural related-party dependency with no independent EPC competitive tension, and the parent captures a chunk of the value-add before the asset even reaches AVTL's books. |
| 4 | **Repeated commissioning/timeline slippage** | **6/10** | Examples across the five calls: JNPA liquid capacity guided "before end of this financial year" (Aug 2025) → "Q1 of FY27" (Feb 2026) → "Q3 of FY27" (Aug 2026). KGPL pipeline connectivity guided "Q2 FY26" → "Q4 FY26 (Mar-26)" → "worst case June '26" → "H1 FY27" → still "during the year" as of Aug 2026. A consistent multi-quarter pattern of guided dates slipping right, which is relevant given the FY27E projections below depend on several of these connections landing on schedule. |
| 5 | **Large mandatory equity dilution ahead** | **6/10** | AVTL must dilute to 25% public float by **June 2, 2028** (SEBI minimum public shareholding norms, 3 years post-IPO) — management confirms this will be a primary funding source for the $5 bn capex plan. This is a known, quantifiable EPS-dilution event not yet priced into most simple per-share projections; timing and issue price are still undecided. |
| 6 | **Reduced disclosure comparability mid-track record** | **5/10** | (a) FY25 and FY26 financials were **restated mid-year** for HALPG consolidation, meaning Q1–Q3 FY26 quarterly figures (as originally reported) are not directly comparable to the restated Q4 FY26/FY26-total figures or to Q1 FY27's YoY commentary. (b) Q1 FY27 is the first quarter management reported **"Cash PAT" (₹124.9 Cr)** instead of the "Net Profit" metric used in the prior four quarters, without a reconciliation — a mild but real reduction in like-for-like comparability just as growth optics matter most. |
| 7 | **No throughput/volume guidance provided** | **4/10** | Management explicitly and repeatedly declines to give forward throughput guidance ("we do not give guidance on throughput... it depends on our customers"), and per-segment EBITDA splits are only disclosed "at year end." This limits external forecastability and increases reliance on qualitative management commentary each quarter. |
| 8 | **Concentration in a handful of large anchor counterparties** | **4/10** | HPCL (exclusive at Haldia to 2038), Hindustan Zinc (~28% of Pipavav ammonia capacity for 15 years), and an unnamed large conglomerate (0.5 mn MT/yr at Pipavav) collectively anchor a growing share of new capacity. Positive for revenue visibility, but a single large counterparty renegotiation, default or delay (e.g., Hindustan Zinc's DAP plant timeline) would be disproportionately felt at that specific terminal. |
| 9 | **No pricing power in Gas; realization is essentially flat** | **4/10** | Gas throughput tariffs are explicitly standardized across all terminals (~₹1,175–1,200/ton) and do not escalate with product scarcity or inflation — *"tariff is same across all terminals... the emphasis is on volume growth, not escalation."* Nearly all Gas segment growth must come from volume/utilization, not price, structurally capping margin-expansion optionality on that segment. |
| 10 | **Large headline capex commitments remain non-binding** | **4/10** | The L&T ammonia-at-Kandla MoU and the ₹20,000 Cr Vadhavan Port MoU are both explicitly "non-binding" as of the latest (Aug 2026) call, roughly a year after first being announced. The $5 bn 2030 capex figure is a stated ambition rather than a fully contracted pipeline — management itself says "it may be lumpy... time will tell." |

**Bull case rationale:** KGPL pipeline connects on schedule (H1 FY27) and immediately lifts Kandla/Pipavav gas throughput; the 0.5 mn MT/yr Pipavav conglomerate contract starts on time (end-CY2026); ammonia ramps faster than the guided 20–25% first-year utilization; Gas segment fully recovers from the Hormuz disruption by Q2/Q3 FY27 and resumes 25%+ YoY volume growth (per management's stated target); JNPA liquid phase-1 commissions on schedule in Q3 FY27 at strong realization from day one (as management explicitly expects, unlike prior phased ramps).

**Base case rationale:** Growth continues but with the pattern of quarter-to-quarter timeline slippage seen across all five calls persisting at a similar cadence; Gas recovers only gradually through FY27 rather than snapping back; EBITDA margin holds near FY26/Q1FY27 levels but PAT growth lags EBITDA growth as depreciation and interest step up with newly commissioned assets (ammonia, HALPG full-year, NCD interest annualizing) — a standard infra-company pattern of PAT growing slower than EBITDA in a heavy capex-commissioning year.

**Bear case rationale:** Hormuz-related disruption persists or recurs through FY27; JNPA/Kochi/KGPL slip further (consistent with the repeated slippage pattern flagged in Red Flag #4); rising interest/depreciation from the ₹1,690 Cr of FY26 NCDs and newly commissioned assets outpaces EBITDA growth, compressing PAT margin; one or more anchor counterparties defer offtake.

### Probability-Weighted Fair Value Calculation

```
Weighted FV = (₹288 × 25%) + (₹176 × 50%) + (₹81 × 25%)
            = ₹72.0 + ₹88.0 + ₹20.3
            = ₹180.3/share  ≈  ₹180/share
```

**At the reference CMP of ₹297.5, this implies the stock trades at a ~65% premium to probability-weighted fair value** — i.e., on this bottom-up framework, the market is pricing in a materially more optimistic outcome than even this note's Bull case (₹288) delivers on its own. This is the central finding of this analysis and should be read alongside Red Flag #1.

### Probability Shifters

**Bull probability rises if:**
- KGPL pipeline connects at both Kandla and Pipavav within the guided H1 FY27 window without further slippage
- Gas segment revenue returns to positive YoY growth (reversing the Q1 FY27 -3.5% print) by Q2/Q3 FY27
- The 0.5 mn MT/yr Pipavav conglomerate take-or-pay contract commences on schedule (end-CY2026) at full disclosed volume
- Ammonia terminal utilization exceeds the guided 20–25% first-year run rate
- JNPA liquid phase-1 (~100,000 CBM) commissions in Q3 FY27 as currently guided, with no further push-out

**Bear probability rises if:**
- The Strait of Hormuz/Middle East disruption to LPG imports persists or recurs beyond H1 FY27
- JNPA, Kochi or KGPL timelines slip again (extending the pattern already seen across five consecutive quarters)
- The mandatory Phase-2 equity dilution (to 25% public float by June 2028) is executed at a depressed price, or earlier/larger than currently signaled
- Interest and depreciation from FY26's NCD raises and newly commissioned assets outpace EBITDA growth, compressing PAT margin below ~33%
- Any of the three large anchor counterparties (HPCL, Hindustan Zinc, the unnamed Pipavav conglomerate) defers or renegotiates its committed volumes

## Investment Verdict

**Lead with the number:** On a probability-weighted basis (Section 6), Fair Value is estimated at **~₹180/share**, against a reference CMP of **~₹297.5/share** (Sep 9, 2026 quote — dated ~12 days before this note; treat as approximate). That is a **~65% premium to fair value**, placing the stock firmly in **REDUCE** territory per the entry-point framework above. Even this note's own **Bull case (₹288/share)** sits below the reference CMP — the market is currently pricing in an outcome more optimistic than this analysis's most aggressive scenario.

**Bull case (25% probability, ₹288/share):** Every major near-term catalyst lands on schedule for once — KGPL pipeline connects at both Kandla and Pipavav in H1 FY27, the Gas segment snaps back from the Hormuz-related decline, the Pipavav conglomerate contract and JNPA liquid phase-1 both commission on guided dates, and the newly-commissioned ammonia terminal ramps faster than guided. FY27E PAT reaches ₹470 Cr (+37% YoY).

**Base case (50% probability, ₹176/share):** Growth continues at a healthy clip but the pattern of quarter-to-quarter guidance slippage seen across all five reviewed calls persists; Gas recovers gradually rather than snapping back; PAT growth (₹405 Cr, +18.5% YoY) lags EBITDA growth as depreciation/interest scale with newly commissioned assets — a typical infra-company dynamic in a heavy capex-commissioning year.

**Bear case (25% probability, ₹81/share):** The Hormuz-related disruption persists into FY27, capacity timelines slip further, the mandatory Phase-2 equity dilution lands at a depressed price, and rising interest/depreciation compress margins. FY27E PAT falls to ₹280 Cr (-18% YoY).

**Rating: REDUCE**, on valuation grounds specifically — not on business-quality grounds. The underlying franchise is genuinely strong: scarce, hard-to-replicate 40-year-life infrastructure assets; a credible JV parentage (Aegis Logistics + Royal Vopak) with real construction cost/speed advantages; growing take-or-pay revenue visibility (HPCL to 2038, Hindustan Zinc for 15 years); a disciplined balance sheet (debt-light post-IPO, targeted 0.6x gearing); and a clear, well-articulated $5bn capacity roadmap to 2030. But at ~104–115x TTM earnings and a ~65% premium to this note's probability-weighted fair value — a premium that survives even under this note's own Bull-case assumptions — the entry price matters more than the story right now. Investors already holding the stock from lower levels, or willing to underwrite continued multiple expansion on momentum/scarcity value alone, may choose to hold; fresh capital is better deployed waiting for either (a) a pullback toward the ₹180–190 zone, or (b) clear evidence that the FY27 catalysts (KGPL, JNPA phase-1, Pipavav conglomerate offtake, ammonia ramp) are landing on schedule and the earnings base has genuinely caught up to the price.

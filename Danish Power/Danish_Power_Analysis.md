## 1. Business Model Overview

Danish Power is a Jaipur-based (Mahindra World City SEZ/DTA) maker of **liquid-filled and dry-type transformers**, plus **control & relay panels and substation automation**. It was incorporated in 1985 and listed on the **NSE Emerge (SME) platform in Oct 2024** through a ~₹198 Cr IPO that was mainly used for capacity expansion. It is not yet eligible to migrate to the main board: about three years of SME listing are needed, and management will "look in that direction" once that period ends (*per H1FY26 concall, Nov 2025*).

**Core franchise - Inverter Duty Transformers (IDT) for solar:**
- One of the first Indian makers of IDTs, starting in **2011–12**, when "nobody was doing IDTs" (*per H2FY26 concall*).
- **~14 GW of installed IDT base** in Indian solar projects. Against ~120 GW of national solar capacity, management estimates its **market share at ~10–11%**. There is no published data to confirm this (*per H1FY26 concall*).
- IDTs are **~65–70% of revenue** (~70% in H1FY26). Management wants to keep IDTs at or below this level by diversifying (*per H1FY26 concall*).
- Most of today's output is **33 kV-class** (IDT plus small 33 kV power transformers), with some 11 kV distribution and a small amount of 66/132 kV.

**Other segments:**

| Segment | Share / status | Source |
|---------|---------------|--------|
| IDT (solar) | ~65–70% of revenue | *H1FY26 concall* |
| Distribution + small power transformers (≤33 kV) | Most of the remaining ~25% | *H1FY26 concall* |
| BESS transformers | Already supplying. **~20–25% of the current order book** | *H2FY26 concall* |
| Power transformers (up to 100 MVA / 220–245 kV) | New. Prototypes and type-testing under way. Real revenue from **FY28** | *H2FY26 concall* |
| Dry-type transformers (data centres) | Made for 14 years but small. Floor space now allocated and certifications in progress | *H2FY26 concall* |
| Control relay panels & substation automation (up to 400 kV) | ~5% of revenue in H1FY26, **7–9%** in FY26 | *H1FY26 & H2FY26 concalls* |

**Customer and geography mix:**
- **~90% private sector**. Government discoms are avoided because they pay late (*per both concalls*). Most revenue comes from repeat clients.
- **Exports were ~8–9% of FY26 revenue**, up from a negligible share after COVID. Danish has exported since 2003 to the Middle East, Europe, Africa and Australasia, and ~99% of exports are direct. Targets: **15–20% in FY27** (*May 2026*). In Nov 2025 the targets were 20% in FY27 and 30% in FY28, so they have been trimmed. Exports to the US are on hold because of tariffs, and Danish has no UL certification (*H1FY26 concall*).
- Management says export margins are **+1–5% above domestic** depending on the project, though some export markets earn lower margins than India (*per both concalls*).

**Seasonality:** The business earns most of its revenue in H2. H1 is "much lighter", and in the past H2 has run above nameplate capacity (*per both concalls*).

**Competitors named by management (IDT):** Shilchar Technologies, Wilson Power (unlisted), Crompton/CG, and Atlanta Electric (*per H1FY26 concall*). **Waaree** has entered transformers through an acquisition. Management says it has lost no orders to Waaree so far and has added 2–3 new large clients (*per H2FY26 concall*).

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

1. **Capacity has doubled and is now ramping (FY27–FY28).** Capacity went from ~5,000 to ~11,000 MVA. The FY27 output guide (7,000–8,000 MVA) is 27–45% above FY26 (~5,500 MVA). Phase 2 contributes a full year for the first time in FY27, and a mostly H2-weighted ramp suits the company's seasonality.

2. **BESS as the second IDT-like wave.** BESS is already 20–25% of the order book. Storage paired with new solar projects, and retrofits on existing plants, add transformer demand on top of IDTs. Danish's 14 GW IDT installed base and its long-standing solar EPC and developer relationships give it a natural route into these customers.

3. **Moving up the voltage ladder (from 33 kV to 132/220 kV).** If the 100 MVA / 245 kV power transformers qualify, Danish enters a market with fewer competitors and higher entry barriers (type tests, audits, field track record). Management expects some margin give-up at first to gain entry. This is the FY28+ diversification lever away from solar-only exposure.

4. **Export re-acceleration.** Exports rose from ~0 to 8–9% of revenue in FY26. Long-term framework agreements have been signed with international clients, and the FY27 target is 15–20%. Exports earn +1–5% margins on select projects. The constraint had been capacity, not demand.

5. **Margin resilience as a differentiator.** Danish held ~19% EBITDA through commissioning costs and a commodity spike, while peers reportedly took hits (an analyst made this observation on the H2FY26 call). Its approach is to choose clients carefully (90% private sector, repeat customers, prompt payers) and to avoid L1 (lowest-bid) tenders.

6. **Next expansion of +80–100%.** Land is already secured, and the decision was due ~Aug 2026, with 12–18 months to come online. That would add capacity from roughly late FY28 to FY29, about when the current plant reaches full utilisation. This is what keeps growth going after FY28.

7. **Rerating triggers:** the CRISIL A- upgrade, a possible main-board migration once eligible (~late 2027), more frequent order disclosures (promised), and Ind AS reporting.

### Probability Shifters

**Bull probability ↑ if:**
- H1FY27 revenue is **>₹290 Cr** (≥+37% YoY on ₹211 Cr) with EBITDA margin **≥18%** despite the oil spike
- The order book is **>₹650 Cr** at the H1FY27 call, with exports ≥15% of the book
- A board-approved expansion of **+80–100%** that is funded mostly by debt or accruals (no deep dilution)
- The first **132/220 kV type test is passed** and a power-transformer order is won before FY28
- BESS stays at ≥25% of the order book, and the first data-centre dry-type orders arrive
- FY27 output reaches **≥8,000 MVA**

**Bear probability ↑ if:**
- H1FY27 revenue is **<₹240 Cr** (<+14% YoY) or EBITDA margin is **<16%**
- The order book is flat or below ₹500 Cr, or the inquiry-to-order win rate falls below 20%
- FY27 guidance is cut below ₹650 Cr, or the next expansion is deferred or funded by a large equity raise
- Inventory and receivables rise sharply, or operating cash flow turns negative (check in the FY27 annual report)
- Clear IDT price competition (Waaree, Shilchar, unlisted players), or a slowdown in solar additions
- A failed or delayed type test, pushing power-transformer revenue to FY29 or later

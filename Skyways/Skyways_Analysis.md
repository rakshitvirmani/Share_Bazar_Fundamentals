## 1. Business Model Overview

Skyways Air Services Limited (Skyways Group / brand "SLS") is a 40-year-old (founded 1984, New Delhi) **asset-light logistics integrator and freight forwarder** — it owns no aircraft, ships, or a meaningful truck fleet, and instead aggregates cargo and negotiates capacity with airlines, shipping lines, and a network of **26,300+ partner agents across 180+ countries** *(per FY26 Annual Report, p.14)*. Originally a customs house agent, the company became IATA-accredited in 1987 and has since built out ocean freight (2000), an overseas office network (Germany 2012, Vietnam/Bangladesh 2017, Dubai 2021, Hong Kong 2022), and a string of bolt-on acquisitions — Rahat Continental & RIV Worldwide (2023), CTC Air Carrier (2024), and **Odyssey Logistics** (Feb-2025, 51% stake) — the last of which pivoted the business meaningfully toward regulated/pharma cargo *(AR FY26, p.6-9)*.

Per WorldACD data the company is the **#1 air freight forwarder in India** by chargeable volumes for FY22–FY25 and Q1FY27, ranked 44th globally, with Indian market share strengthening from 5.9% to 6.2% quarter-on-quarter in Q1FY27 *(per Q1FY27 concall)*.

The company runs a **single reportable operating segment** ("Logistic services and other related services") under Ind AS-108 — no further internal segmental P&L is disclosed *(AR FY26, Note 37.1, p.152)*. It served **9,504 customers across 65 industries** in FY26 (up from 7,721 in FY25), with no single customer exceeding 10% of revenue *(Note 37.3, p.153)*. **Pharmaceutical cargo jumped from 9.1% to 22.9% of revenue YoY**, almost entirely via the Odyssey Logistics acquisition, and management flags pharma/temperature-sensitive cargo as a deliberate margin-protection and stickiness strategy *(CMD letter, p.9)*.

**Group structure:** Parent + **28 subsidiaries** (many not wholly owned — 51–95% stakes) + 3 associates, spanning 12 countries (India, USA, Canada, UK, Germany, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Hong Kong, Vietnam, Cambodia, Thailand, Bangladesh) and 28 Indian cities across 12 states *(AR FY26, AOC-1, p.44-49)*. Several subsidiaries are individually loss-making (e.g., Phantom Road Express, SLS Retail Supermart, Skart Global Express), while **Odyssey Logistics is the standout profit contributor** (PAT ~₹28.7 cr FY26 on a 51%-owned basis) — this matters directly for per-share valuation (see Section 6).

**Quality of earnings improved sharply**: Cash from Operations swung from near-zero (₹2.01cr, FY25) to **₹113.62cr (FY26)** — for FY26, CFO actually exceeded consolidated PAT, a genuine positive signal, though the multi-year trend shows this is a recent inflection rather than a sustained pattern.

Management explicitly chose to sacrifice unit yield to secure volume and airline/shipping-line capacity commitments (CMD letter, p.8).

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

1. **Consolidator strategy**: Aggregating shipments from smaller/mid-sized forwarders and sub-agents to negotiate better carrier terms than any single small player could — management calls this a "key area of investment" going forward (CMD letter, p.8).
2. **Pharma/regulated-cargo mix shift**: Pharma rose from 9.1% to 22.9% of revenue in one year via the Odyssey Logistics acquisition — a deliberate pivot toward higher-stickiness, less commoditized cargo.
3. **Technology-driven operating leverage**: In-house platforms (SLS 100X — 5,587 registered users, SLS Hike, Cargo Dash, Skart-Edge) plus the upcoming unified "ASAP" platform are explicitly aimed at growing volumes "without increasing headcount/other costs at the historic rate."
4. **Capital-light international expansion**: Five new Asian manufacturing-hub geographies for just ₹30 cr total commitment, leveraging existing forwarder relationships rather than heavy new investment.
5. **Deliberate volume-over-yield positioning**: Management is consciously sacrificing per-unit realization to gain share and lock in airline/shipping-line capacity — a bet that volume gains compound while rates eventually normalize.
6. **Post-IPO deleveraging**: ~₹140cr of debt already repaid from IPO proceeds; expected to structurally lower finance costs from Q3/Q4 FY27.
7. **Structural India air-cargo tailwind**: Ministry of Civil Aviation target to grow India's air cargo from 3.96 million MT to 10 million MT over 5 years; India's broader logistics industry projected to reach ~$500bn by 2031 (40–50% growth over 5 years).
8. **Opportunistic share gains from geopolitical disruption**: The West Asia conflict disrupted ~20% of regional air capacity at points; Skyways used its global PLI (performance-linked incentive) contracts with carriers to secure alternate capacity and gain customers — Indian market share rose from 5.9% to 6.2% QoQ in Q1FY27.

### Probability Shifters

**Bull probability ↑ if:**
- Genuine (non-fuel) volume growth sustains above 25% YoY for 2+ consecutive quarters
- EBITDA margin expands past 5% on a sustained basis
- New Asian geographies reach EBITDA breakeven faster than the guided 15-18 months
- EOW investigation is closed with no material finding
- Net debt declines meaningfully and finance costs fall as a % of revenue

**Bear probability ↑ if:**
- Brent crude falls sharply (below ~$70/bbl), reversing yield gains without a volume offset
- Volume growth decelerates below 15% YoY
- EOW investigation escalates or surfaces a material finding
- Brace Port or another group entity shows further value erosion or a disclosure lapse
- NCI's share of consolidated PAT rises further, diluting owners' earnings growth
- New Asian market expansion overruns its ₹30cr budget or 2-4 year gestation guidance

### FY30+ Terminal Value (Base Case trajectory)

Assuming revenue growth moderates to a ~17-20% CAGR through FY30 (FY27E ₹3,800cr → FY30E ~₹6,400cr) as the base scales, with PAT margin normalizing to ~2.6% on operating leverage:

- FY30E Group PAT: ₹6,400cr × 2.6% ≈ **₹166 cr**
- Owners' PAT (~65%): ≈ **₹108 cr**
- Terminal P/E multiple (mature logistics business): 18x
- FY30E Implied Market Cap: ₹108cr × 18 ≈ **₹1,944 cr** → **₹133.8/share**
- Discounted back 3 years at ~14% cost of equity: PV ≈ **₹90.3/share** (context only; the FY27E-anchored weighted fair value above is the primary valuation reference)

**Reconciling with the Base Case (₹123.3 vs. ₹133.8):** The arithmetic in both is correct (₹56cr × 32 = ₹1,792cr; ₹108cr × 18 = ₹1,944cr; both ÷ 14.53cr shares). But they sit awkwardly together. Owners' PAT nearly doubles from FY27E to FY30E (₹56cr → ₹108cr, ~24% CAGR), yet value per share rises only ~8.5% (₹123.3 → ₹133.8), because the multiple compresses from 32x to 18x. Discounted at 14%, the FY30 value is worth ₹90.3 today, **~27% below the Base Case fair value**. Holding ₹123.3 today and earning a 14% return needs an FY30 value of ~₹183/share (~₹2,655cr market cap). That means either:
- a terminal multiple of **~24.5x** on the Base Case ₹108cr owners' PAT, not 18x; or
- owners' PAT of **~₹148cr** at 18x, about 37% above the Base Case trajectory.

**Implication:** The Base Case ₹123.3 depends on the market keeping a growth multiple (32x) for longer than this note's own normalized trajectory supports. If the multiple de-rates to 18x by FY30 as modelled, today's price already discounts most of the next three years of earnings growth. This cross-check leans bearish and is a key reason the verdict is HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE. Note also that owners' PAT grew only 4.8% in FY26, so the Base Case +37% FY27E owners' PAT growth (₹41cr → ₹56cr) is itself a step-up that must be proven.

## 7. Key Metrics to Monitor

1. **Volume growth vs. reported revenue growth** — the single most important number each quarter; watch air tonnage and ocean TEU growth *independently* of headline revenue, since fuel pass-through can inflate the latter substantially (see Section 2/5).
2. **Air cargo yield (₹/kg) trend vs. Brent crude price** — yield is explicitly a fuel pass-through per management; a falling yield alongside falling crude is expected and healthy, but a falling yield with stable/rising crude would signal real pricing pressure.
3. **EBITDA margin trajectory** — currently thin at ~4.1-4.5%; watch for sustained expansion as evidence of the tech/operating-leverage thesis playing out.
4. **Net debt and finance cost as % of revenue** — track the promised post-IPO deleveraging (₹140cr already repaid) and whether finance costs actually decline from Q3/Q4 FY27 as guided.
5. **NCI's share of consolidated PAT / owners' EPS growth** — the metric that matters for per-share valuation, distinct from the "consolidated PAT" headline.
6. **Odyssey Logistics (pharma) standalone performance** — currently the single largest profit contributor to group growth; its growth rate and margin trajectory disproportionately drives consolidated numbers.
7. **Resolution of the EOW investigation** — any update on timeline or outcome.
8. **New Asian geography rollout (China, Philippines, Malaysia)** — progress against the 15-18 month EBITDA breakeven and ₹30cr capex guidance.
9. **ASAP platform launch and adoption** — expected within 30-60 days of the Sep-2026 concall; watch for stated impact on customer acquisition, especially Tier 2/3 markets.
10. **Brace Port and other listed/near-listed group-entity performance** — a proxy for broader group-structure and disclosure quality risk.
11. **Customer concentration and retention** — currently no client >10% of revenue and management claims "no loss of customers" through recent disruption; watch for any change.

### Investment Verdict

**Rating: HOLD** (probability-weighted fair value ≈ ₹127.1/share vs. CMP ₹126.97). That is formally 0.06% inside the ACCUMULATE band, within rounding. HOLD is kept deliberately: the FY30 terminal cross-check (PV ≈ ₹90/share) shows the Base Case relies on a 32x multiple holding up, so there is no real margin of safety.

The core bull case — a genuine, real-volume-driven consolidator gaining share in a structurally growing Indian air-cargo market, with a credible tech-driven operating-leverage story and a deliberate pivot toward stickier pharma cargo — is real and supported by hard data (19% YoY volume growth vs. 3.8% market growth; share gain from 5.9% to 6.2% QoQ). But the market has already priced most of this in at a ~45x trailing P/E, and the reported financials require real care: roughly two-thirds of the eye-catching Q1FY27 revenue growth is fuel-cost pass-through rather than durable volume expansion, and about a third of consolidated group profit belongs to minority shareholders in subsidiaries, not to Skyways' own shareholders. Layer on an unresolved EOW investigation, heavy promoter personal guarantees, rising net debt pre-IPO, and a governance structure that is only months old as a public company, and the stock looks fully — not cheaply — valued at current levels. Accumulate only on a pullback toward the ₹110-115 zone, or on clearer evidence over 2-3 quarters that volume growth (not fuel pass-through) is driving the numbers.

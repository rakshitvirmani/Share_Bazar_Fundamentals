## 1. Business Model Overview

Tega was founded in 1976 in Kolkata. It built its franchise in **mill liners**, the wear-resistant linings inside the grinding mills of copper, gold and iron ore concentrators. It claims to be one of the world's leading producers of polymer-based mill liners and has customers in 92+ countries *(AR FY26, MD&A)*. Revenue is roughly 85–90% **recurring aftermarket consumables**: liners wear out and are replaced on a cycle, whatever the mine's capex cycle.

On **1-Jun-2026** Tega completed the **acquisition of Molycop** (USA/Australia/LatAm) in a consortium with Apollo Funds, at an **enterprise value of ~USD 1.5 bn**. Tega holds **84.18%** and Apollo holds the rest *(AR FY26, Note 47; Chairman's/MD's message)*. The deal makes Tega roughly **8× larger** by revenue and changes what kind of company it is.

### Strategic logic

- **Same customer, same mill.** Liners (Tega) and grinding media (Molycop) are the two largest wear consumables in a SAG/ball mill. The pitch is a "one-stop mill optimisation solution" *(AR FY26, MD's message)*.
- **Complementary geographies.** Tega is strong in EMEA, CIS, Africa and LatAm. Molycop is strong in the USA, Canada, LatAm (largest volumes) and Australasia. Molycop claims **>50% share in its core regions** *(per Q1FY27 concall)*.
- **Synergy split.** Revenue synergies are expected mostly on the Tega side (cross-selling liners into Molycop accounts). Cost synergies sit mostly in Molycop. Non-core asset sales (land parcels) will be used to pay down debt *(per Q4FY26 concall)*.

**Manufacturing footprint (legacy Tega):** India has Dahej (2013), Samali (1985) and Kalyani (1978), plus four Tega McNally plants (Vadodara, Bangalore, Asansol, Kumardubi). Overseas plants are in Chile (2011, via Acotec), South Africa (2006) and Australia (2010). A **new greenfield Chile plant** is under construction *(AR FY26, MD&A)*.

### Key Observations

1. **FY26 was a lost year for Consumables.** Revenue was flat against a Feb-2026 guide of +8% and a long-run guide of 15%. Management blamed orders bunching into Feb–Mar plus Middle East shipping disruption, which left ~₹50 Cr of finished goods stuck, worth about ₹100 Cr of revenue *(per Q4FY26 concall)*. **Q1FY27 bore this out: Consumables +36% YoY at a 24.1% EBITDA margin.** Some of that is Q4 spill-over, so the run-rate is lower than the headline.
2. **Gross margin kept expanding** (57% → 60% → 62%) despite raw material and freight volatility. Pass-through works with about a one-quarter lag *(per Q1FY27 concall)*. This is the clearest evidence of pricing power in the legacy franchise.
3. **Working capital improved.** Debtor days fell from 113 to 99 and OCF nearly doubled to ₹350 Cr. However, inventory days rose (217 → 233) and the slow-moving/obsolete inventory provision jumped to **₹25.7 Cr from ₹10.0 Cr** *(AR FY26 cash flow)*.
4. **FY26 other income was inflated** (₹82 Cr) by gains on treasury investments of the deal money. It will normalise to ~₹30–40 Cr in FY27 because the cash has now been deployed into Molycop.
5. **Equipment is lumpy.** Growth was +65% in H1FY26, flat in H2FY26 and −44% in Q1FY27 because of delayed customer clearances. Management still guides **~25% growth at 12–13% EBITDA** for FY27 *(per Q4FY26 concall)*. That now needs a strong H2.

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

### DNA Shift: from niche high-margin Indian exporter to global, levered mining-consumables platform

| Dimension | Pre-deal (FY26) | Post-deal (FY28E) |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | ~₹1,700 Cr | ~₹17,500 Cr |
| EBITDA margin | ~19–22% | ~14–15% |
| Gross debt | ~₹320 Cr | ~₹10,000+ Cr (incl. RPS) |
| Main product | Mill liners | Grinding media (~85% of revenue) |
| Geographic centre | India manufacturing, global export | Americas/Australia local manufacturing |
| Equity story | Compounder trading at 30–40× P/E | Deleveraging plus synergies; a 20–25× P/E is more appropriate |

### Catalysts

1. **Deleveraging-led EPS growth.** Management targets **≤3× leverage within 3–4 years** *(per Q4FY26 concall)*. At closing, Molycop's debt was cut by USD 235 mn and net debt fell from USD 1.0 bn to **USD 672.5 mn** *(per Q1FY27 concall)*. Each USD 100 mn repaid saves about ₹70–80 Cr of pre-tax interest, which is ~10% of FY28E PAT.
2. **USD 20 mn of cost synergies over 2–2.5 years** (SG&A, procurement, operations) *(per Q1FY27 concall)*. That is ~₹190 Cr of EBITDA, about 8% of the FY28E group figure.
3. **Cross-selling revenue synergies** from Q3–Q4FY27. These are not in guidance yet; management asked for "a couple of quarters of patience" *(per Q1FY27 concall)*. Tega liners sold into Molycop's 400+ mines in the Americas and Australasia, and Molycop media sold into Tega's Africa/CIS base, are both upside.
4. **Copper and gold cycle tailwind.** Copper is above USD 14,000/t and gold around USD 4,400/oz *(per Q1FY27 concall)*. Copper demand is projected to rise 40%+ by 2040 (UNCTAD), and falling ore grades mean more tonnes have to be ground per tonne of metal, which lifts consumable intensity *(AR FY26 MD&A)*.
5. **Mine restarts in FY28.** Grasberg is returning to full capacity by end-CY27, and Cobre Panama could restart. These are large Molycop customers, so their return is a volume step-up *(per Q4FY26 & Q1FY27 concalls)*.
6. **Chile greenfield (Mar-2027).** Local manufacturing for LatAm, Tega's biggest growth market, cuts freight and lead times.
7. **Non-core land sales** to be used to repay debt *(per Q1FY27 concall)*.
8. **Molycop was ahead of plan in June 2026** on both revenue and EBITDA *(per Q1FY27 concall, CFO)*. That is a small sample, but it points the right way.

## 5. Red Flags & Risk Analysis

| # | Risk | Severity | Detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Leverage step-change** | **8/10** | Group gross debt is **₹11,200 Cr including ~₹2,600 Cr of Apollo RPS** *(per Q1FY27 concall)*. ### Investment Verdict

**Weighted Fair Value: ₹2,200 | CMP: ₹2,092 | Expected return: +5% | Rating: ACCUMULATE** (BUY below ₹1,980; REDUCE above ₹2,310)

- **Bull (25%) — ₹3,220.** Tega executes a clean integration. Molycop's cost synergies (USD 20 mn) arrive early, cross-selling liners into Molycop's 400+ mines starts to show in FY28, and Grasberg and Cobre Panama come back. Net debt falls toward 3× by FY28, and the market keeps a "compounder" multiple (24×) on FY28E EPS of ~₹134.
- **Base (50%) — ₹2,266.** Molycop delivers USD 195–205 mn EBITDA with modest volume growth. Tega Consumables recovers to ~15% growth at 21–22% margins. Deleveraging proceeds as planned (≤3× by FY29–30). FY28E EPS of ~₹103 at 22×.
- **Bear (25%) — ₹1,060.** Chinese forged media erode Molycop's LatAm volumes, margins slip to ~12%, synergies slip, and RPS plus debt costs stay high. FY28E EPS of ~₹66, and the stock de-rates to a 16× grinding-media multiple.

**Bottom line:** The legacy Tega franchise is excellent: 60%+ gross margins, recurring aftermarket revenue and proven pricing power. But it is now only ~15% of group revenue. At ₹2,092 the market already prices the stock at ~20× FY28E EPS, which assumes deleveraging goes to plan, so the risk-reward is **balanced, with a slight positive skew**. Accumulate gradually, and add more aggressively below ₹1,980, especially if the Q2FY27 and Q3FY27 numbers confirm Molycop's EBITDA run-rate and net debt reduction. The first full-quarter print (Q2FY27, due Nov-2026) is the key de-risking event.

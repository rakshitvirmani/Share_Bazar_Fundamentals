## 1. Business Model Overview

Steamhouse India is the pioneer and a market leader of the **"community boiler" model** in India — a centralized, pipeline-distributed model for supplying steam and other industrial gases to clusters of industrial customers, as an alternative to each factory operating its own standalone (captive) boiler. The promoter is credited with introducing this model in India in 2014 *(per RHP, "Basis for Offer Price," page 208, and F&S Report)*.

**Three (going on four) business lines** *(per RHP, "Our Business – Overview," page 305)*:

| Offering | FY26 Revenue (₹ Cr) | % of Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Generation & distribution of steam (own boilers) | 256.26 | 52.14% |
| Purchase & distribution of steam (third-party steam) | 87.00 | 17.70% |
| Coal trading | 132.33 | 26.92% |
| Generation & distribution of nitrogen | 0.58 | 0.12% |
| Others (flow meters, scrap, construction services) | 15.36 | 3.12% |
| **Total Revenue from Operations** | **491.51** | **100%** |

- **Own-generation steam**: 7 community steam boilers (6 owned + 1 leased) in Gujarat — Vapi Phase 1, Vapi WTE unit, Ankleshwar Phase 1 & 2, Sarigam, Nandesari, Panoli — combined installed capacity of **345 TPH (2,185,920 TPA)** as of July 31, 2026.
- **Purchase-and-distribute steam**: buys steam from third parties and redistributes via its own pipeline at Dahej GIDC (Phase 1) and Sachin GIDC (an O&M arrangement with **group company Sanjoo Dyeing**, which owns the underlying boiler assets). New 5-year steam-purchase agreements signed April 2026 for **Dahej SEZ** and **Haldia**, expected to commence within 12 months of the RHP.
- **Nitrogen**: commenced Feb 1, 2025 at Ankleshwar (350 Nm³/hour) — Steamhouse says it is **the only company in India** supplying nitrogen via a distributed pipeline network rather than cryogenic tanks or onsite generation *(per F&S Report)*. Still nascent (0.12% of FY26 revenue).
- **Coal trading**: the company buys coal (its primary boiler fuel) and, opportunistically, on-sells it (including to related parties Sanjoo Dyeing and Sanjoo Prints) — a low-margin, pass-through activity that has grown from ~0% of revenue (FY24) to **26.92%** (FY26).
- **Pipeline network**: 60,151 metres of operational pipeline as of July 31, 2026, up from 41,539m at end-FY24.
- **Unrelated diversification**: obtained a DGCA Non-Scheduled Operator license (Oct 21, 2025, valid to 2030) and leases a six-seater aircraft from **Sanjoo Dyeing Inc.** (a US subsidiary of group company Sanjoo Dyeing) at US$240,000/year — the RHP explicitly discloses that "our promoters and management have no experience in operations and management of aircraft services" *(Risk Factor #22)*.
- **Customers**: 202 served in FY26 (up from 173 in FY25, 125 in FY24) across pharmaceuticals, chemicals, textiles, agrochemicals, tyres, dyes & pigments, polymers and paints. Named customers include Aether Industries, Anupam Rasayan India, Gujarat Polysol Chemicals, K. Patel Chemo Pharma, Orgo Chem Gujarat, and Subhasri Pigments. Repeat-customer revenue was **90.72%** in FY26.
- **Leadership**: Vishal Sanwarprasad Budhia (Chairman & Managing Director) — 30+ years in textiles, 11 years in the community boiler industry — holds 86.97% of pre-offer capital individually (94.99% for the full promoter group).
- **Employees**: 235 permanent employees as at March 31, 2026 (up from 182 a year earlier).
- **Subsidiary**: Steamhouse Welfare Foundation — a wholly-owned Section 8 (non-profit/CSR) company, not material to consolidated financials.

## 4. Key Catalysts / DNA Shift

1. **First-mover moat in a niche infrastructure model**: pioneer of the community boiler concept in India (since 2014), with structural barriers to entry — pipeline rights-of-way, geographic space constraints in established industrial clusters, and customer switching costs once a landing point is installed *(Basis for Offer Price, Qualitative Factor #2)*.
2. **Capacity doubling underway**: 345 TPH → 705 TPH planned, funded via a mix of IPO Net Proceeds, debt, lease and a municipal subsidy (Pirana/AMC) — meaningful operating leverage if utilization holds.
3. **Only pipeline-distributed nitrogen supplier in India** *(per F&S Report)* — still tiny (0.12% of FY26 revenue) but a genuine white-space diversification beyond steam.
4. **Asset-light expansion lever**: "purchase & distribute" model (Dahej SEZ, Haldia, Kilburn Chemicals) lets the company add distribution volume with materially lower capital intensity than owning new boilers.
5. **Structural industry tailwind**: India's total process steam demand is projected by Frost & Sullivan to grow from ~203,472 TPH (FY26) to ~318,853 TPH (FY31E), a 9.4% CAGR; the F&S Report further estimates community boilers could see 55–60% CAGR in installed capacity over five years off a very low base (~3% penetration target) — this figure is a consultant estimate commissioned and paid for by the company and should be treated with caution, not as an independent forecast.
6. **Post-IPO deleveraging**: ₹180 Cr of Net Proceeds earmarked for debt repayment, which should reduce Net Debt/Equity (1.57x in FY26) and finance costs (₹21.73 Cr in FY26) going forward.
7. **Eco-friendly / ESG positioning**: 3 of 8 new plants (Nandesari Ph2, Vapi Ph3, Pirana) are designed for non-fossil fuel (agro-waste, RDF, plastic/textile waste) — aligns with the sustainability mandates of pharma/chemical/textile customers.
8. **Sticky, repeat-revenue customer base**: 90.72% of FY26 revenue from repeat customers; several top-10 relationships span 5+ years.
9. **Rich peer-set valuation benchmark**: listed comparables Linde India (P/E 99.17x) and Ellenbarrie Industrial Gases (P/E 42.74x) — average 70.96x — signal the market's willingness to pay premium multiples for essential-utility, high-revenue-visibility gas businesses in India.

**Bull probability rises if:**

- Any of the 8 new facilities slips its commissioning timeline by more than 2 quarters (repeat of the Nandesari/Dahej Phase 1 pattern).
- EBITDA margin falls below 15% (further coal-trading mix dilution, or input-cost pressure not passed through).
- Further ICRA/rating-agency downgrade, or the pledge-release condition is breached and promoter shares are re-pledged.
- Loss of, or a reduction in, the Sanjoo Dyeing relationship (largest customer at 18.36% of FY26 revenue, also a key supplier and O&M counterparty).

### Investment Verdict

**Weighted Fair Value: ₹95/share | CMP: ₹98 (Sept 21, 2026) | Rating: HOLD**

At CMP ₹98, Steamhouse India trades at a ~3.2% premium to its probability-weighted FY27E-based fair value of ₹95/share, placing it in the HOLD band on the entry-point framework — with the stock already priced at a post-issue P/E (~70x) essentially in line with the listed industrial-gas peer average (71x), despite being smaller, more leveraged, and far more related-party-dependent than either Linde India or Ellenbarrie Industrial Gases.

**Bull Case (25% probability) — what needs to go right**: All three IPO-funded facilities (Ankleshwar Ph3, Panoli Ph2, Dahej Ph2) commission on schedule and ramp utilization above FY26 levels; the Dahej SEZ and Haldia purchase-and-distribute agreements go live and scale; EBITDA margin recovers toward 18.5% as alt-fuel plants reduce coal dependency and post-IPO debt repayment cuts finance costs; nitrogen scales beyond token revenue. This would justify a re-rating toward ₹155/share (65x FY27E P/E), still below Linde India's multiple.

**Base Case (50% probability) — most likely path**: Revenue growth decelerates from FY26's 24.4% to ~20% as new capacity ramps gradually (consistent with the historical pattern of utilization lagging capacity additions — utilization actually *fell* in FY26 despite volume growth), EBITDA margin stabilizes near 17%, and PAT margin improves modestly as lower finance costs partially offset rising depreciation. This supports a fair value near ₹92/share (50x FY27E P/E) — broadly consistent with the current market price.

**Bear Case (25% probability) — what could go wrong**: A repeat of the company's own history of project delays (Nandesari Phase 1, Dahej GIDC Phase 1 both slipped 6–12 months) across the 8 simultaneously-planned new facilities, continued margin dilution from a rising coal-trading mix, an adverse development in the Sanjoo Dyeing related-party relationship (largest customer, key supplier, and O&M counterparty all at once), or the promoter share re-pledge condition being triggered — any of these would drive de-rating toward a discount to the peer-low multiple (35x), implying a fair value of only ₹42/share, a >55% downside from CMP.

**Rating: HOLD.** Steamhouse operates a genuinely differentiated, first-mover "community boiler" utility model with real structural tailwinds (9.4% CAGR India process-steam demand per F&S Report, the only pipeline-distributed nitrogen supplier in India, 90.72% repeat-customer revenue) and a capacity-doubling roadmap (345 → 705 TPH) partly funded by fresh IPO equity. However, three consecutive years of margin compression (EBITDA margin 23.45% → 16.99%), extreme related-party revenue/supplier/O&M dependency (73.74% of FY26 revenue-linked transactions with group companies, concentrated in a single relationship), a 76%-pledged promoter shareholding released only weeks before the RHP with an explicit re-pledge trigger, an inexplicable non-core diversification into aircraft leasing, and a valuation that has already re-rated to the peer average within days of listing collectively leave little margin of safety at CMP. Investors should await either a price correction toward the ₹85–95 ACCUMULATE band, or 2–3 quarters of post-listing results confirming margin stabilization, on-schedule capacity commissioning, and a genuine reduction in related-party revenue dependency, before adding meaningfully.

## 7. Key Metrics to Monitor

Track each quarter (post-listing quarterly results, exchange filings, investor presentations):

1. **Capacity utilization** — fell to 41.51% (FY26) from 45.58% (FY25) even as volumes grew; watch for recovery as Panoli Ph2/Ankleshwar Ph3 ramp, versus further dilution as new capacity outpaces offtake.
2. **Coal-trading revenue share** — grew from ~0% (FY24) to 26.92% (FY26); a rising share continues to dilute blended EBITDA/PAT margins and is a key bear-case driver.
3. **EBITDA margin trend** — fell from 23.45% (FY24) to 16.99% (FY26); a break below 15% would be a bear-case signal, a sustained recovery above 18% a bull-case signal.
4. **Related-party revenue/transaction share** — 73.74% of FY26 revenue-linked RPTs with group companies (Sanjoo Dyeing/Sanjoo Prints); watch the Sachin O&M agreement renewal (expires ~FY28) and any governance/regulatory scrutiny of this concentration.
5. **Promoter share pledge status** — released in August 2026 but explicitly re-pledge-contingent on NCD debt repayment; monitor exchange filings for any re-pledge disclosure.
6. **Net Debt/Equity** — 1.57x (FY26); track the post-IPO trajectory following the planned ₹180 Cr debt repayment from Net Proceeds, net of fresh capex borrowing for the debt-funded facilities (Nandesari Ph2, Jhagadia, Vapi Ph3, Tarapur).
7. **New facility commissioning** — 8 planned facilities targeting 345 → 705 TPH; track on-schedule delivery given the company's history of 6–12 month delays (Nandesari Phase 1, Dahej GIDC Phase 1).
8. **Dahej SEZ and Haldia steam-purchase arrangements** — expected to commence within 12 months of the RHP (~mid-2027); Haldia currently has only one identified buyer.
9. **Nitrogen business scale-up** — ₹0.58 Cr FY26 revenue (0.12% of total); watch for traction beyond the pilot Ankleshwar facility.
10. **Credit rating** — ICRA BBB(Stable)/A3+ since the October 2024 downgrade; any further downgrade signals rising financial stress.
11. **Customer concentration** — top 10 = 47.87% of FY26 revenue; largest customer (18.36%) is a related party.
12. **Aircraft-leasing segment** — confirm this remains immaterial to financials and does not absorb disproportionate management time or capital (US$240,000/year lease cost, no operating experience disclosed).
13. **Public float / promoter holding** — ~77% promoter post-offer; watch for any post-lock-in stake sale that could pressure the thinly-floated stock.
14. **Trade payables and working capital cycle** — payables grew from ₹36.48 Cr (FY24) to ₹93.23 Cr (FY26); a further sharp rise would signal working-capital stress alongside the capex ramp.
